Example 3.B.10Telescoping rod on a rotating arm

3D framesCoriolisrelative motion

Problem

The short arm OC is pinned at O to a bracket on a fixed wall and turns about the horizontal $X$ axis at the constant rate $\omega_1 = 1.2$ rad/s. The collar at C carries the telescoping rod AC and turns relative to the arm about the arm axis OC (the $Z$ axis at this instant) at the constant rate $\omega_2 = 1.5$ rad/s. The rod extends at the constant rate $\dot L = 0.06$ m/s. At the instant shown the rod AC is aligned with the fixed $Y$ axis, $L = 0.12$ m and $d = 0.02$ m, where $L$ is the length CA and $d$ is the length OC.

Find (a) the velocity and (b) the acceleration of the end A of the telescoping rod.

Solution

Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Choose the reference frames

Let the $XYZ$ axes be fixed with origin at the pin O. Attach the $xyz$ axes to the outer tube of the rod, with origin at O, $y$ along the rod and $z$ along the arm OC. At this instant the two sets of axes coincide. The frame turns about $X$ with the arm and about the arm axis OC with the collar. Both axes pass through O, so O is a fixed point of the moving frame.

This view matches the book’s figure. Z points to the left from O through C, Y is vertical and X points out of the page (drawn down and to the right). The pin at O turns about X, and the collar at C turns about the arm axis OC.

Step 2 · Find the angular velocity and angular acceleration of the frame
$$\vec\omega = \omega_1\hat I + \omega_2\hat k = 1.2\,\hat\imath + 1.5\,\hat k\ \text{rad/s}$$

The arm axis $\hat k$ turns with the arm, so $d\hat k/dt = \omega_1\hat I\times\hat k = -\omega_1\hat\jmath$. Both rates are constant, so

$$\vec\alpha = \omega_2\frac{d\hat k}{dt} = -\omega_1\omega_2\,\hat\jmath = -1.80\,\hat\jmath\ \text{rad/s}^2$$
Step 3 · Describe the position and the motion relative to the frame

The position of A from the fixed point O, and the velocity and acceleration of A seen by an observer on the outer tube, are

$$\vec r_{A/O} = d\,\hat k + L\,\hat\jmath = 0.12\,\hat\jmath + 0.02\,\hat k\ \text{m}, \qquad \vec v_{rel} = \dot L\,\hat\jmath = 0.06\,\hat\jmath\ \text{m/s}, \qquad \vec a_{rel} = \ddot L\,\hat\jmath = \vec 0$$
Step 4 · Find the velocity of A
$$\vec v_A = \vec v_O + \vec\omega\times\vec r_{A/O} + \vec v_{rel}$$
$$\vec\omega\times\vec r_{A/O} = \left(1.2\,\hat\imath + 1.5\,\hat k\right)\times\left(0.12\,\hat\jmath + 0.02\,\hat k\right) = -0.180\,\hat\imath - 0.024\,\hat\jmath + 0.144\,\hat k\ \text{m/s}$$
$$\vec v_A = -0.180\,\hat\imath + 0.036\,\hat\jmath + 0.144\,\hat k\ \text{m/s}$$
Step 5 · Find the acceleration of A
$$\vec a_A = \vec a_O + \vec\alpha\times\vec r_{A/O} + \vec\omega\times\left(\vec\omega\times\vec r_{A/O}\right) + 2\,\vec\omega\times\vec v_{rel} + \vec a_{rel}$$

The four nonzero terms are

$$\vec\alpha\times\vec r_{A/O} = -0.036\,\hat\imath, \qquad \vec\omega\times\left(\vec\omega\times\vec r_{A/O}\right) = 0.036\,\hat\imath - 0.443\,\hat\jmath - 0.029\,\hat k$$
$$2\,\vec\omega\times\vec v_{rel} = -0.180\,\hat\imath + 0.144\,\hat k$$

All in m/s². Adding them gives

$$\vec a_A = -0.180\,\hat\imath - 0.443\,\hat\jmath + 0.115\,\hat k\ \text{m/s}^2$$

The Coriolis term $2\,\vec\omega\times\vec v_{rel}$ is the largest contribution to the $\hat\imath$ and $\hat k$ components. It appears because the rod extends while it turns.

Answer

(a) $\vec v_A = -0.180\,\hat\imath + 0.036\,\hat\jmath + 0.144\,\hat k$ m/s, with $|\vec v_A| = 0.233$ m/s.

(b) $\vec a_A = -0.180\,\hat\imath - 0.443\,\hat\jmath + 0.115\,\hat k$ m/s², with $|\vec a_A| = 0.492$ m/s².
