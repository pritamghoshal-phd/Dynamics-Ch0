Example 3.B.5Gyroscope

3D framesgyroscopeangular accelerationcarried axis

Problem

The disk of a gyroscope rotates about its own axis at a constant rate $\omega_2 = 600$ rev/min. The gimbal that supports the axle of the disk rotates at a constant rate $\omega_1 = 10$ rad/s about a fixed vertical axis. The observer and the $xyz$ axes are attached to the disk, and the $XYZ$ axes are fixed in space. At the instant shown, the axle of the disk is along $\hat I$.

Find (a) the angular velocity and (b) the angular acceleration of the observer at the instant shown.

Solution

Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Choose the observer and the axes

The observer rides on the disk. The spin axis of the disk is the axle of the gimbal, which we call $\hat\imath$. The gimbal carries the axle around the vertical axis. At the instant shown $\hat\imath = \hat I$ and the $xyz$ and $XYZ$ axes are aligned.

The spin rate in rad/s is

$$\omega_2 = 600\ \frac{\text{rev}}{\text{min}}\cdot\frac{2\pi\ \text{rad}}{1\ \text{rev}}\cdot\frac{1\ \text{min}}{60\ \text{s}} = 62.83\ \text{rad/s}$$

We take both rates as positive along $+\hat J$ and $+\hat\imath$, as drawn.

Step 2 · Find the angular velocity
$$\vec\omega = \omega_1\,\hat J + \omega_2\,\hat\imath = 10\,\hat J + 62.83\,\hat I\ \text{rad/s}$$

The gimbal rate is along the fixed vertical axis. The spin is along the axle î, which the gimbal carries around.

Step 3 · Differentiate to find the angular acceleration

Both rates are constant and $\hat J$ is fixed. The axle direction $\hat\imath$ turns with the gimbal at $\omega_1\hat J$, so $d\hat\imath/dt = \omega_1\hat J\times\hat\imath$. Then

$$\vec\alpha = \omega_2\,\frac{d\hat\imath}{dt} = \omega_1\omega_2\left(\hat J\times\hat I\right) = -(10)(62.83)\,\hat K = -628.3\,\hat K\ \text{rad/s}^2$$

The tip of î moves in the −Z direction, which is up the page in a top view.

This large angular acceleration is what the bearings of a gyroscope must supply when its axle is turned. In Chapter 6 it becomes the gyroscopic moment.

Step 4 · Write the results in the observer's axes

The axes are aligned at this instant, so

$$\vec\omega = 62.83\,\hat\imath + 10\,\hat\jmath\ \text{rad/s},\qquad \vec\alpha = -628.3\,\hat k\ \text{rad/s}^2$$
Answer

(a) $\vec\omega = 62.83\,\hat I + 10\,\hat J = 62.83\,\hat\imath + 10\,\hat\jmath$ rad/s, with $|\vec\omega| = 63.6$ rad/s. (b) $\vec\alpha = -628.3\,\hat K = -628.3\,\hat k$ rad/s².

Explore

  • Reverse the gimbal rate $\omega_1$. Which way does $\vec\alpha$ point now?
  • Set the spin to zero. Is the angular acceleration still nonzero? Explain.
  • Watch the graph. Which fixed component of $\vec\omega$ stays constant, and which two trade places as the gimbal turns?
