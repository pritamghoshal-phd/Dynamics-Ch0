Example 3.B.2Radar antenna

3D framesangular velocityangular accelerationcomponents in moving axes

Problem

A radar antenna rotates about a fixed vertical axis at a constant rate $\Omega = 0.2$ rad/s. The elevation angle $\theta$ of the dish increases at a constant rate $\dot\theta = 0.5$ rad/s. The observer and the $xyz$ axes are attached to the antenna dish, with $x$ along the axis of the dish. The $XYZ$ axes are fixed.

Find (a) the angular velocity and (b) the angular acceleration of the observer when $\theta = 36.87^\circ$.

Solution

Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Choose the observer and the axes

The observer rides on the dish. The $x$ axis points along the axis of the dish toward the feed P, the $y$ axis is perpendicular to it in the vertical plane of the dish, and the $z$ axis is along the elevation pin at O. At the instant shown the $XYZ$ and $xyz$ axes share the same $z$ direction, so $\hat k = \hat K$, and

$$\hat J = \sin\theta\,\hat\imath + \cos\theta\,\hat\jmath,\qquad \hat I = \cos\theta\,\hat\imath - \sin\theta\,\hat\jmath$$

For $\theta = 36.87^\circ$ we have $\sin\theta = 0.6$ and $\cos\theta = 0.8$.

Step 2 · Find the angular velocity

The dish turns about the vertical axis at $\Omega$, and about the elevation pin at $\dot\theta$. The pin is carried around by the base, so

$$\vec\omega = \Omega\,\hat J + \dot\theta\,\hat k$$

The rate Ω acts along the fixed vertical axis. The rate θ̇ acts along the elevation pin, which points out of the page at this instant.

$$\vec\omega = 0.2\,\hat J + 0.5\,\hat K = 0.2(0.6\,\hat\imath + 0.8\,\hat\jmath) + 0.5\,\hat k = 0.12\,\hat\imath + 0.16\,\hat\jmath + 0.5\,\hat k\ \text{rad/s}$$
Step 3 · Differentiate to find the angular acceleration

Both rates are constant and $\hat J$ is fixed. The elevation pin axis $\hat k$ turns with the base at $\Omega\hat J$, so $d\hat k/dt = \Omega\hat J\times\hat k$. Then

$$\vec\alpha = \dot\theta\,\frac{d\hat k}{dt} = \Omega\dot\theta\left(\hat J\times\hat k\right) = (0.2)(0.5)\,\hat I = 0.1\,\hat I\ \text{rad/s}^2$$
Step 4 · Write the results in the observer's axes

Using $\hat I = \cos\theta\,\hat\imath - \sin\theta\,\hat\jmath$,

$$\vec\alpha = 0.1(0.8\,\hat\imath - 0.6\,\hat\jmath) = 0.08\,\hat\imath - 0.06\,\hat\jmath\ \text{rad/s}^2$$

The angular acceleration is horizontal and perpendicular to the elevation pin. Its size, $\Omega\dot\theta$, does not depend on the elevation angle.

Answer

(a) $\vec\omega = 0.2\,\hat J + 0.5\,\hat K = 0.12\,\hat\imath + 0.16\,\hat\jmath + 0.5\,\hat k$ rad/s, with $|\vec\omega| = 0.539$ rad/s.

(b) $\vec\alpha = 0.1\,\hat I = 0.08\,\hat\imath - 0.06\,\hat\jmath$ rad/s².
