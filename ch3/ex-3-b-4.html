Example 3.B.4Shell leaving a tank barrel

3D framesCoriolismoving originacceleration

Problem

The turret of a tank rotates about a fixed vertical axis at a constant rate $\Omega = 0.4$ rad/s. The barrel, pinned to the turret at C, is being raised at a constant rate $\dot\theta = 0.4$ rad/s. A shell is fired with a constant muzzle speed $v_{rel} = 200$ ft/s relative to the barrel. The observer and the $xyz$ axes are attached to the barrel, while the $XYZ$ axes are fixed. The pin C is a distance $r = 3$ ft from the vertical axis, and the barrel has length $L = 15$ ft from C to the muzzle P.

Find (a) the angular velocity of the barrel, (b) the angular acceleration of the barrel, and (c) the acceleration of the shell as it leaves the barrel at P, at the instant shown. The figure shows the barrel at an elevation of about $\theta = 30^\circ$, and we use that value for the numbers.

Solution

Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Choose the observer and the axes

The observer rides on the barrel with its origin at the pin C. The $x$ axis points along the barrel toward P, the $y$ axis is perpendicular to the barrel in the vertical plane, and the $z$ axis is along the pin at C. At the instant shown $\hat k = \hat K$, and

$$\hat J = \sin\theta\,\hat\imath + \cos\theta\,\hat\jmath,\qquad \hat I = \cos\theta\,\hat\imath - \sin\theta\,\hat\jmath$$
Step 2 · Find the angular velocity and the angular acceleration

The barrel turns with the turret about the vertical axis and about the pin at C:

$$\vec\omega = \Omega\,\hat J + \dot\theta\,\hat k = 0.4\,\hat J + 0.4\,\hat K\ \text{rad/s}$$

Both rates are constant, and the pin axis $\hat k$ is carried around by the turret, so

$$\vec\alpha = \dot\theta\left(\Omega\hat J\times\hat k\right) = (0.4)(0.4)\,\hat I = 0.16\,\hat I\ \text{rad/s}^2$$

At $\theta = 30^\circ$ these become

$$\vec\omega = 0.2\,\hat\imath + 0.346\,\hat\jmath + 0.4\,\hat k\ \text{rad/s},\qquad \vec\alpha = 0.139\,\hat\imath - 0.08\,\hat\jmath\ \text{rad/s}^2$$
Step 3 · Find the motion of the origin C

The pin C is on the turret, at a distance $r$ from the vertical axis. It moves on a horizontal circle at the constant rate $\Omega$, so its acceleration points toward the axis:

$$\vec a_C = -\Omega^2 r\,\hat I = -(0.4)^2(3)\,\hat I = -0.48\,\hat I = -0.416\,\hat\imath + 0.24\,\hat\jmath\ \text{ft/s}^2$$
Step 4 · Describe what the observer sees

The observer sees the shell move straight along the barrel at constant speed. At the muzzle,

$$\vec r_{P/C} = L\,\hat\imath = 15\,\hat\imath\ \text{ft},\qquad \left(\vec v_{P/C}\right)_{rel} = 200\,\hat\imath\ \text{ft/s},\qquad \left(\vec a_{P/C}\right)_{rel} = \vec 0$$
Step 5 · Apply the moving-frame acceleration equation
$$\vec a_P = \vec a_C + \left(\vec a_{P/C}\right)_{rel} + \vec\alpha\times\vec r_{P/C} + 2\vec\omega\times\left(\vec v_{P/C}\right)_{rel} + \vec\omega\times\left(\vec\omega\times\vec r_{P/C}\right)$$
TermValue at $\theta = 30^\circ$ (ft/s²)
$\vec a_C$$-0.416\,\hat\imath + 0.24\,\hat\jmath$
$\left(\vec a_{P/C}\right)_{rel}$$\vec 0$
$\vec\alpha\times\vec r_{P/C}$$1.2\,\hat k$
$2\vec\omega\times\left(\vec v_{P/C}\right)_{rel}$$160\,\hat\jmath - 138.6\,\hat k$
$\vec\omega\times\left(\vec\omega\times\vec r_{P/C}\right)$$-4.2\,\hat\imath + 1.04\,\hat\jmath + 1.2\,\hat k$

The Coriolis term dominates. Its ĵ part comes from raising the barrel and its k̂ part comes from turning the turret.

Adding the terms gives

$$\vec a_P = -4.62\,\hat\imath + 161.3\,\hat\jmath - 136.2\,\hat k\ \text{ft/s}^2,\qquad |\vec a_P| = 211\ \text{ft/s}^2$$

For a general angle the same steps give

$$\vec a_P = -\left(2.4 + 0.48\cos\theta + 2.4\cos^2\theta\right)\hat\imath + \left(160 + 0.48\sin\theta + 1.2\sin 2\theta\right)\hat\jmath + \left(4.8\sin\theta - 160\cos\theta\right)\hat k\ \text{ft/s}^2$$
Answer

(a) $\vec\omega = 0.4\,\hat J + 0.4\,\hat K = 0.2\,\hat\imath + 0.346\,\hat\jmath + 0.4\,\hat k$ rad/s. (b) $\vec\alpha = 0.16\,\hat I = 0.139\,\hat\imath - 0.08\,\hat\jmath$ rad/s².

(c) At $\theta = 30^\circ$, $\vec a_P = -4.62\,\hat\imath + 161.3\,\hat\jmath - 136.2\,\hat k$ ft/s² $= -84.6\,\hat I + 137.4\,\hat J - 136.2\,\hat K$ ft/s², with $|\vec a_P| = 211$ ft/s².
