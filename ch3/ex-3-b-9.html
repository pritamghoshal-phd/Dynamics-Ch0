Example 3.B.9Disk rolling around a vertical axis

3D framesrollingangular acceleration

Problem

The arm OC rotates about the fixed vertical $Y$ axis at the constant rate $\Omega$. The disk at C can turn freely about the arm OC, and it rolls without slipping on the fixed horizontal surface. The arm has length $R$ (from the $Y$ axis to the plane of the disk) and the disk has radius $r$, so O is a height $r$ above the surface. The $xyz$ axes are attached to the disk, with $x$ along OC.

Find the angular acceleration of the disk.

The book's statement calls the disk radius $R$, but its figure labels the arm length $R$ and the disk radius $r$. We follow the figure.

Solution

Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Choose the reference frames and write ω

Let the $XYZ$ axes be fixed, with $Y$ vertical and $X$ along the arm at the instant considered. The disk turns with the arm about $\hat J$ at the rate $\Omega$, and it spins relative to the arm about the arm axis $\hat\imath$ at an unknown rate $\omega_s$. Its angular velocity is therefore

$$\vec\omega = \Omega\,\hat J + \omega_s\,\hat\imath$$

The spin ω_s about the arm is negative (about −x) for Ω > 0. The angular velocity ω points along the dashed line OQ, which is the instantaneous axis of rotation, because both O and Q have zero velocity.

Step 2 · Use the rolling condition

The point Q of the disk in contact with the floor has zero velocity. The center C moves on a horizontal circle of radius $R$, so $\vec v_C = \Omega\hat J\times R\,\hat\imath = -\Omega R\,\hat k$. With $\vec r_{Q/C} = -r\,\hat\jmath$,

$$\vec v_Q = \vec v_C + \vec\omega\times\vec r_{Q/C} = -\Omega R\,\hat k + \omega_s\hat\imath\times\left(-r\,\hat\jmath\right) = -\left(\Omega R + \omega_s r\right)\hat k = \vec 0$$
$$\omega_s = -\frac{R}{r}\,\Omega, \qquad \vec\omega = \Omega\,\hat J - \frac{R}{r}\,\Omega\,\hat\imath$$

The vector $\vec\omega$ is parallel to the line from O to Q. This line is the instantaneous axis of rotation of the disk, because both O and Q have zero velocity.

Step 3 · Differentiate ω

The rate $\Omega$ is constant, so $\omega_s$ is also constant. The direction $\hat J$ is fixed, but the arm direction $\hat\imath$ turns with the arm at $d\hat\imath/dt = \Omega\hat J\times\hat\imath = -\Omega\,\hat k$. Therefore

$$\vec\alpha = \omega_s\frac{d\hat\imath}{dt} = \left(-\frac{R}{r}\,\Omega\right)\left(-\Omega\,\hat k\right) = \frac{R}{r}\,\Omega^2\,\hat k$$

The angular acceleration is horizontal and perpendicular to the arm. Its magnitude is constant, but its direction turns with the arm, as the graph in the animation shows.

Answer

$\vec\alpha = \dfrac{R}{r}\,\Omega^2\,\hat k$, horizontal and perpendicular to the arm OC. With the values of the animation ($R = 0.6$ m, $r = 0.2$ m, $\Omega = 1$ rad/s), $|\vec\alpha| = 3$ rad/s².

Explore

  • Reverse the sign of $\Omega$. Does the direction of $\vec\alpha$ change? Explain with $\vec\alpha = \omega_s\, d\hat\imath/dt$.
  • Pause the animation and check that the contact point Q is the only point of the rim with zero velocity.
  • How would the answer change if the arm were pinned to the post at a height different from $r$?
