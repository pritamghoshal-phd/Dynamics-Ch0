Example 3.B.3Disk spinning on a rotating arm

3D framesangular velocityangular accelerationcarried axis

Problem

A disk rotates at a constant rate $\omega_1 = 20$ rad/s relative to arm OC, about an axis through its center C that is perpendicular to the disk. The arm OC rotates about a fixed vertical axis at a constant rate $\omega_2 = 5$ rad/s. The observer and the $xyz$ axes are attached to the disk, while the $XYZ$ axes are fixed. At the instant shown, the $XYZ$ and $xyz$ axes are aligned.

Find (a) the angular velocity and (b) the angular acceleration of the observer at the instant shown.

Solution

Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Choose the observer and the axes

The observer rides on the disk. The spin axis of the disk is perpendicular to the plane of the arm, and we call its unit vector $\hat k$. The arm carries this axis around the vertical shaft. At the instant shown $\hat k = \hat K$.

In the figure the disk spins clockwise in the front view, so its spin is $-\omega_1\hat k$. The arm turns counterclockwise when seen from above, so its rate is $+\omega_2\hat J$.

Step 2 · Find the angular velocity
$$\vec\omega = \omega_2\,\hat J - \omega_1\,\hat k = 5\,\hat J - 20\,\hat K\ \text{rad/s}$$

The arm rate is along the fixed vertical axis. The spin of the disk is along k̂, which points into the page for a clockwise spin.

Step 3 · Differentiate to find the angular acceleration

Both rates are constant and $\hat J$ is fixed. The spin axis $\hat k$ turns with the arm, so $d\hat k/dt = \omega_2\hat J\times\hat k$, and

$$\vec\alpha = -\omega_1\,\frac{d\hat k}{dt} = -\omega_1\omega_2\left(\hat J\times\hat K\right) = -(20)(5)\,\hat I = -100\,\hat I\ \text{rad/s}^2$$

The tip of k̂ moves in the +X direction, and the spin is along −k̂, so α points in the −X direction.

Step 4 · Write the results in the observer's axes

The two sets of axes are aligned at this instant, so the components are the same:

$$\vec\omega = 5\,\hat\jmath - 20\,\hat k\ \text{rad/s},\qquad \vec\alpha = -100\,\hat\imath\ \text{rad/s}^2$$
Answer

(a) $\vec\omega = 5\,\hat J - 20\,\hat K = 5\,\hat\jmath - 20\,\hat k$ rad/s, with $|\vec\omega| = 20.6$ rad/s. (b) $\vec\alpha = -100\,\hat I = -100\,\hat\imath$ rad/s².
