Example 3.A.7Pin on a rolling disk in the slot of an arm

rollingobserver on a bodyCoriolis

Problem

A disk of radius $R$ rolls without slipping to the right with the constant angular speed $\omega_d$. Pin P is fixed to the disk at a distance $r$ from the center A, and it slides in the slot of arm BD, which is pinned to the wall at B. Point B is at the height of A. At the instant shown, P is directly above A, and A is a horizontal distance $x_A$ from B.

The data are $\omega_d = 20$ rad/s (clockwise), $x_A = 0.48$ m, $r = 0.14$ m and $R = 0.2$ m.

Find (a) the angular acceleration of the disk and (b) the acceleration of P as seen by an observer on arm BD.

Solution

Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Find the motion of P from the disk

(a) The disk turns at a constant rate, so its angular acceleration is zero, $\vec\alpha_d = \vec 0$. Because it rolls without slipping, its center moves at $\vec v_A = \omega_d R\,\hat\imath = 4\,\hat\imath$ m/s with $\vec a_A = \vec 0$.

P is a point of the disk, with $\vec r_{P/A} = r\,\hat\jmath = 0.14\,\hat\jmath$ m and $\vec\omega_d = -20\,\hat k$ rad/s. The rigid-body equations give

$$\vec v_P = 4\,\hat\imath + (-20\,\hat k)\times(0.14\,\hat\jmath) = 6.8\,\hat\imath\ \text{m/s}, \qquad \vec a_P = -\omega_d^2\,\vec r_{P/A} = -400(0.14)\,\hat\jmath = -56\,\hat\jmath\ \text{m/s}^2$$
Step 2 · Choose the moving frame on the arm

The observer rides on arm BD, so attach the $xyz$ frame to the arm with its origin at the fixed pin B. The arm's angular velocity $\omega_{BD}$ and angular acceleration $\alpha_{BD}$ are unknown. P is at

$$\vec r_{P/B} = 0.48\,\hat\imath + 0.14\,\hat\jmath\ \text{m}, \qquad s = |\vec r_{P/B}| = 0.5\ \text{m}, \qquad \hat e = 0.96\,\hat\imath + 0.28\,\hat\jmath$$

Relative to the arm, P slides along the straight slot, so $\vec v_{rel} = \dot s\,\hat e$ and $\vec a_{rel} = \ddot s\,\hat e$. We also use $\hat n = \hat k\times\hat e = -0.28\,\hat\imath + 0.96\,\hat\jmath$, which is perpendicular to the slot.

Step 3 · Solve the velocity equation
$$6.8\,\hat\imath = \omega_{BD}\hat k\times\vec r_{P/B} + \dot s\,\hat e = 0.5\,\omega_{BD}\,\hat n + \dot s\,\hat e$$

Taking components along $\hat e$ and along $\hat n$,

$$\dot s = 6.8(0.96) = 6.528\ \text{m/s}, \qquad 0.5\,\omega_{BD} = 6.8(-0.28) \ \Rightarrow\ \omega_{BD} = -3.808\ \text{rad/s}$$
Step 4 · Solve the acceleration equation
$$-56\,\hat\jmath = 0.5\,\alpha_{BD}\,\hat n - \omega_{BD}^2\,\vec r_{P/B} + 2\,\omega_{BD}\,\dot s\,\hat n + \ddot s\,\hat e$$

The component along the slot contains only the centripetal term and the relative acceleration, because the other two terms are along $\hat n$.

$$\hat e:\ \ -56(0.28) = -(3.808)^2(0.5) + \ddot s \ \Rightarrow\ \ddot s = -15.68 + 7.25 = -8.43\ \text{m/s}^2$$
$$\hat n:\ \ -56(0.96) = 0.5\,\alpha_{BD} + 2(-3.808)(6.528) \ \Rightarrow\ \alpha_{BD} = -8.09\ \text{rad/s}^2$$

The observer on the arm sees only the relative acceleration, which is along the slot.

$$\vec a_{rel} = \ddot s\,\hat e = -8.43\,(0.96\,\hat\imath + 0.28\,\hat\jmath) = -8.09\,\hat\imath - 2.36\,\hat\jmath\ \text{m/s}^2$$

The Coriolis term is by far the largest. The observer on the arm does not see it, because it is caused by the rotation of the arm itself.

Answer

(a) The disk turns at a constant rate, so its angular acceleration is zero. (b) The observer on BD sees $\vec a_{rel} = -8.43$ m/s² along the slot, which is $-8.09\,\hat\imath - 2.36\,\hat\jmath$ m/s², directed toward B. At this instant the arm turns at 3.81 rad/s clockwise with an angular acceleration of 8.09 rad/s² clockwise.
