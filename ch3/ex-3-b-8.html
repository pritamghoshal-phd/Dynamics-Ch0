Example 3.B.8Spinning disk on a rotating arm

3D framesbase pointrotating axes

Problem

A vertical shaft turns in fixed bearings at the constant rate $\omega_1$ about the $Y$ axis. A horizontal arm OA of length $L$ is attached to the shaft at O. A disk of radius $R$ is mounted at A on an axle perpendicular to the disk, and it spins relative to the arm at the constant rate $\omega_2$. At the instant shown the arm lies along the $X$ axis and the disk lies in the vertical $XY$ plane.

Find (a) the velocity and (b) the acceleration of the point P on the rim of the disk at the instant when P is directly above the center A.

Solution

Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Choose the reference frames

Let the $XYZ$ axes be fixed with $Y$ along the shaft. Attach the $xyz$ axes to the arm OA, with $x$ along OA, $y$ vertical and $z$ along the axle of the disk. These axes turn with the shaft at $\omega_1\hat J$, and at the instant shown they coincide with $XYZ$. Point A is on the arm, so its motion is simple. We then use A as the base point for the disk.

Drawn with the sample values used in the animation (L = 0.5 m, R = 0.2 m, ω₁ = 1 rad/s, ω₂ = 3 rad/s).

Step 2 · Find the angular velocity and angular acceleration of the disk

The disk turns with the arm at $\omega_1\hat J$ and spins relative to the arm about $\hat k$ at $\omega_2$.

$$\vec\omega = \omega_1\hat J + \omega_2\hat k$$

The axle direction $\hat k$ is carried around by the arm, so $d\hat k/dt = \omega_1\hat J\times\hat k = \omega_1\hat\imath$. Both rates are constant, so

$$\vec\alpha = \omega_2\frac{d\hat k}{dt} = \omega_1\omega_2\,\hat\imath$$
Step 3 · Find the motion of the center A

Point A moves on a horizontal circle of radius $L$ about the shaft at the constant rate $\omega_1$.

$$\vec v_A = \omega_1\hat J\times L\hat\imath = -\omega_1 L\,\hat k, \qquad \vec a_A = \omega_1\hat J\times\vec v_A = -\omega_1^2 L\,\hat\imath$$
Step 4 · Find the velocity of P

Point P and point A are both on the disk, with $\vec r_{P/A} = R\,\hat\jmath$. The rigid-body velocity equation gives

$$\vec v_P = \vec v_A + \vec\omega\times\vec r_{P/A} = -\omega_1 L\,\hat k + \left(\omega_1\hat J + \omega_2\hat k\right)\times R\,\hat\jmath = -\omega_2 R\,\hat\imath - \omega_1 L\,\hat k$$

Note that O is not a point of the disk with zero velocity. The disk spins about an axle through A, so the velocity of P cannot be found as $\vec\omega\times\vec r_{P/O}$.

Step 5 · Find the acceleration of P
$$\vec a_P = \vec a_A + \vec\alpha\times\vec r_{P/A} + \vec\omega\times\left(\vec\omega\times\vec r_{P/A}\right)$$
$$\vec\alpha\times R\,\hat\jmath = \omega_1\omega_2 R\,\hat k, \qquad \vec\omega\times\left(\vec\omega\times R\,\hat\jmath\right) = \vec\omega\times\left(-\omega_2 R\,\hat\imath\right) = \omega_1\omega_2 R\,\hat k - \omega_2^2 R\,\hat\jmath$$
$$\vec a_P = -\omega_1^2 L\,\hat\imath - \omega_2^2 R\,\hat\jmath + 2\,\omega_1\omega_2 R\,\hat k$$

As a check, use the arm as a moving frame instead. Relative to the arm, P moves on a circle with $\vec v_{rel} = -\omega_2 R\,\hat\imath$ and $\vec a_{rel} = -\omega_2^2 R\,\hat\jmath$. The Coriolis term $2\,\omega_1\hat J\times\vec v_{rel} = 2\,\omega_1\omega_2 R\,\hat k$ is exactly the last term above.

Answer

(a) $\vec v_P = -\omega_2 R\,\hat\imath - \omega_1 L\,\hat k$

(b) The acceleration of P is

$$\vec a_P = -\omega_1^2 L\,\hat\imath - \omega_2^2 R\,\hat\jmath + 2\,\omega_1\omega_2 R\,\hat k$$

With the values of the animation ($L = 0.5$ m, $R = 0.2$ m, $\omega_1 = 1$ rad/s, $\omega_2 = 3$ rad/s), $\vec v_P = -0.6\,\hat\imath - 0.5\,\hat k$ m/s and $\vec a_P = -0.5\,\hat\imath - 1.8\,\hat\jmath + 1.2\,\hat k$ m/s².
