Example 3.B.7Crane boom raised while the cab turns

3D framesangular accelerationrigid body

Problem

The cab of a crane turns about the fixed vertical $Y$ axis at the constant rate $\omega_1 = 0.30$ rad/s. At the same time the boom OP, of length $L = 12$ m, is raised at the constant rate $\dot\theta = 0.5$ rad/s, where $\theta$ is the angle between the boom and the horizontal. The pivot O of the boom lies on the axis of rotation of the cab. The $XYZ$ axes are fixed, and the $xyz$ axes are attached to the boom with $x$ along OP.

Find when $\theta = 30^\circ$: (a) the angular velocity of the boom, (b) the angular acceleration of the boom, (c) the velocity of the end P and (d) the acceleration of the end P.

Solution

Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Choose the reference frames

Let the $XYZ$ axes be fixed, with $Y$ vertical. Attach the $xyz$ axes to the boom with origin at the pivot O, which is a fixed point because it lies on the axis of the cab. The unit vector $\hat\imath$ points along OP, $\hat\jmath$ is perpendicular to the boom in its vertical plane, and $\hat k$ is horizontal and perpendicular to that plane. At the instant shown the boom lies in the $XY$ plane, so $\hat k = \hat K$ and

$$\hat J = \sin\theta\,\hat\imath + \cos\theta\,\hat\jmath$$

The boom lies in the vertical XY plane at this instant. The axis z is perpendicular to that plane, so z = Z. The two vectors on the right are drawn to scale in a perspective view.

Step 2 · Find the angular velocity

The boom turns with the cab about the fixed $\hat J$ axis and is raised about the $\hat k$ axis. The angular velocity is the sum of the two rates.

$$\vec\omega = \omega_1\hat J + \dot\theta\,\hat k = 0.30\,(0.5\,\hat\imath + 0.866\,\hat\jmath) + 0.5\,\hat k = 0.150\,\hat\imath + 0.260\,\hat\jmath + 0.500\,\hat k\ \text{rad/s}$$
Step 3 · Find the angular acceleration

Both rates are constant and $\hat J$ is fixed. The axis $\hat k$, however, is carried around by the cab, so it changes direction at the rate $d\hat k/dt = \omega_1\hat J\times\hat k$. Differentiating $\vec\omega$ gives

$$\vec\alpha = \dot\theta\,\frac{d\hat k}{dt} = \omega_1\dot\theta\,\left(\hat J\times\hat k\right) = \omega_1\dot\theta\left(\cos\theta\,\hat\imath - \sin\theta\,\hat\jmath\right) = 0.130\,\hat\imath - 0.075\,\hat\jmath\ \text{rad/s}^2$$

The boom has an angular acceleration even though both rates are constant, because the axis of the raising rotation keeps turning.

Step 4 · Find the velocity of P

Point P is fixed on the boom and O is a fixed point of the boom, so the rigid-body velocity equation gives

$$\vec v_P = \vec\omega\times\vec r_{P/O} = \left(0.150\,\hat\imath + 0.260\,\hat\jmath + 0.500\,\hat k\right)\times 12\,\hat\imath = 6.00\,\hat\jmath - 3.12\,\hat k\ \text{m/s}$$

The component $6.00\,\hat\jmath$ comes from raising the boom ($L\dot\theta$), and the component $-3.12\,\hat k$ comes from the turning cab ($\omega_1 L\cos\theta$).

Step 5 · Find the acceleration of P

The rigid-body acceleration equation with the fixed point O gives

$$\vec a_P = \vec\alpha\times\vec r_{P/O} + \vec\omega\times\left(\vec\omega\times\vec r_{P/O}\right) = \vec\alpha\times 12\,\hat\imath + \vec\omega\times\vec v_P$$
$$\vec\alpha\times 12\,\hat\imath = 0.900\,\hat k, \qquad \vec\omega\times\vec v_P = -3.81\,\hat\imath + 0.468\,\hat\jmath + 0.900\,\hat k$$
$$\vec a_P = -3.81\,\hat\imath + 0.468\,\hat\jmath + 1.80\,\hat k\ \text{m/s}^2$$

In the fixed axes the same vector is $\vec a_P = -3.53\,\hat I - 1.50\,\hat J + 1.80\,\hat K$ m/s². The graph in the animation shows these three components measured from the path of P, and they agree with the dots at $\tau = 0$.

Answer

(a) $\vec\omega = 0.150\,\hat\imath + 0.260\,\hat\jmath + 0.500\,\hat k$ rad/s. (b) $\vec\alpha = 0.130\,\hat\imath - 0.075\,\hat\jmath$ rad/s².

(c) $\vec v_P = 6.00\,\hat\jmath - 3.12\,\hat k$ m/s, with $|\vec v_P| = 6.76$ m/s. (d) $\vec a_P = -3.81\,\hat\imath + 0.468\,\hat\jmath + 1.80\,\hat k$ m/s², with $|\vec a_P| = 4.24$ m/s².

Explore

  • Set $\omega_1 = 0$. Explain why $\vec\alpha$ and the $\hat k$ component of $\vec a_P$ both vanish.
  • Set $\dot\theta = 0$, so that P moves on a horizontal circle. Check that $|\vec a_P| = \omega_1^2 L\cos\theta$.
  • Both rates are constant, but $\vec\alpha \neq \vec 0$. Use the animation to explain which axis is turning.
