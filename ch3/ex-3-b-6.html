Example 3.B.6Disk on a shaft carried by a rotating shaft

3D framesaccelerationCoriolisrotating shafts

Problem

Shaft BD rotates about a fixed horizontal axis at a constant rate $\omega_1$. Shaft OA is rigidly attached to shaft BD, with OA perpendicular to BD. A disk of radius $R$ rotates about shaft OA at a constant rate $\omega_2$ relative to OA. The center O of the disk is a distance $d$ from the axis of BD.

Use $\omega_1 = 5$ rad/s, $\omega_2 = 8$ rad/s, $R = 6$ in and $d = 4$ in.

Find the acceleration of point P on the edge of the disk for the position shown, where P is at the top of the disk and OA is horizontal.

Solution

Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Choose the observer and the axes

Let the observer ride on the two shafts, BD and OA, which form one rigid body. Place the origin at A, the point where OA meets the axis of BD. Point A is on the fixed axis, so it does not move. Let $\hat I$ point along BD toward D, $\hat J$ point up, and $\hat K$ point along OA toward O at the instant shown. Attach $xyz$ to the shafts so that they line up with $XYZ$ at this instant.

The observer turns with the shafts, so

$$\vec\omega = \omega_1\,\hat I = 5\,\hat I\ \text{rad/s},\qquad \vec\alpha = \vec 0$$

The angular acceleration is zero because the rate is constant and the axis of BD is fixed.

Step 2 · Describe what the observer sees

The observer sees the disk spin about OA at the rate $\omega_2$, so P moves on a circle of radius $R$ about O at constant speed. With P at the top of the disk and a counterclockwise spin in the side view,

$$\vec r_{P/A} = d\,\hat K + R\,\hat J = 4\,\hat K + 6\,\hat J\ \text{in}$$
$$\left(\vec v_{P/A}\right)_{rel} = \omega_2\hat K\times R\hat J = -\omega_2 R\,\hat I = -48\,\hat I\ \text{in/s}$$
$$\left(\vec a_{P/A}\right)_{rel} = -\omega_2^2 R\,\hat J = -384\,\hat J\ \text{in/s}^2$$

The relative acceleration points from P toward O. The centripetal term from ω₁ points from P toward the axis of BD, so it has a part along −Ĵ and a part along −K̂.

Step 3 · Apply the moving-frame acceleration equation
$$\vec a_P = \vec a_A + \left(\vec a_{P/A}\right)_{rel} + \vec\alpha\times\vec r_{P/A} + 2\vec\omega\times\left(\vec v_{P/A}\right)_{rel} + \vec\omega\times\left(\vec\omega\times\vec r_{P/A}\right)$$

Here $\vec a_A = \vec 0$ and $\vec\alpha = \vec 0$. The Coriolis term is also zero, because the relative velocity is parallel to $\vec\omega$:

$$2\vec\omega\times\left(\vec v_{P/A}\right)_{rel} = 2(5\hat I)\times(-48\hat I) = \vec 0$$

The centripetal term is

$$\vec\omega\times\left(\vec\omega\times\vec r_{P/A}\right) = 5\hat I\times\left[5\hat I\times(6\hat J + 4\hat K)\right] = 5\hat I\times(30\hat K - 20\hat J) = -150\,\hat J - 100\,\hat K\ \text{in/s}^2$$
Step 4 · Add the terms
$$\vec a_P = -384\,\hat J - 150\,\hat J - 100\,\hat K = -534\,\hat J - 100\,\hat K\ \text{in/s}^2$$

The size is $|\vec a_P| = \sqrt{534^2 + 100^2} = 543$ in/s², or 45.3 ft/s².

In general $\vec a_P = -\left(\omega_1^2 + \omega_2^2\right)R\,\hat J - \omega_1^2 d\,\hat K$ at this position. The directions of the two rates do not matter here, because the Coriolis term vanishes for this position.

Answer

$\vec a_P = -534\,\hat J - 100\,\hat K$ in/s² ($-44.5\,\hat J - 8.33\,\hat K$ ft/s²), with $|\vec a_P| = 543$ in/s².
