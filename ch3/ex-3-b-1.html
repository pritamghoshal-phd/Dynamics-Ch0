Example 3.B.1Bar pinned to a rotating arm

3D framesangular velocityangular accelerationcarried axis

Problem

Bar BC is pinned at end B to bar AB. Bar AB rotates about a fixed vertical axis at A at a constant rate $\Omega = 5$ rad/s. The angle $\theta$ between BC and AB is increasing at a constant rate $\dot\theta = 4$ rad/s. The observer and the $xyz$ axes are attached to bar BC, with $x$ along BC. The $XYZ$ axes are fixed.

Find (a) the angular velocity and (b) the angular acceleration of the observer when $\theta = 90^\circ$.

Solution

Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Choose the observer and the axes

The observer rides on bar BC, and the $xyz$ axes move with it. The bar is turned by two separate rotations. Arm AB carries the whole pin, and with it the bar, around the vertical axis at the rate $\Omega$. At the same time, the pin at B lets the bar turn relative to the arm at the rate $\dot\theta$. The axis of that pin is perpendicular to the plane of the arm and the bar. We call the unit vector along the pin axis $\hat k$. At the instant shown, $\hat k = \hat K$.

We take $\Omega$ as positive about $+\hat J$, which is counterclockwise when seen from above, and $\dot\theta$ as positive about $+\hat k$, which is the direction in which $\theta$ grows in the figure.

Step 2 · Find the angular velocity

The angular velocity of the bar is the sum of the two rates, each along its own axis:

$$\vec\omega = \Omega\,\hat J + \dot\theta\,\hat k$$

The rate Ω is along the fixed vertical axis. The rate θ̇ is along the pin axis k̂, which points out of the page at this instant.

At the instant shown, $\hat k = \hat K$, so

$$\vec\omega = 5\,\hat J + 4\,\hat K\ \text{rad/s}$$
Step 3 · Differentiate to find the angular acceleration

Both rates are constant, and $\hat J$ is a fixed direction. The pin axis $\hat k$ is not fixed, however. It is attached to arm AB, so it turns with the arm at the rate $\Omega\hat J$. The derivative of a unit vector that turns with angular velocity $\vec\Omega$ is $\vec\Omega \times$ (the unit vector). Therefore

$$\vec\alpha = \frac{d\vec\omega}{dt} = \dot\Omega\,\hat J + \ddot\theta\,\hat k + \dot\theta\,\frac{d\hat k}{dt} = \dot\theta\left(\Omega\hat J\times\hat k\right)$$

In a short time dt the arm turns through Ω dt, and the tip of k̂ moves in the +X direction. So dk̂/dt = Ω Î at this instant.

$$\vec\alpha = (4)(5)\left(\hat J\times\hat K\right) = 20\,\hat I\ \text{rad/s}^2$$

The angular acceleration is not zero even though both rates are constant. It comes entirely from the change in direction of the pin axis.

Step 4 · Write the results in the observer's axes

At $\theta = 90^\circ$ the bar is vertical. The $x$ axis points along BC, so $\hat\imath = \hat J$. The $y$ axis is perpendicular to BC in the plane of the arm, pointing back toward A, so $\hat\jmath = -\hat I$. Finally $\hat k = \hat K$. Then

$$\vec\omega = 5\,\hat\imath + 4\,\hat k\ \text{rad/s},\qquad \vec\alpha = -20\,\hat\jmath\ \text{rad/s}^2$$
Answer

(a) $\vec\omega = 5\,\hat J + 4\,\hat K = 5\,\hat\imath + 4\,\hat k$ rad/s, with $|\vec\omega| = 6.40$ rad/s. (b) $\vec\alpha = 20\,\hat I = -20\,\hat\jmath$ rad/s².
