Chapter 3 · Moving Reference Frames

So far, every velocity and acceleration was measured by an observer standing still. Many real problems are easier, or only possible, if you describe the motion as seen by an observer who is moving and turning: a pin sliding in a rotating slot, a person walking on a merry-go-round, a shell travelling down a barrel that is swinging around. This chapter gives the equations that convert what a moving observer sees into what a fixed observer sees.

The idea in one picture

In Chapter 2 we related two points A and B on the same rigid body: $\vec v_B = \vec v_A + \vec\omega\times\vec r_{B/A}$. That works because the distance AB never changes. When B is not fixed to the body (a pin in a slot, a bead on a wire, a shell in a barrel), we attach an observer and a set of $xyz$ axes to the moving body at A, and let the observer report how B moves relative to them. The fixed-frame motion of B is then

$$\vec v_B = \vec v_A + (\vec v_{B/A})_{rel} + \vec\omega\times\vec r_{B/A}$$
$$\vec a_B = \vec a_A + (\vec a_{B/A})_{rel} + \vec\alpha\times\vec r_{B/A} + 2\vec\omega\times(\vec v_{B/A})_{rel} + \vec\omega\times(\vec\omega\times\vec r_{B/A})$$

Here $\vec\omega$ and $\vec\alpha$ belong to the observer, and the "rel" terms are what the observer sees. Section A derives these equations in the plane; Section B shows that they hold unchanged in 3D, with more care needed in finding $\vec\omega$ and $\vec\alpha$.
