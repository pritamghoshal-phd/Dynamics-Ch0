Example 6.C.1Rolling disk driven by a harmonic force

forced vibrationrolling without slippingphase

Problem

A homogeneous disk of mass $m$ and radius $R$ rolls without slipping on a rough horizontal surface. A spring of stiffness $k$ connects the disk center O to the wall, and a horizontal force $F(t) = F_0\sin\omega t$ acts at O. The coordinate $x$ gives the position of O, and the spring is unstretched when $x = 0$.

Find (a) the equation of motion of the disk in terms of $x$, (b) the particular solution of the equation of motion, and (c) the amplitude of the response for $\omega = 15$ rad/s. Is the response in phase or out of phase with the forcing?

Use $m = 2$ kg, $k = 300$ N/m and $F_0 = 20$ N.

Solution

Every solution starts from free-body diagrams in a displaced position, then writes the equation of motion and reads off the vibration properties. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagram

We isolate the disk at a position $x > 0$. The forces are the spring force $kx$ toward the wall, the applied force $F_0\sin\omega t$, the weight $mg$, the normal force $N$ and the friction force $f$ at the contact point.

The spring is stretched by $x$, so it pulls O back toward the wall with $kx$. The friction force $f$ at the contact point is unknown, so we draw it in the $+x$ direction and let the equations decide its sign.

Step 2 · Write the Newton–Euler equations

The disk translates in $x$ and rotates clockwise with angular acceleration $\alpha$. Its moment of inertia about O is $I_O = \tfrac12 mR^2$.

$$\sum F_x = m\ddot x: \qquad F_0\sin\omega t - kx + f = m\ddot x$$ $$\sum M_O = I_O\alpha_{cw}: \qquad -fR = \tfrac12 mR^2\,\alpha$$

The friction force produces a counterclockwise moment about O when it points in $+x$, which is why it appears with a minus sign for a clockwise $\alpha$.

Step 3 · Add the rolling condition

The disk rolls without slipping, so $\ddot x = R\alpha$. The moment equation then gives the friction force.

$$f = -\tfrac12 mR\alpha = -\tfrac12 m\ddot x$$

Substituting into the force equation gives the equation of motion.

$$\tfrac32 m\ddot x + kx = F_0\sin\omega t$$

The rolling disk behaves like a mass of $\tfrac32 m$. Its natural frequency is

$$\omega_n = \sqrt{\frac{k}{\tfrac32 m}} = \sqrt{\frac{2k}{3m}} = \sqrt{\frac{2(300)}{3(2)}} = 10\ \text{rad/s}$$
Step 4 · Find the particular solution

With no damping, the particular solution has the same form as the forcing, $x_p = X\sin\omega t$. Substituting it gives

$$\left(k - \tfrac32 m\omega^2\right)X = F_0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad X = \frac{F_0/k}{1 - r^2}, \qquad r = \frac{\omega}{\omega_n}$$

so the particular solution is

$$x_p(t) = \frac{F_0/k}{1 - (\omega/\omega_n)^2}\,\sin\omega t$$
Step 5 · Evaluate at ω = 15 rad/s

The frequency ratio is $r = 15/10 = 1.5$, and the static deflection is $F_0/k = 20/300 = 0.0667$ m.

$$X = \frac{0.0667}{1 - 1.5^2} = \frac{0.0667}{-1.25} = -0.0533\ \text{m}$$

The amplitude is $|X| = 53.3$ mm. Because $X$ is negative, the disk is displaced to the left whenever the force points to the right. The response is $180°$ out of phase with the forcing, as expected above resonance ($r > 1$). The animation shows the same thing: the blue curve is a mirror image of the dashed one in steady state.

Answer

(a) $\tfrac32 m\ddot x + kx = F_0\sin\omega t$, with $\omega_n = \sqrt{2k/3m} = 10$ rad/s.

(b) $x_p(t) = \dfrac{F_0/k}{1 - (\omega/\omega_n)^2}\sin\omega t$.

(c) $|X| = 53.3$ mm at $\omega = 15$ rad/s, and the response is $180°$ out of phase with the forcing.
