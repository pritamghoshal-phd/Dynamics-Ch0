Example 6.C.3Damping ratio from two measured amplitudes

frequency responsedamping ratiomeasurement

Problem

The steady-state response of the spring–mass–damper system shown can be written as $x_{ss}(t) = X(\omega)\sin(\omega t - \phi)$. The steady-state amplitude is measured at two excitation frequencies, $\omega_1$ and $\omega_2$, with $\omega_2/\omega_1 = 2$. The measurements give $X(\omega_1)/X(\omega_2) = 4$.

Find the damping ratio $\zeta$ of the system.

Solution

Every solution starts from free-body diagrams in a displaced position, then writes the equation of motion and reads off the vibration properties. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagram

The system is the same as in Example 6.C.2. We isolate the block at a displaced position while it moves to the right.

This is the same system as in Example 6.C.2. Only the horizontal forces enter the equation of motion.

Step 2 · Write the equation of motion and its steady state

Newton's second law gives $m\ddot x + c\dot x + kx = f_0\sin\omega t$. The steady-state amplitude is

$$X(\omega) = \frac{f_0/k}{\sqrt{(1 - r^2)^2 + (2\zeta r)^2}}, \qquad r = \frac{\omega}{\omega_n}$$

The ratio of two amplitudes does not depend on $f_0/k$.

$$\frac{X(\omega_1)}{X(\omega_2)} = \sqrt{\frac{(1 - 4r_1^2)^2 + (4\zeta r_1)^2}{(1 - r_1^2)^2 + (2\zeta r_1)^2}} = 4$$
Step 3 · Count the unknowns

This one equation contains two unknowns, the damping ratio $\zeta$ and the frequency ratio $r_1 = \omega_1/\omega_n$. A second piece of information is needed. The usual laboratory procedure, and the intended reading of the problem, is that the first measurement is taken at resonance, where the phase angle is $90°$, so that $\omega_1 = \omega_n$ and $r_1 = 1$.

If $\omega_1$ is not the natural frequency, each choice of $r_1$ gives a different $\zeta$. For example, $r_1 = 0.9$ requires $\zeta = 0.338$, and for $r_1$ below about $0.76$ no damping ratio can produce a ratio of 4. The second preset in the animation shows the $r_1 = 0.9$ case.

Step 4 · Solve with ω₁ = ωₙ

With $r_1 = 1$ and $r_2 = 2$, the two amplitudes are

$$X(\omega_1) = \frac{f_0/k}{2\zeta}, \qquad X(\omega_2) = \frac{f_0/k}{\sqrt{(1 - 4)^2 + (4\zeta)^2}} = \frac{f_0/k}{\sqrt{9 + 16\zeta^2}}$$

Their ratio must be 4.

$$\frac{\sqrt{9 + 16\zeta^2}}{2\zeta} = 4 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad 9 + 16\zeta^2 = 64\zeta^2 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \zeta^2 = \frac{9}{48}$$ $$\zeta = \frac{\sqrt3}{4} = 0.433$$
Answer

Taking the first measurement at resonance ($\omega_1 = \omega_n$), the damping ratio is $\zeta = \sqrt3/4 = 0.433$. Without that information the problem has a family of solutions, one for each $\omega_1/\omega_n$.

Explore

  • Use the first preset and confirm that the amplitude ratio in the statistics is 4.
  • Keep $\zeta = 0.433$ and move $\omega_1/\omega_n$ away from 1. How sensitive is the measured ratio to the frequency you chose?
