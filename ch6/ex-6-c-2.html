Example 6.C.2Damped system driven by a harmonic force

forced vibrationdampingtransmitted force

Problem

A block of mass $m$ rides on wheels and is connected to a fixed base by a spring of stiffness $k$ and a damper of coefficient $c$. A harmonic force $f_0\sin\omega t$ acts on the block. The coordinate $x$ is zero when the spring is unstretched.

Find (a) the steady-state response of the block, $x_{ss}(t)$, and (b) the largest force transmitted to the base.

Use $m = 3$ kg, $k = 7500$ N/m, $c = 45$ kg/s, $f_0 = 75$ N and $\omega = 40$ rad/s.

Solution

Every solution starts from free-body diagrams in a displaced position, then writes the equation of motion and reads off the vibration properties. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagrams

We isolate the block at a position $x > 0$ while it moves to the right. The spring pulls it back with $kx$ and the damper resists the motion with $c\dot x$. The applied force acts to the right. The second diagram shows the base, which receives the spring and damper forces in the opposite direction.

The wheels roll freely, so the floor exerts only vertical forces. The spring and the damper are the only parts that connect the block to the base, so the base receives $kx + c\dot x$ in the opposite direction.

Step 2 · Write the equation of motion

Newton's second law in the $x$ direction gives

$$m\ddot x + c\dot x + kx = f_0\sin\omega t$$

The natural frequency, damping ratio and frequency ratio are

$$\omega_n = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}} = \sqrt{\frac{7500}{3}} = 50\ \text{rad/s}, \qquad \zeta = \frac{c}{2m\omega_n} = \frac{45}{2(3)(50)} = 0.15, \qquad r = \frac{40}{50} = 0.8$$
Step 3 · Find the steady-state response

The steady-state (particular) solution is $x_{ss}(t) = X\sin(\omega t - \phi)$ with

$$X = \frac{f_0/k}{\sqrt{(1 - r^2)^2 + (2\zeta r)^2}}, \qquad \tan\phi = \frac{2\zeta r}{1 - r^2}$$

With $f_0/k = 75/7500 = 0.01$ m,

$$X = \frac{0.01}{\sqrt{(1 - 0.64)^2 + (0.24)^2}} = \frac{0.01}{0.4327} = 0.02311\ \text{m} = 23.1\ \text{mm}$$ $$\phi = \tan^{-1}\frac{0.24}{0.36} = 33.7° = 0.588\ \text{rad}$$

so $x_{ss}(t) = 23.1\sin(40t - 0.588)$ mm.

Step 4 · Find the force transmitted to the base

The base receives $F_T = kx + c\dot x$. In steady state both terms are harmonic and $90°$ apart, so their sum has the amplitude

$$F_{T,max} = X\sqrt{k^2 + (c\omega)^2} = 0.02311\sqrt{7500^2 + (45 \times 40)^2} = 0.02311(7713) = 178.3\ \text{N}$$

This is about $2.4$ times the applied force amplitude. Below $\omega = \sqrt2\,\omega_n$ the spring and damper always transmit more force than is applied.

Answer

(a) $x_{ss}(t) = 23.1\sin(40t - 0.588)$ mm, that is $X = 23.1$ mm and $\phi = 33.7°$.

(b) The largest force transmitted to the base is $F_{T,max} = 178$ N.

Explore

  • Set $\omega = \omega_n = 50$ rad/s. Check that the phase angle becomes $90°$ and that $X = (f_0/k)/(2\zeta)$.
  • Find the frequency above which the base feels less force than $f_0$. (Look for $\omega > \sqrt2\,\omega_n$.)
  • Increase $c$ to 250 kg/s. What happens to the peak of the frequency-response curve and to the phase at $\omega = 40$ rad/s?
