Example 6.C.8Particular solution for a block driven through a spring

base excitationparticular solutionequation of motion

Problem

Block C, of mass $m$, rolls on a smooth floor. It is connected to a base B by a spring of stiffness $k$ and to a fixed wall by a spring of stiffness $4k$. The base is given the motion $x_B(t) = b\sin\omega t$. The coordinate $x$ is measured from the position where both springs are unstretched.

Find the differential equation of motion for the block and the particular solution of this equation.

The book gives no numbers. The animation uses $m = 2$ kg, $k = 100$ N/m and $b = 20$ mm, which you can change with the sliders.

Solution

Every solution starts from free-body diagrams in a displaced position, then writes the equation of motion and reads off the vibration properties. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagram

Draw the block with $x > x_B > 0$. The spring between B and the block is stretched by $x - x_B$, and the spring to the wall is compressed by $x$. Both spring forces act to the left.

The spring between B and the block is stretched by $x - x_B$, and the spring to the wall is compressed by $x$. Both forces act to the left when $x > x_B > 0$.

Step 2 · Write the equation of motion

Newton's second law in the $x$ direction gives

$$m\ddot x = -k\,(x - x_B) - 4kx \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad m\ddot x + 5kx = k\,b\sin\omega t$$

The base motion enters the equation as a harmonic force of amplitude $k\,b$. The natural frequency is $\omega_n = \sqrt{5k/m}$.

Step 3 · Find the particular solution

The forcing is a sine and there is no damping, so try $x_p = X\sin\omega t$. Then $\ddot x_p = -\omega^2 X\sin\omega t$, and substitution gives

$$\left(5k - m\omega^2\right) X = k\,b \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad X = \frac{k\,b}{5k - m\omega^2} = \frac{b/5}{1 - r^2}, \qquad r = \frac{\omega}{\omega_n}$$

For $\omega < \omega_n$ the block moves in phase with the base. For $\omega > \omega_n$, $X$ is negative and the block moves opposite to the base.

Step 4 · Check with numbers

With $m = 2$ kg, $k = 100$ N/m, $b = 20$ mm and $\omega = 10$ rad/s, the natural frequency is $\omega_n = \sqrt{500/2} = 15.81$ rad/s and

$$X = \frac{(100)(0.020)}{500 - 2(10)^2} = \frac{2}{300}\ \text{m} = 6.67\ \text{mm}$$

The stats under the animation show the same value.

Answer

The equation of motion is $m\ddot x + 5kx = k\,b\sin\omega t$, and the particular solution is

$$x_p(t) = \frac{k\,b}{5k - m\omega^2}\,\sin\omega t$$

Explore

  • Raise $\omega$ above $\sqrt{5k/m}$. Watch the block and the base in the time plot. When do they move in opposite directions?
  • Show that at very low frequency the block moves with amplitude $b/5$. Explain this result with the two springs acting as a divider.
