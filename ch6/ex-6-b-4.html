Example 6.B.4Two carts linked to a light inverted bar

equation of motionnatural frequencysmall anglesmassless bar

Problem

Two carts, each of mass $m$, are connected by links to a massless bar that can rotate about a pin O at its lower end. One cart is linked to the top of the bar, a height $L$ above O. The other is linked to the middle of the bar, a height $L/2$ above O. Each cart is connected to the ground by a spring of stiffness $k$ and moves on a smooth surface. The coordinate $x$ is the displacement of the upper cart, measured from its position when the bar is vertical. The springs are unstretched when $x = 0$. Assume small motions, so the bar rotates through small angles only.

Find (a) the equation of motion in terms of the coordinate $x$, and (b) the natural frequency of the system.

The animation uses $m = 2$ kg, $k = 50$ N/m and $L = 1$ m, with the upper cart released from rest at $x_0 = 0.08$ m.

Solution

Every solution starts from free-body diagrams in a displaced position, then writes the equation of motion and reads off the vibration properties. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagrams

The links are two-force members, so each one pushes or pulls along its own line, which is horizontal. The bar has no mass, so the moments of the link forces about O must cancel. The link forces are horizontal, so the vertical pin force at O is zero and only $O_x$ is drawn.

We use three diagrams: the upper cart, the lower cart and the bar. Each link carries a horizontal force, $P_1$ for the upper link and $P_2$ for the lower one.

Step 2 · Write the kinematics

For a small rotation $\phi$ of the bar, the top moves $L\phi$ and the middle moves $L\phi/2$ horizontally. The links carry these motions to the carts.

$$x = L\phi, \qquad x_{\text{lower}} = \frac{x}{2}, \qquad \ddot x_{\text{lower}} = \frac{\ddot x}{2}$$
Step 3 · Apply the Newton–Euler equations

For the upper cart, the spring is compressed by $x$ and pushes left.

$$-kx - P_1 = m\ddot x$$

For the lower cart, the spring is stretched by $x/2$ and pulls left.

$$-k\,\frac{x}{2} + P_2 = m\,\frac{\ddot x}{2}$$

The bar is massless, so the sum of the moments about O is zero even though the bar rotates.

$$\sum M_O = 0: \qquad P_2\,\frac{L}{2} - P_1 L = 0 \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad P_2 = 2P_1$$
Step 4 · Eliminate the link forces

The lower-cart equation gives $P_2 = \tfrac12(m\ddot x + kx)$, so $P_1 = \tfrac14(m\ddot x + kx)$. Substituting into the upper-cart equation gives

$$m\ddot x + kx + \tfrac14(m\ddot x + kx) = 0 \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \tfrac54 m\ddot x + \tfrac54 kx = 0$$
Step 5 · Find the natural frequency $$\omega_n = \sqrt{\frac{\tfrac54 k}{\tfrac54 m}} = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}$$

For the values in the animation, $\omega_n = \sqrt{50/2} = 5$ rad/s. The lower cart adds $\tfrac14 m$ to the effective mass and its spring adds $\tfrac14 k$ to the effective stiffness, so the ratio, and the frequency, are the same as for a single cart on a single spring.

Answer

(a) $$\tfrac54 m\ddot x + \tfrac54 kx = 0, \text{ that is, } m\ddot x + kx = 0$$ (b) $$\omega_n = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}$$

Explore

  • The answer does not depend on $L$. Explain why with the kinetic and potential energies of the two carts.
  • Suppose the lower link were attached at a height $aL$ instead of $L/2$. Show that the effective mass becomes $(1 + a^2)m$ and the effective stiffness $(1 + a^2)k$.
  • Why may we set the sum of the moments on the bar to zero while the bar is accelerating?
