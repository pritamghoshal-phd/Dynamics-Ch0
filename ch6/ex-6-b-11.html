Example 6.B.11Damping coefficient from a free-response plot

Problem

The free response of the single-degree-of-freedom system $$M\ddot x + C\dot x + Kx = f(t)$$ is shown below. It is known that $M = 2$ kg. The book gives no unit for $x$, so read it as a length in metres.

Find the damping coefficient $C$ from this free response plot.

Solution

Every solution starts from free-body diagrams in a displaced position, then writes the equation of motion and reads off the vibration properties. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagram

The floor is smooth, so it pushes only perpendicular to itself. In a free response the external force f(t) is zero.

Displace the block by $x$ to the right and give it a velocity $\dot x$ to the right. The spring pulls back with $Kx$ and the dashpot resists the motion with $C\dot x$. The weight and the normal force are vertical, so they do not enter the horizontal equation.

Step 2 · Write the equation of motion in standard form

Newton’s second law in the $x$ direction gives $M\ddot x = -Kx - C\dot x$, so

$$M\ddot x + C\dot x + Kx = 0 \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \ddot x + 2\zeta\omega_n\dot x + \omega_n^2 x = 0$$

Comparing the two forms shows that $C = 2\zeta\omega_n M$. The plot can give us $\zeta$ and $\omega_n$, so it can give us $C$.

Step 3 · Read the damped period and the decay from the plot

The peaks of the plotted curve are at about

Using peaks that are several cycles apart reduces the reading error. Four cycles separate the first and the fifth peak, so

$$T_d = \frac{26.00 - 0.74}{4} = 6.31\ \text{s}, \qquad \delta = \frac{1}{4}\ln\frac{x_1}{x_5} = \frac{1}{4}\ln\frac{1.38}{0.11} = 0.632$$
Step 4 · Find the damping ratio and the natural frequency

The logarithmic decrement is related to the damping ratio by $\delta = 2\pi\zeta/\sqrt{1-\zeta^2}$. Solving for $\zeta$ gives

$$\zeta = \frac{\delta}{\sqrt{4\pi^2 + \delta^2}} = \frac{0.632}{\sqrt{39.48 + 0.40}} = 0.100$$

The damped frequency is $\omega_d = 2\pi/T_d = 0.995$ rad/s, and the natural frequency is

$$\omega_n = \frac{\omega_d}{\sqrt{1-\zeta^2}} = \frac{0.995}{\sqrt{1 - 0.010}} = 1.00\ \text{rad/s}$$
Step 5 · Solve for C $$C = 2\zeta\omega_n M = 2\,(0.100)(1.00\ \text{rad/s})(2\ \text{kg}) = 0.40\ \text{N·s/m}$$

As a by-product, $K = M\omega_n^2 = 2.0$ N/m. A reading error of about 0.01 in the small fifth peak changes $C$ by about 0.01 N·s/m, so the result is reliable to roughly two significant figures. This is why it pays to use peaks several cycles apart.

Answer

$C \approx 0.40$ N·s/m (with $\zeta \approx 0.10$, $\omega_n \approx 1.0$ rad/s and $K \approx 2.0$ N/m).
