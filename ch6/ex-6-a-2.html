Example 6.A.2Disk rolling between two springs

equation of motionrolling without slippingrigid body

Problem

A homogeneous disk of mass $m$ and radius $r$ rolls without slipping on a rough horizontal surface. Two springs, having stiffnesses of $k$ and $2k$, are attached between the disk center O and the ground, as shown. Let $x$ describe the position of O, where the springs are unstretched when $x = 0$.

Find the equation of motion for the disk in terms of the coordinate $x$.

The animation uses $m = 2$ kg, $r = 0.16$ m and $k = 50$ N/m, with the disk released from rest at $x_0 = 0.12$ m.

Solution

Every solution starts from free-body diagrams in a displaced position, then writes the equation of motion and reads off the vibration properties. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagram

Both springs act at O. The left spring is stretched by $x$ and pulls left with $kx$. The right spring is compressed by $x$ and pushes left with $2kx$. The ground exerts a normal force $N$ and a friction force $F$ at the contact point C.

We draw the disk with its center displaced a distance $x$ to the right. Both spring forces point to the left, toward the position $x = 0$. The ground pushes up with the normal force $N$ and exerts a friction force $F$, which we draw to the right. Its true direction will come out of the solution.

Step 2 · Write the Newton–Euler equations

The center of mass G is at O. Taking $x$ to the right as positive and clockwise rotation as positive (the direction the disk turns when it rolls to the right), the force and moment equations are

$$\sum F_x = m\ddot x: \qquad -kx - 2kx + F = m\ddot x$$ $$\sum M_O = I_O\,\alpha: \qquad -Fr = \tfrac12 m r^2\,\alpha$$

The friction force acts at the bottom of the disk, a distance $r$ below O. A friction force to the right turns the disk counterclockwise, which is why it enters the moment equation with a minus sign.

Step 3 · Add the rolling kinematics

Rolling without slipping links the rotation to the motion of the center.

$$\alpha = \frac{\ddot x}{r} \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad F = -\tfrac12 m\ddot x$$
Step 4 · Write the equation of motion

Substituting the friction force into the force equation gives

$$-3kx - \tfrac12 m\ddot x = m\ddot x \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \tfrac32 m\ddot x + 3kx = 0$$

Dividing by $\tfrac32 m$ gives $\ddot x + (2k/m)\,x = 0$, so the natural frequency is $\omega_n = \sqrt{2k/m}$. For the values in the animation, $\omega_n = \sqrt{2(50)/2} = 7.07$ rad/s.

The same result follows from energy. The kinetic energy is $T = \tfrac12 m\dot x^2 + \tfrac12(\tfrac12 m r^2)(\dot x/r)^2 = \tfrac12(\tfrac32 m)\dot x^2$ and the potential energy is $V = \tfrac12(3k)x^2$. The rolling disk behaves like a mass $\tfrac32 m$ on a spring $3k$.

Answer

$$\tfrac32 m\ddot x + 3kx = 0, \qquad \omega_n = \sqrt{\frac{2k}{m}}$$ The radius $r$ does not appear.

Explore

  • Make the disk heavier. The period grows as $\sqrt{m}$, which the plot shows directly.
  • Why does the radius $r$ drop out of the equation of motion? Look at how $I_O$ and the rolling condition both contain $r$.
  • Watch the friction force: from the moment equation, $F = -\tfrac12 m\ddot x$. When is it largest, and which way does it point then?
