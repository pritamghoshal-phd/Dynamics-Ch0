Example 6.B.10Damping that lengthens the period by 25 percent

damped perioddamping ratio

Problem

Damping is added to an undamped one-degree-of-freedom system. As a result, the period of its free vibration increases by 25 percent.

Find the damping ratio of the system after the damping is added.

Solution

Every solution starts from free-body diagrams in a displaced position, then writes the equation of motion and reads off the vibration properties. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Relate the two periods

The undamped period is $T_n = 2\pi/\omega_n$ and the damped period is $T_d = 2\pi/\omega_d$, with $\omega_d = \omega_n\sqrt{1 - \zeta^2}$. The stiffness and the mass do not change when the damper is added, so $\omega_n$ is the same in both cases and

$$\frac{T_d}{T_n} = \frac{\omega_n}{\omega_d} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - \zeta^2}}$$

Successive zero crossings of the damped response are half a damped period apart. With $\zeta = 0.6$ the oscillation is barely visible after one cycle, but its zero crossings still mark $T_d$.

Step 2 · Solve for the damping ratio

A 25 percent increase means $T_d/T_n = 1.25$.

$$\sqrt{1 - \zeta^2} = \frac{1}{1.25} = 0.8 \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \zeta^2 = 1 - 0.64 = 0.36 \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \zeta = 0.600$$

A large damping ratio is needed for a noticeable change in the period. For example, $\zeta = 0.1$ lengthens the period by only 0.5 percent.

Answer

$\zeta = 0.600$.
