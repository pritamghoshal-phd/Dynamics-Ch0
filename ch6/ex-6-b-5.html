Example 6.B.5Stepped pulley with a hanging block and two springs

equation of motionrotationnatural frequency

Problem

A stepped pulley turns about a fixed axle at O and has a mass moment of inertia $I_O$ about O. A cable wrapped on the inner radius $r$ supports a block of mass $m$ on the left. A second cable on the inner radius runs down on the right to a spring of stiffness $k_1$ anchored to the ground. A third cable leaves the top of the outer radius $4r$ horizontally and is attached to a spring of stiffness $k_2$ anchored to a wall. All cables stay taut. The coordinate $\theta$ measures the counterclockwise rotation of the pulley from static equilibrium.

Find (a) the equation of motion of the system in terms of $\theta$ and (b) the natural frequency of the system.

Solution

Every solution starts from free-body diagrams in a displaced position, then writes the equation of motion and reads off the vibration properties. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Choose the coordinate and draw the free-body diagrams

We use $\theta$, the counterclockwise rotation of the pulley measured from static equilibrium. When the pulley turns by $\theta$, the left side of the inner drum moves down by $r\theta$, so the block moves down by $y = r\theta$. The right side of the inner drum moves up by $r\theta$, which stretches spring $k_1$ by $r\theta$. The top of the outer drum moves to the left by $4r\theta$, which stretches spring $k_2$ by $4r\theta$.

In static equilibrium the springs are already stretched, by $\delta_1$ and $\delta_2$. We draw the pulley and the block separately so that the cable tension $T$ appears.

Positions are measured from static equilibrium. The block moves down by $y = r\theta$ when the pulley turns counterclockwise by $\theta$.

Step 2 · Write the kinetics equations

For the block, with $y$ positive downward, Newton's second law gives

$$m\ddot y = mg - T \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad T = mg - m r\ddot\theta$$

For the pulley we take moments about the fixed point O, with counterclockwise moments positive. The pin forces and the weight of the pulley act at O and have no moment about it. The tension $T$ acts at the left of the inner drum, spring $k_1$ pulls down at the right of the inner drum, and spring $k_2$ pulls to the right at the top of the outer drum.

$$\sum M_O = T r - k_1\left(\delta_1 + r\theta\right) r - k_2\left(\delta_2 + 4r\theta\right) 4r = I_O\,\ddot\theta$$
Step 3 · Use the static equilibrium condition

In static equilibrium $\theta = 0$, $\ddot\theta = 0$ and $T = mg$. The moment equation then gives

$$m g\,r = k_1\delta_1\,r + 4 k_2\delta_2\,r$$

These terms cancel when we substitute $T$ into the moment equation, which is why measuring $\theta$ from static equilibrium removes gravity from the equation of motion.

Step 4 · Write the equation of motion and the natural frequency

Substituting $T = mg - m r\ddot\theta$ and using the static condition gives

$$\left(I_O + m r^2\right)\ddot\theta + \left(k_1 + 16 k_2\right) r^2\,\theta = 0$$

This has the standard form $m_{eq}\ddot\theta + k_{eq}\theta = 0$, so the natural frequency is

$$\omega_n = \sqrt{\frac{k_{eq}}{m_{eq}}} = r\,\sqrt{\frac{k_1 + 16 k_2}{I_O + m r^2}}$$

Spring $k_2$ counts sixteen times because both its stretch and its moment arm are four times larger. With the default values of the animation ($r = 0.1$ m, $I_O = 0.6$ kg·m², $m = 20$ kg, $k_1 = 1000$ N/m, $k_2 = 200$ N/m) the formula gives $\omega_n = 7.25$ rad/s, a period of 0.867 s.

Answer

(a) $\left(I_O + m r^2\right)\ddot\theta + \left(k_1 + 16 k_2\right) r^2\,\theta = 0$. (b) $\omega_n = r\sqrt{\dfrac{k_1 + 16 k_2}{I_O + m r^2}}$.
