Example 6.A.1Particle on a spring and a dashpot

equation of motionspringdashpotgravity

Problem

A particle of mass $m$ is supported by a spring of stiffness $k$ and a dashpot with damping constant $c$. A vertical force $f(t)$ acts upward on the particle, as shown in the animation. Let $x$ describe the position of the particle, where $x$ is measured downward from the position of the particle when the spring is unstretched.

Find the equation of motion (EOM) of this system in terms of the coordinate $x$.

The animation uses $m = 1$ kg, $k = 100$ N/m and $c = 2$ N·s/m. You can change them, and you can switch on a harmonic force $f(t) = F_0\sin\Omega t$.

Solution

Every solution starts from free-body diagrams in a displaced position, then writes the equation of motion and reads off the vibration properties. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagram

The spring is stretched by $x$, so it pulls up with $kx$. The particle moves down with speed $\dot x$, so the dashpot resists with $c\dot x$ upward.

We draw the particle at a general position $x$ below the unstretched position, moving downward with speed $\dot x$. Four forces act on it. The spring is stretched by $x$ and pulls up with $kx$. The dashpot resists the motion with $c\dot x$, which points up when $\dot x$ is positive. Gravity pulls down with $mg$, and the applied force $f(t)$ pushes up.

Step 2 · Apply Newton's second law

We take down as positive, the direction of $x$. Summing the forces on the particle gives

$$\sum F_x = m\ddot x \quad\Longrightarrow\quad mg - kx - c\dot x - f(t) = m\ddot x$$
Step 3 · Write the equation of motion

Moving the terms that contain $x$ and its derivatives to the left side gives the equation of motion.

$$m\ddot x + c\dot x + kx = mg - f(t)$$

Gravity appears as a constant forcing term because $x$ is measured from the unstretched position. If we measured the position from static equilibrium instead, with $x = mg/k + y$, the constant would cancel and the equation would become $m\ddot y + c\dot y + ky = -f(t)$.

Step 4 · Read off the vibration properties

Dividing by $m$ puts the equation in standard form,

$$\ddot x + 2\zeta\omega_n\dot x + \omega_n^2 x = g - \frac{f(t)}{m}, \qquad \omega_n = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}, \qquad \zeta = \frac{c}{2\sqrt{km}}$$

For the values in the animation, $\omega_n = \sqrt{100/1} = 10$ rad/s and $\zeta = 2/(2\sqrt{100}) = 0.1$, so the particle oscillates and the motion decays slowly about $x = mg/k = 0.0981$ m.

Answer

$$m\ddot x + c\dot x + kx = mg - f(t)$$ with $x$ measured downward from the unstretched position. The natural frequency is $\omega_n = \sqrt{k/m}$ and the damping ratio is $\zeta = c/(2\sqrt{km})$.

Explore

  • Choose No damping. The particle now oscillates forever between $x = 0$ and $x = 2mg/k$. Why is the lowest point exactly twice the static deflection?
  • Choose Heavy damping ($c = 20$ N·s/m gives $\zeta = 1$). The particle creeps to equilibrium without overshooting. This is critical damping.
  • Switch on the harmonic force. After the start-up dies away, the particle settles into a steady oscillation at the forcing frequency $\Omega$ about $x = mg/k$.
