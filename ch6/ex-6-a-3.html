Example 6.A.3Disk under a board that rests on a smooth post

equation of motionrolling without slippingtwo bodies

Problem

A homogeneous disk of mass $m$ and radius $r$ rolls without slipping on a rough horizontal surface. A spring of stiffness $k$ is attached between the disk center O and the ground. A block (a long board), also of mass $m$, is in no-slip contact with the top surface of the disk and rests on a smooth vertical support at A. A second spring of stiffness $k$ is connected between the block and the ground. Let $x$ describe the position of O, where the springs are unstretched when $x = 0$.

Find the equation of motion for the disk in terms of the coordinate $x$.

The animation uses $m = 2$ kg, $r = 0.15$ m and $k = 40$ N/m, with the disk released from rest at $x_0 = 0.07$ m.

Solution

Every solution starts from free-body diagrams in a displaced position, then writes the equation of motion and reads off the vibration properties. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagrams

At the top of the disk the board and the disk push on each other with equal and opposite forces $N_2$ and $F_2$. The post at A is smooth, so it exerts only the vertical force $N_A$. The board has moved $2x$, which compresses the right spring by $2x$.

We separate the disk from the board because the contact force between them is unknown. At the top of the disk the board pushes down with $N_2$ and the friction force $F_2$ acts on the disk, while the opposite forces act on the board. The ground exerts $N_1$ and $F_1$ on the disk. The smooth post exerts only the vertical force $N_A$ on the board.

Step 2 · Write the kinematics

The disk rolls without slipping on the ground, so its contact point C is at rest and $\omega = \dot x/r$ (clockwise). The top of the disk is a distance $2r$ above C, so it moves at $2\dot x$. The board does not slip on the disk, so it moves with the top of the disk.

$$x_{\text{board}} = 2x, \qquad \ddot x_{\text{board}} = 2\ddot x, \qquad \alpha = \frac{\ddot x}{r}$$
Step 3 · Write the Newton–Euler equations

Taking $x$ to the right and clockwise rotation as positive, the equations for the disk and the horizontal equation for the board are

$$\text{disk, forces:}\qquad -kx + F_1 + F_2 = m\ddot x$$ $$\text{disk, moments about O:}\qquad F_2 r - F_1 r = \tfrac12 m r^2\,\frac{\ddot x}{r}$$ $$\text{board:}\qquad -F_2 - k(2x) = m(2\ddot x)$$
Step 4 · Eliminate the contact forces

The board equation gives $F_2 = -2m\ddot x - 2kx$. The moment equation gives $F_1 = F_2 - \tfrac12 m\ddot x$. Substituting both into the disk force equation gives

$$-kx + 2F_2 - \tfrac12 m\ddot x = m\ddot x \quad\Longrightarrow\quad -kx - 4m\ddot x - 4kx = \tfrac32 m\ddot x$$ $$\tfrac{11}{2}m\ddot x + 5kx = 0$$
Step 5 · Find the natural frequency

Dividing by $\tfrac{11}{2}m$ gives $\ddot x + \frac{10k}{11m}x = 0$, so

$$\omega_n = \sqrt{\frac{10k}{11m}}$$

For the values in the animation, $\omega_n = \sqrt{10(40)/(11 \cdot 2)} = 4.26$ rad/s.

Energy gives a quick check. The kinetic energy is $T = \tfrac12(\tfrac32 m)\dot x^2 + \tfrac12 m(2\dot x)^2 = \tfrac12(\tfrac{11}{2}m)\dot x^2$ and the potential energy is $V = \tfrac12 kx^2 + \tfrac12 k(2x)^2 = \tfrac12(5k)x^2$.

Answer

$$\tfrac{11}{2}m\ddot x + 5kx = 0, \qquad \omega_n = \sqrt{\frac{10k}{11m}}$$

  • The right spring has the same stiffness as the left one, yet it contributes $4k$. Explain why with the energy $\tfrac12 k(2x)^2$.
