Example 6.B.6Hanging block with a spring and a dashpot

static deflectionnatural frequencydamping ratio

Problem

A block of mass $m$ hangs from the ceiling on a spring of stiffness $k$. A dashpot with damping constant $c$ connects the block to the floor. The coordinate $x$ measures the downward displacement of the block from static equilibrium.

The data are $m = 0.25$ kg, $k = 100$ N/m and $c = 2$ kg/s.

Find (a) the static deformation of the spring and (b) the undamped natural frequency and the damping ratio of the system.

The book lists the mass as "0.25 m", which is a misprint for 0.25 kg.

Solution

Every solution starts from free-body diagrams in a displaced position, then writes the equation of motion and reads off the vibration properties. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagram

We draw the block in a position $x$ below static equilibrium, moving downward with velocity $\dot x$. The spring is stretched by $\delta_{st} + x$, so it pulls the block up with a force $k(\delta_{st} + x)$. The dashpot opposes the velocity with a force $c\dot x$ upward. The weight $mg$ acts downward.

The spring is stretched by $\delta_{st} + x$, and the dashpot force opposes the velocity $\dot x$.

Step 2 · Write the equation of motion

Newton's second law in the $x$ direction gives

$$m\ddot x = mg - k\left(\delta_{st} + x\right) - c\dot x$$

In static equilibrium $x = \dot x = \ddot x = 0$, so $k\delta_{st} = mg$. These two terms cancel, and the equation of motion is

$$m\ddot x + c\dot x + k x = 0$$
Step 3 · Find the static deformation $$\delta_{st} = \frac{mg}{k} = \frac{0.25(9.81)}{100} = 0.0245\ \text{m} = 24.5\ \text{mm}$$
Step 4 · Find the natural frequency and the damping ratio $$\omega_n = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}} = \sqrt{\frac{100}{0.25}} = 20.0\ \text{rad/s}$$ $$\zeta = \frac{c}{2\sqrt{k m}} = \frac{2}{2\sqrt{100(0.25)}} = \frac{2}{10} = 0.200$$

The system is underdamped because $\zeta < 1$. The critical damping constant is $c_{cr} = 2\sqrt{km} = 10$ kg/s.

Answer

(a) $\delta_{st} = 24.5$ mm. (b) $\omega_n = 20.0$ rad/s and $\zeta = 0.200$.
