Example 6.B.1Block on an incline pulled through a movable pulley

equation of motionnatural frequencypulleysconstraint

Problem

Block A (mass $m$) rests on a smooth incline of angle $\theta$ and is connected to the ground by a spring of stiffness $k$. A cable runs from A over a fixed pulley, down around a movable pulley and back up to the axle of the fixed pulley. Block B (mass $m$) hangs from the movable pulley, and a force $f(t)$ acts downward on B. The pulleys are light. Let $x$ describe the position of A up the incline, measured from the position where the spring is unstretched.

Find (a) the EOM of this system in terms of the coordinate $x$, and (b) the natural frequency of the system.

The animation uses $m = 2$ kg, $k = 80$ N/m and $\theta = 30°$.

Solution

Every solution starts from free-body diagrams in a displaced position, then writes the equation of motion and reads off the vibration properties. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagrams

The incline is smooth, so it pushes only along its normal. The pulley is light, so the two cable segments that hold it up carry the same tension $T$. The spring is stretched by $x$ and pulls A down the incline.

We use two free-body diagrams. The first is block A, displaced a distance $x$ up the incline. The second is the movable pulley together with block B, which removes the force in the short hanger from the problem.

Step 2 · Write the cable constraint

The cable has a fixed length. When A moves up the incline by $x$, the length of cable between A and the fixed pulley shortens by $x$. That length is shared by the two vertical segments that hold the movable pulley, so each lengthens by $x/2$ and B moves down by $x/2$.

$$y_B = \frac{x}{2}, \qquad \ddot y_B = \frac{\ddot x}{2} \quad (\text{downward})$$
Step 3 · Apply Newton's second law to each free-body diagram

For A, along the incline with up-the-incline positive,

$$T - kx - mg\sin\theta = m\ddot x$$

For the pulley and B, with downward positive,

$$mg + f(t) - 2T = m\,\frac{\ddot x}{2} \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad T = \frac{mg + f(t)}{2} - \frac{m\ddot x}{4}$$
Step 4 · Write the equation of motion

Substituting the tension into the equation for A gives

$$\frac{mg + f(t)}{2} - \frac{m\ddot x}{4} - kx - mg\sin\theta = m\ddot x$$ $$\tfrac54 m\ddot x + kx = \frac{f(t)}{2} + mg\left(\tfrac12 - \sin\theta\right)$$

The constant term on the right moves the equilibrium position to $x_{eq} = mg(\tfrac12 - \sin\theta)/k$. It does not change the natural frequency. For $\theta = 30°$ the two gravity terms cancel exactly, so the equilibrium is at the unstretched position.

Step 5 · Find the natural frequency

The equation has the form $m_{eq}\ddot x + k_{eq}x = \ldots$ with $m_{eq} = \tfrac54 m$ and $k_{eq} = k$.

$$\omega_n = \sqrt{\frac{k}{\tfrac54 m}} = \sqrt{\frac{4k}{5m}}$$

For the values in the animation, $\omega_n = \sqrt{4(80)/(5 \cdot 2)} = 5.66$ rad/s, a period of 1.11 s.

Answer

(a) $$\tfrac54 m\ddot x + kx = \frac{f(t)}{2} + mg\left(\tfrac12 - \sin\theta\right)$$ (b) $$\omega_n = \sqrt{\frac{4k}{5m}}$$
