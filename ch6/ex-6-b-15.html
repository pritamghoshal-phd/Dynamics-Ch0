Example 6.B.15Center of percussion of a baseball bat

physical pendulumlinearizationcenter of percussionexperiment

Problem

The location of the center of percussion P of a rigid body is given by $h = I_O/(md)$, where $h$ is the distance from the support point O to P, $d$ is the distance from O to the center of mass G, and $I_O$ is the moment of inertia of the body about O. In this example, we use the free vibration of a baseball bat suspended from a support point O to locate the bat’s center of percussion.

Find

  1. a free-body diagram of the bat;
  2. the equation of motion (EOM) of the bat in terms of the angle $\theta$, and its linearized form for small $\theta$;
  3. the natural frequency of the free response, in terms of the parameters of the problem;
  4. the relationship between the distance $h$ to the center of percussion and the natural frequency;
  5. a simple experiment that determines the location of the center of percussion.

Solution

Every solution starts from free-body diagrams in a displaced position, then writes the equation of motion and reads off the vibration properties. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · (a) Draw the free-body diagram

The pin force acts at O, so only the weight has a moment about O. Its moment arm is d sin θ.

Two forces act on the bat: the pin force at O (components $O_x$ and $O_y$) and the weight $mg$ at the center of mass G.

Step 2 · (b) Write and linearize the equation of motion

The bat turns about the fixed pin O, so take moments about O. The weight has moment arm $d\sin\theta$ and tends to reduce $\theta$:

$$\sum M_O = I_O\ddot\theta: \qquad -mgd\sin\theta = I_O\ddot\theta \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad I_O\ddot\theta + mgd\sin\theta = 0$$

For small $\theta$, $\sin\theta \approx \theta$, and the linearized EOM is

$$I_O\ddot\theta + mgd\,\theta = 0$$
Step 3 · (c) Find the natural frequency $$\omega_n = \sqrt{\frac{mgd}{I_O}}, \qquad T = \frac{2\pi}{\omega_n} = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{I_O}{mgd}}$$

For the bat in the animation, $\omega_n = \sqrt{(0.963)(9.81)(0.557)/0.341} = 3.93$ rad/s and $T = 1.60$ s.

Step 4 · (d) Relate h to the natural frequency

Divide the definition of $h$ by the expression for $\omega_n^2$:

$$h = \frac{I_O}{md} = \frac{g}{\;mgd/I_O\;} = \frac{g}{\omega_n^2} = \frac{g\,T^2}{4\pi^2}$$

So the center of percussion is at the length of the simple pendulum that has the same period as the bat. For the animated bat, $h = 9.81/3.93^2 = 0.635$ m from O, or about 0.665 m (26 in) from the knob.

Step 5 · (e) Design the experiment

Hang the bat from a low-friction pin through (or just below) the knob, so that it can swing freely in a vertical plane. Pull it aside by a small angle, about 10° or less, so that the linearized EOM holds, and release it. Time a large number of complete swings, for example 20, with a stopwatch, and divide by the number of swings to get $T$ accurately. Then compute $h = gT^2/(4\pi^2)$ and mark P at that distance from the pin along the bat.

No scale or inertia measurement is needed. Only a length (from the pin) and a time are required. The large-swing preset in the animation shows why small angles matter: at 45° the period is about 4% longer, so $h$ would come out about 8% too large.

Answer

(b) $I_O\ddot\theta + mgd\sin\theta = 0$, linearized $I_O\ddot\theta + mgd\,\theta = 0$. (c) $\omega_n = \sqrt{mgd/I_O}$. (d) $h = g/\omega_n^2 = gT^2/4\pi^2$. (e) Time small free swings about O and use $h = gT^2/4\pi^2$.

Explore

  • Use the “Large swing” preset. How far off is the measured $h$ compared with the exact $I_O/(md)$? Why?
  • Add a little pivot friction. Does the measured period change noticeably? Does the amplitude?
  • Why does striking a ball at P produce no reaction force at the pin (and so no sting in the batter’s hands)?
