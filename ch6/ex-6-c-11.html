Example 6.C.11Bar in a horizontal plane driven through a spring

base excitationrigid bodysmall angles

Problem

A thin homogeneous bar OA of length $L$ and mass $m$ is pinned to the ground at O and moves in a horizontal plane. A spring of stiffness $k$ connects the end A to a base B, which is given the motion $y(t) = y_0\sin\omega t$ perpendicular to the bar. The spring is unstretched when $\theta = 0$ and $y = 0$.

Find the differential equation of motion for the system in terms of the rotation angle $\theta$, assuming small oscillations.

The book gives no numbers. The animation uses $m = 2$ kg, $L = 1$ m, $k = 150$ N/m and $y_0 = 30$ mm.

Solution

Every solution starts from free-body diagrams in a displaced position, then writes the equation of motion and reads off the vibration properties. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagram

Draw the bar at a small angle $\theta$. The forces in the horizontal plane are the pin forces $O_x$ and $O_y$ and the spring force at A. For small angles, A moves a distance $L\theta$ perpendicular to the bar, so the spring is stretched by $y - L\theta$ and pulls A with $F_s = k\,(y - L\theta)$.

The motion is in a horizontal plane, so gravity acts perpendicular to the page and is balanced by vertical support forces that do not appear in this view. The spring force at A is $F_s = k\,(y - L\theta)$ for small angles. The pin forces at O have no moment about O.

Step 2 · Write the moment equation about O

The bar rotates about the fixed point O, so use $\sum M_O = I_O\,\ddot\theta$ with $I_O = mL^2/3$. The pin forces have no moment about O, and gravity acts perpendicular to the plane of motion.

$$\frac{mL^2}{3}\,\ddot\theta = L\,k\,(y - L\theta)$$
Step 3 · Rearrange into standard form $$\frac{mL^2}{3}\,\ddot\theta + kL^2\,\theta = kL\,y_0\sin\omega t \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \ddot\theta + \frac{3k}{m}\,\theta = \frac{3k\,y_0}{mL}\sin\omega t$$

The natural frequency is $\omega_n = \sqrt{3k/m}$. It does not depend on $L$, because both the stiffness moment and the inertia grow as $L^2$.

Step 4 · Find the steady-state amplitude

Substituting $\theta_p = \Theta\sin\omega t$ gives

$$\Theta = \frac{y_0/L}{1 - r^2}, \qquad r = \frac{\omega}{\omega_n}$$

With the animation's values, $\omega_n = 15$ rad/s. At $\omega = 10$ rad/s, $\Theta = 0.054$ rad $= 3.09°$, so A moves 54 mm while the base moves 30 mm.

Answer $$\frac{mL^2}{3}\,\ddot\theta + kL^2\,\theta = kL\,y_0\sin\omega t, \qquad\text{or}\qquad \ddot\theta + \frac{3k}{m}\,\theta = \frac{3k\,y_0}{mL}\sin\omega t$$
