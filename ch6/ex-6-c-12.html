Example 6.C.12Frame with a rotating imbalance

rotating imbalancesteady stateamplitude

Problem

A frame of mass $M$ moves horizontally on rollers between two rails. It is connected to fixed walls by a spring of stiffness $k$ on the left and a spring of stiffness $2k$ on the right. A rotor inside the frame carries a particle of mass $m$ at a distance $e$ from its axle O and spins at the constant rate $\omega$, with $\theta = \omega t$ measured from the horizontal. The coordinate $x$ of the frame is measured from the position where both springs are unstretched.

The data are $m = 0.1$ kg, $M = 4.9$ kg, $k = 1500$ N/m, $e = 20$ mm and $\omega = 40$ rad/s.

Find (a) the equation of motion for this system in terms of $x$ and (b) the amplitude of the steady-state response.

Solution

Every solution starts from free-body diagrams in a displaced position, then writes the equation of motion and reads off the vibration properties. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagram

Take the frame and the particle together as the system, so the force between the arm and the particle is internal. In a displaced position $x > 0$, the left spring is stretched by $x$ and the right spring is compressed by $x$. Both push or pull the frame to the left, with forces $kx$ and $2kx$. The rail forces and the weights are vertical.

The system is the frame M together with the rotating particle m. The force between the arm and the particle is internal. The left spring is stretched by $x$ and the right spring is compressed by $x$, so both push or pull the frame to the left. The rail forces $N_t$, $N_b$ and the weights are vertical.

Step 2 · Write the kinematics of the particle

The particle is at $x_P = x + e\cos\omega t$, so its horizontal acceleration is

$$\ddot x_P = \ddot x - e\,\omega^2\cos\omega t$$
Step 3 · Write the equation of motion

Newton's second law for the system in the $x$ direction adds the mass times acceleration of each part.

$$M\ddot x + m\left(\ddot x - e\,\omega^2\cos\omega t\right) = -kx - 2kx$$ $$\left(M + m\right)\ddot x + 3kx = m\,e\,\omega^2\cos\omega t$$

The spinning particle acts like a harmonic force of amplitude $F_0 = m e\omega^2$ on the frame.

Step 4 · Find the steady-state amplitude $$\omega_n = \sqrt{\frac{3k}{M + m}} = \sqrt{\frac{4500}{5.0}} = 30\ \text{rad/s}, \qquad r = \frac{40}{30} = 1.333$$ $$F_0 = m e\omega^2 = (0.1)(0.020)(40)^2 = 3.2\ \text{N}$$ $$X = \frac{F_0/(3k)}{1 - r^2} = \frac{3.2/4500}{1 - 1.778} = -0.000914\ \text{m}$$

The amplitude is $|X| = 0.914$ mm. Because $r > 1$, the frame moves opposite to the horizontal position of the particle.

Answer

(a) $(M + m)\,\ddot x + 3kx = m e\,\omega^2\cos\omega t$. (b) The steady-state amplitude is $|X| = 0.914$ mm.

Explore

  • Spin the rotor very fast. Show that $X$ approaches $-me/(M + m) = -0.4$ mm, and explain why the mass center of the system then stays still.
  • Set $\omega = 30$ rad/s. Why does the amplitude grow so quickly even though $F_0$ is only 1.8 N?
