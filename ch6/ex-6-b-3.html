Example 6.B.3Largest acceleration of a block on three springs

free vibrationsprings in parallelamplitude

Problem

A block of mass $m$ is held by two springs of stiffness $k$ above it and a spring of stiffness $3k$ below it. The block has a downward speed of $v_0$ as it passes through its equilibrium position.

Find the maximum acceleration of the block over one cycle of oscillation.

An earlier version of the book gives $k = 1000$ N/m, $m = 50$ kg and $v_0 = 2$ m/s. The animation uses these values.

Solution

Every solution starts from free-body diagrams in a displaced position, then writes the equation of motion and reads off the vibration properties. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagram

At static equilibrium the upper springs are stretched by $\delta_1$ and the lower spring is compressed by $\delta_3$, with $2k\delta_1 + 3k\delta_3 = mg$. Moving the block down by $x$ stretches the upper springs further and compresses the lower spring further, so all three spring forces grow by the same amounts $kx$, $kx$ and $3kx$.

We measure $x$ downward from the static equilibrium position. In that position the spring forces already balance the weight, which is why the stretch $\delta_1$ and the compression $\delta_3$ appear in the diagram.

Step 2 · Write the equation of motion

With down positive,

$$mg - 2k(\delta_1 + x) - 3k(\delta_3 + x) = m\ddot x$$

The equilibrium condition $2k\delta_1 + 3k\delta_3 = mg$ removes the constant terms, leaving

$$m\ddot x + 5kx = 0, \qquad \omega_n = \sqrt{\frac{5k}{m}}$$

All three springs act on the same block and stretch or compress by the same amount $x$, so they behave like springs in parallel with $k_{eq} = k + k + 3k = 5k$.

Step 3 · Write the free response

At $t = 0$ the block is at equilibrium ($x = 0$) and moving down with speed $v_0$. The free response that satisfies these conditions is

$$x(t) = X\sin\omega_n t, \qquad X = \frac{v_0}{\omega_n}$$
Step 4 · Find the largest acceleration

Differentiating twice gives $\ddot x = -\omega_n^2 X\sin\omega_n t$, whose largest magnitude is

$$|\ddot x|_{max} = \omega_n^2 X = \omega_n v_0 = v_0\sqrt{\frac{5k}{m}}$$

With $k = 1000$ N/m and $m = 50$ kg, $\omega_n = \sqrt{5(1000)/50} = 10$ rad/s. The amplitude is $X = 2/10 = 0.2$ m and the largest acceleration is

$$|\ddot x|_{max} = 10(2) = 20\ \text{m/s}^2$$

It occurs at the two extreme positions, $x = \pm 0.2$ m, where the block momentarily stops.

Answer

$$|\ddot x|_{max} = v_0\sqrt{\frac{5k}{m}}$$ which is $20$ m/s² for $k = 1000$ N/m, $m = 50$ kg and $v_0 = 2$ m/s.

Explore

  • Double the stiffness. The amplitude shrinks by $\sqrt2$, but the largest acceleration grows by $\sqrt2$.
  • Why do the static deflections $\delta_1$ and $\delta_3$ not appear in the answer?
  • Watch the acceleration arrow and the plot: the acceleration is zero when the speed is largest, and largest when the speed is zero.
