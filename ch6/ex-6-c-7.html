Example 6.C.7Keeping a base-excited block below 1.5 times the base motion

base excitationfrequency bandresonance

Problem

Block A, of mass $m$, rolls on a smooth floor between two springs of stiffness $k$. The left spring is attached to a base B that is given the prescribed motion $y(t) = y_0\sin\omega t$. The right spring is attached to a fixed wall. The coordinate $x$ of the block is measured from the position where both springs are unstretched.

The data are $m = 4$ kg and $k = 800$ N/m.

Find the range of forcing frequencies $\omega$ for which the amplitude of the motion of block A does not exceed $1.5\,y_0$.

Solution

Every solution starts from free-body diagrams in a displaced position, then writes the equation of motion and reads off the vibration properties. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagram

Draw the block in a displaced position with $x > 0$ while the base is displaced by $y$. The left spring is then stretched by $x - y$, and the right spring is compressed by $x$. Both spring forces act to the left.

The block is drawn with $x > 0$ and the base displaced by $y$. The left spring is stretched by $x - y$ and pulls the block to the left. The right spring is compressed by $x$ and pushes the block to the left. The weight and the normal forces are vertical, so they do not enter the $x$ equation.

Step 2 · Write the equation of motion

Newton's second law in the $x$ direction gives

$$m\ddot x = -k\,(x - y) - kx \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad m\ddot x + 2kx = k\,y_0\sin\omega t$$

This is an undamped oscillator with equivalent stiffness $2k$ and a harmonic force of amplitude $F_0 = k\,y_0$.

Step 3 · Find the natural frequency and the steady-state amplitude

The natural frequency is

$$\omega_n = \sqrt{\frac{2k}{m}} = \sqrt{\frac{2(800)}{4}} = 20\ \text{rad/s}$$

The particular solution has the form $x_p = X\sin\omega t$. Substituting it into the equation of motion gives

$$X = \frac{F_0/(2k)}{1 - r^2} = \frac{y_0/2}{1 - r^2}, \qquad r = \frac{\omega}{\omega_n}$$

A negative $X$ means that the block moves opposite to the base.

Step 4 · Apply the amplitude limit

The amplitude of the motion is $|X|$, and we need $|X| \le 1.5\,y_0$.

$$\frac{1}{2\,|1 - r^2|} \le 1.5 \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad |1 - r^2| \ge \frac13$$

Below resonance ($r < 1$) this requires $1 - r^2 \ge 1/3$, so $r^2 \le 2/3$. Above resonance ($r > 1$) it requires $r^2 - 1 \ge 1/3$, so $r^2 \ge 4/3$.

$$\omega \le 20\sqrt{2/3} = 16.33\ \text{rad/s} \qquad\text{or}\qquad \omega \ge 20\sqrt{4/3} = 23.09\ \text{rad/s}$$

Between these two frequencies the response grows toward resonance at $\omega_n = 20$ rad/s, and the amplitude exceeds $1.5\,y_0$. The green bands on the frequency-response plot are the allowed ranges.

Answer

The amplitude of A stays at or below $1.5\,y_0$ for $\omega \le 16.33$ rad/s or $\omega \ge 23.09$ rad/s. Frequencies between these values, which surround the natural frequency $\omega_n = 20$ rad/s, must be avoided.
