Example 6.B.7Damping for 50 percent of critical

equivalent stiffnesscritical dampingdamping ratio

Problem

A block of mass $m$ hangs from the ceiling on a spring of stiffness $k$. Below the block, a spring of stiffness $2k$ and a dashpot with damping constant $c$ connect it to the floor. The coordinate $x$ measures the downward displacement of the block from static equilibrium.

The data are $k = 3000$ N/m and $m = 10$ kg.

Find the value of the damping constant $c$ for which the system has 50 percent of critical damping.

Solution

Every solution starts from free-body diagrams in a displaced position, then writes the equation of motion and reads off the vibration properties. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagram

In a position $x$ below static equilibrium the upper spring is stretched by $\delta_1 + x$ and pulls the block up. The lower spring is compressed by $\delta_2 + x$ and pushes the block up. Here $\delta_1$ and $\delta_2$ are the static deflections of the two springs. The dashpot force $c\dot x$ opposes the velocity.

The upper spring is stretched by $\delta_1 + x$ and the lower spring is compressed by $\delta_2 + x$. Both push or pull the block upward.

Step 2 · Write the equation of motion $$m\ddot x = mg - k\left(\delta_1 + x\right) - 2k\left(\delta_2 + x\right) - c\dot x$$

In static equilibrium $k\delta_1 + 2k\delta_2 = mg$, so the constant terms cancel and

$$m\ddot x + c\dot x + 3k\,x = 0$$

The two springs act in parallel because both resist the same displacement $x$, so their stiffnesses add, $k_{eq} = 3k$.

Step 3 · Find the critical damping constant

The damping ratio is $\zeta = c/c_{cr}$, where $c_{cr} = 2\sqrt{k_{eq}\,m}$.

$$c_{cr} = 2\sqrt{3k\,m} = 2\sqrt{3(3000)(10)} = 2\sqrt{90\,000} = 600\ \text{kg/s}$$
Step 4 · Solve for c $$c = \zeta\,c_{cr} = 0.5\,(600) = 300\ \text{kg/s}$$

The natural frequency is $\omega_n = \sqrt{3k/m} = 30$ rad/s.

Answer

$c = 300$ kg/s.

Explore

  • Set $c = 300$ kg/s and read the first overshoot from the graph. Compare it with the prediction $e^{-\pi\zeta/\sqrt{1-\zeta^2}} = 16.3$ percent of the initial displacement.
  • If the lower spring were removed, what value of $c$ would give 50 percent of critical damping?
