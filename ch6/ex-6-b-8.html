Example 6.B.8Block on a smooth incline with two springs and a dashpot

inclineequivalent stiffnesscritical damping

Problem

A block of mass $m$ rests on a smooth surface inclined at 30°. A spring of stiffness $4k$ and a dashpot with damping constant $c$ connect the block to a wall at the lower end of the incline. A spring of stiffness $k$ connects the block to a wall at the upper end. The springs and the dashpot are parallel to the incline. The coordinate $x$ measures the displacement of the block down the incline from static equilibrium.

The data are $k = 2000$ N/m and $m = 10$ kg.

Find the value of $c$ for which the system has 50 percent of critical damping.

Solution

Every solution starts from free-body diagrams in a displaced position, then writes the equation of motion and reads off the vibration properties. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagram

We draw the block a distance $x$ down the incline from static equilibrium. The lower spring is compressed by $\delta_1 + x$ and pushes the block up the incline. The upper spring is stretched by $\delta_2 + x$ and pulls the block up the incline. The dashpot force $c\dot x$ opposes the velocity. The smooth surface exerts only the normal force $N$, and the weight $mg$ acts vertically.

Both springs push or pull the block up the slope, the dashpot force opposes $\dot x$, and the smooth surface gives only the normal force $N$.

Step 2 · Write the equation of motion

Newton's second law along the incline, with $x$ positive down the incline, gives

$$m\ddot x = mg\sin 30° - 4k\left(\delta_1 + x\right) - k\left(\delta_2 + x\right) - c\dot x$$

In static equilibrium $4k\delta_1 + k\delta_2 = mg\sin 30°$, so

$$m\ddot x + c\dot x + 5k\,x = 0$$

The equation normal to the incline only gives $N = mg\cos 30°$.

Step 3 · Find the critical damping constant $$c_{cr} = 2\sqrt{5k\,m} = 2\sqrt{5(2000)(10)} = 2\sqrt{100\,000} = 632.5\ \text{kg/s}$$
Step 4 · Solve for c $$c = 0.5\,c_{cr} = 316.2\ \text{kg/s}$$

The natural frequency is $\omega_n = \sqrt{5k/m} = 31.6$ rad/s. Neither result depends on the angle of the incline.

Answer

$c = 316$ kg/s (more precisely 316.2 kg/s).
