Example 6.B.2Blocks on a ledge and a movable pulley with two springs

equation of motionnatural frequencypulleysstatic equilibrium

Problem

Blocks A and B, having masses $m$ and $3m$, are connected by a cable–pulley system as shown. A spring of stiffness $3k$ is attached between A and the ground. A second spring of stiffness $k$ is attached between block B and the ground. Let $x$ describe the position of A, where the springs are unstretched when $x = 0$. The horizontal surface is smooth and the pulleys are light.

Find (a) the EOM of this system in terms of the coordinate $x$, and (b) the natural frequency of the system.

The animation uses $m = 1$ kg and $k = 60$ N/m, with the system released from rest with both springs unstretched.

Solution

Every solution starts from free-body diagrams in a displaced position, then writes the equation of motion and reads off the vibration properties. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagrams

The left spring (stiffness $3k$) is stretched by $x$ and pulls A to the left. Block B has moved down $x/2$, so the spring below it is compressed by $x/2$ and pushes B up.

We draw block A displaced $x$ to the right, and the movable pulley together with block B, which has moved down by $x/2$.

Step 2 · Write the cable constraint

When A moves right by $x$, the horizontal cable segment shortens by $x$. The two vertical segments that hold the movable pulley share that length, so B moves down by $x/2$.

$$y_B = \frac{x}{2}, \qquad \ddot y_B = \frac{\ddot x}{2}$$
Step 3 · Apply Newton's second law

For A (right positive) and for the pulley with B (down positive),

$$T - 3kx = m\ddot x$$ $$3mg - 2T - k\,\frac{x}{2} = 3m\,\frac{\ddot x}{2} \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad T = \frac{3mg}{2} - \frac{kx}{4} - \frac{3m\ddot x}{4}$$
Step 4 · Write the equation of motion

Substituting $T$ into the equation for A gives

$$\frac{3mg}{2} - \frac{kx}{4} - \frac{3m\ddot x}{4} - 3kx = m\ddot x \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \tfrac74 m\ddot x + \tfrac{13}{4}kx = \tfrac32 mg$$ $$7m\ddot x + 13kx = 6mg$$

The right side is constant, so the system vibrates about the static equilibrium position $x_{eq} = 6mg/(13k)$. For the values in the animation, $x_{eq} = 6(1)(9.81)/(13 \cdot 60) = 0.0755$ m.

Step 5 · Find the natural frequency $$\omega_n = \sqrt{\frac{13k}{7m}}$$

For the values in the animation, $\omega_n = \sqrt{13(60)/(7 \cdot 1)} = 10.6$ rad/s, a period of 0.595 s.

Answer

(a) $$7m\ddot x + 13kx = 6mg$$ (b) $$\omega_n = \sqrt{\frac{13k}{7m}}$$

Explore

  • Release the system from equilibrium plus 5 cm. The oscillation is now centred on $x_{eq}$ with an amplitude of 5 cm, and the period is unchanged.
  • Block B has mass $3m$ but adds only $\tfrac34 m$ to the effective mass, and the spring $k$ adds only $\tfrac14 k$. Both come from the factor $1/2$ in the constraint, squared.
  • Check the energy method: $T = \tfrac12 m\dot x^2 + \tfrac12(3m)(\dot x/2)^2$ and $V = \tfrac12(3k)x^2 + \tfrac12 k(x/2)^2 - 3mg(x/2)$.
