Example 5.A.17Center of percussion of a pinned bar

Newton–Eulerimpact forcescenter of percussion

Problem

A ball of mass $m$ strikes a homogeneous bar of length $L$ and mass $M$ that hangs from a pin at O. The ball hits the bar at a distance $h$ below O.

Find the distance $h$ for which the horizontal reaction force at O on the bar is zero. This point is called the center of percussion of the bar.

The impact is modeled as a very stiff spring between the ball and the bar, so the force histories in the graph come from integrating the equations of motion. The pin force is always the same fraction of the contact force, $O_x/F = 3h/(2L) - 1$, and it vanishes when $h = 2L/3$. The bar has $M = 2$ kg and $L = 1$ m, and the ball has a mass of 0.5 kg and a speed of 3 m/s.

Solution

Every solution follows the four-step method. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagram during the impact

$F$ and $O_x$ are drawn positive in the direction in which the ball moves. The bar is still vertical during the short impact.

During the short impact the bar is acted on by the contact force $F$ from the ball at a distance $h$ below O, the pin reactions $O_x$ and $O_y$, and its weight $Mg$. The bar starts from rest and hardly moves during the impact, so it is still vertical. We take $F$ and $O_x$ as positive in the direction in which the ball is moving.

Step 2 · Write the kinetics equations

Newton's equation in the horizontal direction and Euler's equation about the fixed pin O are

$$F + O_x = M\,a_{Gx}, \qquad F\,h = I_O\,\alpha = \tfrac13 ML^2\,\alpha$$

The weight and $O_y$ have no moment about O while the bar is vertical.

Step 3 · Write the kinematics equation

G moves on a circle of radius $L/2$ about O. The bar starts from rest, so the horizontal acceleration of G is purely tangential.

$$a_{Gx} = \frac L2\,\alpha$$
Step 4 · Solve

Euler's equation gives $\alpha = 3Fh/(ML^2)$. Substituting into Newton's equation,

$$O_x = M\,\frac L2\,\frac{3Fh}{ML^2} - F = F\left(\frac{3h}{2L} - 1\right)$$

The pin force vanishes when

$$h = \frac23\,L$$

This result holds at every instant of the impact, because $O_x$ is proportional to $F$. A practical application is the "sweet spot" of a baseball bat or a tennis racket. When the ball hits the center of percussion, the hands at the pivot feel no sting.

Answer

$h = \tfrac23 L$. At that point $O_x = F\left(\tfrac{3h}{2L} - 1\right) = 0$.
