Example 5.C.9Rod falling on a sliding block

linear momentumwork–energysystems

Problem

A uniform rod of mass $m$ and length $L$ is pinned at its lower end A to a block of mass $M$. The block can slide on a smooth horizontal floor. The rod is released from rest when it makes the angle $\theta = 30°$ with the vertical.

The data are $m = 10$ kg, $M = 30$ kg and $L = 2$ m.

Find (a) the angular velocity of the rod and (b) the velocity of the block when $\theta = 90°$.

Solution

Every solution follows the four-step method. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagram

Take the rod and the block together as the system. The external forces are the two weights and the normal force from the smooth floor, and all of them are vertical. The pin force at A is internal.

All the external forces are vertical: the two weights $mg$ and $Mg$ and the normal forces $N_1$ and $N_2$ from the smooth floor. The pin force at A is internal. No external force acts horizontally, so the horizontal momentum of the system is conserved.

Step 2 · Write the kinetics equations

No external force acts horizontally, so the horizontal linear momentum of the system is conserved. It is zero at release.

$$M\,v_B + m\,v_{Gx} = 0$$

The floor is smooth and the pin is frictionless, so only gravity does work and the total mechanical energy is conserved.

$$m g\,\frac L2\cos 30° = \tfrac12 M v_B^2 + \tfrac12 m\left(v_{Gx}^2 + v_{Gy}^2\right) + \tfrac12\,\frac{mL^2}{12}\,\dot\theta^2 + m g\,\frac L2\cos\theta$$
Step 3 · Write the kinematics equations

The mass center of the rod is at $x_G = x_B + \tfrac L2\sin\theta$ and $y_G = \tfrac L2\cos\theta$, so

$$v_{Gx} = v_B + \tfrac L2\cos\theta\,\dot\theta, \qquad v_{Gy} = -\tfrac L2\sin\theta\,\dot\theta$$

When $\theta = 90°$, these become $v_{Gx} = v_B$ and $v_{Gy} = -\tfrac L2\dot\theta$.

Step 4 · Solve

At $\theta = 90°$ the momentum equation gives $(M + m)\,v_B = 0$, so $v_B = 0$. The energy equation then reduces to

$$m g\,\frac L2\cos 30° = \tfrac12 m\left(\frac L2\right)^2\dot\theta^2 + \tfrac12\,\frac{mL^2}{12}\,\dot\theta^2 = \frac{mL^2}{6}\,\dot\theta^2$$ $$\dot\theta = \sqrt{\frac{3g\cos 30°}{L}} = \sqrt{\frac{3(9.81)(0.866)}{2}} = 3.57\ \text{rad/s}$$

The block is momentarily at rest at this instant, but it has moved $\dfrac{m}{M + m}\cdot\dfrac L2\left(1 - \sin 30°\right) = 0.125$ m backward, because the mass center of the system cannot move horizontally.

Answer

(a) $\omega = 3.57$ rad/s, turning the rod toward the horizontal. (b) $v_B = 0$ at that instant.

  • Change the mass of the block. Why does the angular velocity at $\theta = 90°$ not change?
