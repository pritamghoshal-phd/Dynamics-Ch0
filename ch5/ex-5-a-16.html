Example 5.A.16Disk rolling on an accelerating cart

Newton–Eulerrollingsystems of bodies

Problem

A cart of mass $M$ rests on a smooth floor. A homogeneous disk of mass $m$ and radius $R$, with center G, sits on top of the cart and can roll on it without slipping. The system is initially at rest when a horizontal force $F$ is applied to the cart.

Find the acceleration of G.

Solution

Every solution follows the four-step method. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagrams

The disk and the cart push on each other at the contact point with equal and opposite forces $N$ and $f$. Friction is drawn in its assumed positive direction on the disk (forward), so it acts backward on the cart. The smooth floor exerts only the vertical forces $N_1$ and $N_2$. The curved arrow shows the positive (counterclockwise) sense of $\alpha$.

The disk is acted on by its weight $mg$, the normal force from the cart, and a friction force $f$ from the cart at the contact point. The cart is acted on by $F$, its weight, the floor reactions (vertical only, because the floor is smooth), and the reactions from the disk, which are equal and opposite to those on the disk. We take accelerations positive to the right and the angular acceleration $\alpha$ of the disk positive counterclockwise.

Step 2 · Write the kinetics equations

Disk, Newton's equation and Euler's equation about G:

$$f = m\,a_G, \qquad f\,R = \tfrac12 mR^2\,\alpha$$

Cart, Newton's equation in the horizontal direction:

$$F - f = M\,A$$

Here $A$ is the acceleration of the cart.

Step 3 · Write the kinematics equation

The disk does not slip on the cart, so the contact point of the disk has the same horizontal acceleration as the cart. The contact point lies a distance $R$ below G, so

$$a_G + R\,\alpha = A$$
Step 4 · Solve

The disk equations give $f = m a_G$ and $R\alpha = 2a_G$. The kinematic condition then gives $A = 3a_G$. Substituting into the cart equation,

$$F - m\,a_G = 3M\,a_G \quad\Longrightarrow\quad a_G = \frac{F}{3M + m}$$

The cart accelerates at $A = 3F/(3M + m)$, three times faster than the disk center, so the disk rolls toward the back of the cart while it moves forward.

Answer

$\mathbf a_G = \dfrac{F}{3M + m}\,\hat{\mathbf i}$, in the direction of $F$.

Explore

  • Make the disk very heavy compared with the cart. What happens to $a_G$, and does that agree with the formula?
  • Show that the friction force on the disk equals $mF/(3M + m)$, and check it with the statistic.
  • Replace the disk with a thin ring. Repeat the solution and compare.
