Example 5.A.6A bar hanging from an accelerating collar

Newton–Euleraccelerating pinpendulum

Problem

A collar O slides along a horizontal guide with a constant acceleration $a$ to the left. A thin homogeneous bar of length $L$ and mass $m$ is pinned to the collar and hangs below it.

Find the value of $a$ for which the bar stays at a constant angle $\theta = 25°$ from the vertical.

Solution

Every solution follows the four-step method. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagram

The bar is acted on by its weight $mg$ at its midpoint G and by the pin force at O, with horizontal component $O_x$ and vertical component $O_y$.

$O_x$ and $O_y$ are drawn in the positive $x$ and $y$ directions. The solution finds $O_x = -ma$, so the pin actually pulls the bar to the left.

Step 2 · Write the kinetics equations

If the angle stays constant, the bar moves with the collar. Then $a_G = a$ to the left and $\alpha = 0$. With $x$ to the right and $y$ upward, Newton's equation gives

$$O_x = -m\,a, \qquad O_y - mg = 0$$

G is at $\left(\tfrac L2\sin\theta,\ -\tfrac L2\cos\theta\right)$ relative to O, so O is at $\left(-\tfrac L2\sin\theta,\ \tfrac L2\cos\theta\right)$ relative to G. The moment about G must be zero.

$$\sum M_G = \left(-\tfrac L2\sin\theta\right)O_y - \left(\tfrac L2\cos\theta\right)O_x = 0$$
Step 3 · Write the kinematics equation

The bar translates with the collar, so $\alpha = 0$ and every point has acceleration $a$ to the left.

Step 4 · Solve

Substituting $O_x = -ma$ and $O_y = mg$ into the moment equation gives $-mg\sin\theta + ma\cos\theta = 0$, so

$$a = g\tan\theta = 9.81\tan 25° = 4.57\ \text{m/s}^2$$

The result does not depend on $m$ or $L$. The same answer follows from noticing that the pin force must point along the bar when there is no rotation, because otherwise it would have a moment about G.

Answer

$a = g\tan 25° \approx 0.466\,g \approx 4.57$ m/s² to the left.
