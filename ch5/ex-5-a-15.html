Example 5.A.15Sphere rolling down an incline

Newton–Eulerrollingfriction

Problem

A homogeneous sphere of mass $m$ and radius $R$ is released from rest on a rough incline of angle $\theta$. The sphere rolls without slipping.

Find (a) the acceleration of its center G and (b) the friction force that acts on the sphere.

Solution

Every solution follows the four-step method. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagram

The dashed line is the incline, shown only for reference. The normal force $N$ is perpendicular to it and the friction force $f$ points up the slope. The $x$ axis points down the slope, and $\alpha$ is positive clockwise (the rolling direction).

The sphere is acted on by its weight $mg$ at G, a normal force $N$ at the contact point perpendicular to the incline, and a friction force $f$ at the contact point along the incline. The friction force must point up the slope. It is the only force with a moment about G, and that moment must turn the sphere in the direction in which it rolls. We take the $x$ axis down the slope and the $y$ axis perpendicular to it.

Step 2 · Write the kinetics equations

Newton's equation in the directions along and perpendicular to the incline gives

$$mg\sin\theta - f = m\,a_G, \qquad N - mg\cos\theta = 0$$

Euler's equation about G, with clockwise taken as positive because that is the direction of rolling, gives

$$f\,R = I_G\,\alpha = \tfrac25 mR^2\,\alpha$$
Step 3 · Write the kinematics equation

Because the sphere rolls without slipping, $a_G = R\,\alpha$.

Step 4 · Solve

Combining Euler's equation with the rolling condition gives $f = \tfrac25 m\,a_G$. Substituting this into Newton's equation gives

$$mg\sin\theta = \tfrac75 m\,a_G \quad\Longrightarrow\quad a_G = \tfrac57\,g\sin\theta, \qquad f = \tfrac27\,mg\sin\theta$$

A shorter route is to take moments about the contact point C. The acceleration of C points toward G, so $\sum M_C = I_C\alpha$ with $I_C = \tfrac25mR^2 + mR^2 = \tfrac75mR^2$. The equation $mgR\sin\theta = \tfrac75 mR^2(a_G/R)$ then gives the same answer in one line.

The assumption of rolling without slipping requires $f \le \mu_s N$, which is equivalent to $\mu_s \ge \tfrac27\tan\theta$.

Answer

(a) $a_G = \tfrac57\,g\sin\theta$, directed down the incline. (b) $f = \tfrac27\,mg\sin\theta$, directed up the incline.

Explore

  • Set $\theta = 30°$, compute $a_G$ by hand and compare it with the statistic. Does the mass matter?
  • Find the steepest incline that a sphere with $\mu_s = 0.3$ can roll down without slipping.
  • Replace the sphere with a hoop, for which $I_G = mR^2$. Repeat Steps 2 to 4 on paper and compare the results.
