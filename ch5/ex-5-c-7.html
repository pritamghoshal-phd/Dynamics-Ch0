Example 5.C.7Bar striking a fixed stop

impactrestitutionhorizontal plane

Problem

A thin homogeneous bar of mass $m$ and length $L$ slides on a smooth horizontal surface. It translates without rotating, with its mass center G moving at speed $v_{G1}$ perpendicular to the bar. The bar strikes a fixed stop at point A, which lies a distance $L/4$ from G. The coefficient of restitution for the impact is $e$, with $0 < e < 1$.

Find (a) the velocity of the mass center G and (b) the angular velocity of the bar immediately after the impact.

Solution

Every solution follows the four-step method. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagram during the impact

During the impact the stop exerts a large impulse $\hat F$ on the bar at A, perpendicular to the bar and directed away from the stop. The weight and the normal force from the surface are perpendicular to the plane of motion, so they produce no impulse in the plane.

The stop pushes on the bar at A with the impulse $\hat F$, perpendicular to the bar and away from the stop (the positive $x$ direction). The weight and the normal force from the surface act perpendicular to the page and give no impulse in the plane. The grey arrow is the velocity of G before the impact, not a force.

Step 2 · Write the kinetics equations (impulse and momentum)

Take the $x$ direction perpendicular to the bar, positive away from the stop. Before the impact $v_{Gx} = -v_{G1}$ and $\omega_1 = 0$. Linear impulse and momentum, and angular impulse and momentum about G, give

$$m\,v_{G2} = -m\,v_{G1} + \hat F, \qquad I_G\,\omega_2 = \hat F\,\frac L4, \qquad I_G = \tfrac1{12} mL^2$$

Here $\omega_2$ is positive in the sense that moves point A away from the stop.

Step 3 · Write the kinematics equation (coefficient of restitution)

The stop is fixed, so the coefficient of restitution compares the velocity of A along $x$ after the impact with its value before the impact.

$$v_{A2} = v_{G2} + \omega_2\,\frac L4 = e\,v_{G1}$$
Step 4 · Solve

From the angular equation, $\omega_2 = \dfrac{3\hat F}{mL}$. Substituting both kinetics equations into the restitution equation gives $\tfrac74\hat F/m = (1 + e)\,v_{G1}$, so

$$\hat F = \tfrac47\,m\,(1 + e)\,v_{G1}, \qquad v_{G2} = \frac{4e - 3}{7}\,v_{G1}, \qquad \omega_2 = \frac{12\,(1 + e)}{7}\,\frac{v_{G1}}{L}$$

For $e < 3/4$ the mass center keeps moving toward the stop, more slowly. For $e = 3/4$ it stops, and for $e > 3/4$ it moves away. The kinetic energy after the impact never exceeds the kinetic energy before it, which can be checked in the statistics.

Answer

(a) $v_{G2} = \dfrac{4e - 3}{7}\,v_{G1}$, measured away from the stop, so the speed of G is $\dfrac{|4e - 3|}{7}\,v_{G1}$. (b) $\omega_2 = \dfrac{12(1 + e)}{7}\,\dfrac{v_{G1}}{L}$, in the sense that turns the struck end away from the stop.

Explore

  • Set $e = 1$. Show that the kinetic energy after the impact equals the kinetic energy before it.
  • Suppose the stop struck the bar exactly at G. Show that the bar would then not rotate and that $v_{G2} = e\,v_{G1}$.
