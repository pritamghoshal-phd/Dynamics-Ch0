Example 5.A.12Spool pulled at an angle

Newton–Eulerrollingmoment about the contact point

Problem

A spool has mass $m = 30$ kg, centroidal radius of gyration $k_G = 0.25$ m, outer radius $R = 0.3$ m and inner radius $r = 0.1$ m. A constant force $F = 60$ N is applied at an angle $\theta$ above the horizontal to a cord that is wrapped around the inner radius and leaves it from the lower side. The spool rolls without slipping on a rough horizontal surface.

Find the angular acceleration of the spool as a function of $\theta$.

Solution

Every solution follows the four-step method. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagram

The cord force $F$ leaves the hub tangentially, a distance $r$ from G. Friction $f$ is drawn in its assumed positive direction (to the right), and $\alpha$ is positive clockwise.

The spool is acted on by its weight $mg$ at G, the cord force $F$ at the point where the cord leaves the hub, and the normal force $N$ and friction force $f$ at the contact point C. We take the angular acceleration $\alpha$ as positive when it is clockwise, which corresponds to rolling to the right, and the acceleration $a_G$ as positive to the right.

Step 2 · Write the kinetics equations

Newton's equation in the horizontal direction and Euler's equation about G are

$$F\cos\theta + f = m\,a_G, \qquad -F\,r - f\,R = I_G\,\alpha \;\text{(clockwise +)}$$

The cord leaves the hub tangentially, so its moment arm about G is $r$ and it tends to turn the spool counterclockwise. Friction at C, if it points to the right, also turns the spool counterclockwise.

Step 3 · Write the kinematics equation

The spool rolls without slipping, so $a_G = R\,\alpha$.

Step 4 · Solve

Eliminate $f$ by multiplying Newton's equation by $R$ and adding it to Euler's equation. This is the same as taking moments about C.

$$F\,(R\cos\theta - r) = \left(I_G + mR^2\right)\alpha = m\left(k_G^2 + R^2\right)\alpha$$

With $m(k_G^2 + R^2) = 30(0.0625 + 0.09) = 4.575$ kg·m²,

$$\alpha = \frac{60\,(0.3\cos\theta - 0.1)}{4.575} = 3.93\cos\theta - 1.31\ \text{rad/s}^2 \quad(\text{clockwise})$$

The spool rolls to the right (toward the pull) when $\cos\theta > r/R = 1/3$, that is for $\theta < 70.5°$. For steeper pulls it rolls to the left, away from the pull. At $\theta = 70.5°$ it does not rotate.

Answer

The angular acceleration of the spool is

$$\boldsymbol\alpha = -\frac{F(R\cos\theta - r)}{m(k_G^2 + R^2)}\,\hat{\mathbf k} = -(3.93\cos\theta - 1.31)\,\hat{\mathbf k}\ \text{rad/s}^2$$
