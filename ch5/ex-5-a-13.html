Example 5.A.13Bar sliding down a smooth wall

Newton–Eulergeneral plane motionsmooth contacts

Problem

A thin, homogeneous bar AB of mass $m = 30$ kg and length $L = 4$ m rests with end A on a smooth floor and end B against a smooth vertical wall. The bar makes an angle $\theta = 36.87°$ with the floor. It is released from rest.

Find the angular acceleration of the bar at the instant of release.

Solution

Every solution follows the four-step method. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagram

Both surfaces are smooth, so the floor pushes up on A and the wall pushes horizontally on B, away from the wall (to the left). There is no friction. G is at the middle of the bar, a distance $L/2$ from each end.

The bar is acted on by its weight $mg$ at its center G, a vertical normal force $N_A$ from the floor at A, and a horizontal normal force $N_B$ from the wall at B, pointing away from the wall. Both surfaces are smooth, so there is no friction. We measure $\theta$ from the floor, so the bar turns counterclockwise when $\theta$ increases.

Step 2 · Write the kinetics equations

Place the origin where the wall meets the floor, with $x$ to the right and $y$ upward. Newton's equations for G and Euler's equation about G are

$$-N_B = m\,\ddot x_G, \qquad N_A - mg = m\,\ddot y_G$$ $$-N_A\,\frac L2\cos\theta + N_B\,\frac L2\sin\theta = \frac{1}{12}mL^2\,\ddot\theta$$
Step 3 · Write the kinematics equations

The ends stay on the floor and the wall, so G is at $x_G = -\tfrac L2\cos\theta$ and $y_G = \tfrac L2\sin\theta$. The bar starts from rest, so at release $\dot\theta = 0$ and

$$\ddot x_G = \frac L2\sin\theta\,\ddot\theta, \qquad \ddot y_G = \frac L2\cos\theta\,\ddot\theta$$
Step 4 · Solve

Substitute $N_B = -m\ddot x_G$ and $N_A = mg + m\ddot y_G$ into Euler's equation. The terms in $\ddot\theta$ combine to $\tfrac13 mL^2\ddot\theta$, and

$$\ddot\theta = -\frac{3g}{2L}\cos\theta = -\frac{3(9.81)}{2(4)}(0.8) = -2.94\ \text{rad/s}^2$$

The negative sign means that $\theta$ decreases, so the bar turns clockwise as it slides down the wall.

The reactions at release follow from the same equations: $N_A = 153.0$ N and $N_B = 105.9$ N.

Answer

$\boldsymbol\alpha = -2.94\,\hat{\mathbf k}$ rad/s², that is 2.94 rad/s² clockwise.
