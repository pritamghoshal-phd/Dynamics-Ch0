Example 5.B.8Garage door with a spring

work–energyspringsconstrained motion

Problem

End A of a garage door moves in a smooth horizontal guide, and the midpoint B of the door moves in a smooth vertical guide. The door has length 10 ft (5 ft on each side of B) and weight $W = 200$ lb. A spring of stiffness $k = 40$ lb/ft along the vertical guide connects the corner D, where the guides meet, to B. The spring is unstretched when $\theta = 30°$, where $\theta$ is the angle between the door and the horizontal guide. The door is released from rest at $\theta = 30°$.

Find the speed of end A when the door reaches the vertical position.

Solution

Every solution follows the four-step method. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagram

The door is acted on by its weight $W$ at B, the spring force at B, the normal force from the horizontal guide at A and the normal force from the vertical guide at B. The guides are smooth, so each normal force is perpendicular to the motion of its point and does no work. Gravity and the spring are conservative.

The spring runs along the vertical guide from the corner D to B, so the spring force $F_s$ acts upward at B. The smooth guides push perpendicular to the paths of A and B, so $N_A$ and $N_B$ do no work. Only the weight $W$ and the spring force do work, and both are conservative.

Step 2 · Write the kinetics equation (work–energy)

Mechanical energy is conserved, $T_1 + V_1 = T_2 + V_2$, with $T_1 = 0$. The mass center G coincides with B, so

$$T = \tfrac12 m v_B^2 + \tfrac12 I_G\,\omega^2, \qquad I_G = \tfrac1{12}\frac{W}{g}(10)^2 = \tfrac1{12}\left(\frac{200}{32.2}\right)(100) = 51.76\ \text{slug·ft}^2$$

B is $5\sin\theta$ below D. It starts 2.5 ft below D and ends 5 ft below D, so it falls 2.5 ft. The spring length equals $5\sin\theta$, so it is unstretched at 2.5 ft and stretched by 2.5 ft at the end.

$$V_1 - V_2 = W(2.5) - \tfrac12 k(2.5)^2 = 500 - 125 = 375\ \text{ft·lb}$$
Step 3 · Write the kinematics equations

The positions are $x_A = -5\cos\theta$ (A to the left of D) and $y_B = -5\sin\theta$. Differentiating gives

$$v_A = 5\sin\theta\,\omega, \qquad v_B = 5\cos\theta\,\omega$$

When the door is vertical, $\theta = 90°$, so $v_B = 0$ and $v_A = 5\omega$. At that instant the door rotates about B, and $T_2 = \tfrac12 I_G\omega^2$.

Step 4 · Solve $$\tfrac12(51.76)\,\omega^2 = 375 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \omega = 3.81\ \text{rad/s}, \qquad v_A = 5\omega = 19.0\ \text{ft/s}$$

A direct simulation of the equation of motion from $\theta = 30°$ gives $v_A = 19.03$ ft/s at $\theta = 90°$.

Answer

$v_A = 19.0$ ft/s, directed toward the corner D, when the door is vertical.
