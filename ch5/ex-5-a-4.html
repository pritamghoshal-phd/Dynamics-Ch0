Example 5.A.4A rod held at an angle in an accelerating frame

Newton–Euleraccelerating framesmooth contact

Problem

A uniform slender rod AB of length $L$ rests inside a frame. End A touches the smooth left wall of the frame, and end B rests on the smooth floor of the frame, so the rod makes an angle $\theta$ with the floor. The frame moves to the right with acceleration $a$.

Find the acceleration $a$ for which the rod keeps the orientation shown.

Solution

Every solution follows the four-step method. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagram

The rod is acted on by its weight $mg$ at its midpoint G, by a normal force $N_A$ from the wall at A, which is horizontal and points to the right, and by a normal force $N_B$ from the floor at B, which points up. Both surfaces are smooth, so there are no friction forces.

Both surfaces are smooth, so only normal forces act at A and B.

Step 2 · Write the kinetics equations

If the rod keeps its orientation, it moves with the frame, so $a_G = a$ to the right and $\alpha = 0$. Newton's equation gives

$$N_A = m\,a, \qquad N_B - mg = 0$$

Relative to G, end A is at $\left(-\tfrac L2\cos\theta,\ \tfrac L2\sin\theta\right)$ and end B is at $\left(\tfrac L2\cos\theta,\ -\tfrac L2\sin\theta\right)$. The moment about G must be zero.

$$\sum M_G = -N_A\,\tfrac L2\sin\theta + N_B\,\tfrac L2\cos\theta = 0$$
Step 3 · Write the kinematics equation

The rod translates with the frame, so every point has acceleration $a$ to the right and $\alpha = 0$.

Step 4 · Solve

The moment equation gives $N_A\sin\theta = N_B\cos\theta$. Substituting $N_A = m a$ and $N_B = m g$ gives

$$a = g\cot\theta = \frac{g}{\tan\theta}$$

The answer does not depend on $m$ or $L$. The equilibrium is unstable. If $a$ is slightly larger, the rod swings up toward the wall, and if $a$ is slightly smaller, it slides down toward the floor.

Answer

$a = g\cot\theta$, directed toward the floor end B (to the right in the figure).
