Example 5.C.8Raising the bars on a spinning frame

angular momentumconservationmoment of inertia

Problem

Two thin homogeneous bars, each of length $2R$ and mass $m$, are pinned to a frame of negligible mass at a distance $R$ from the fixed vertical axis of rotation. Initially the frame rotates at $\Omega_1$, with both bars hanging straight down and at rest relative to the frame (position 1). The bars are then slowly raised until they point horizontally outward, perpendicular to the axis (position 2).

The data are $m = 10$ kg, $\Omega_1 = 8$ rad/s and $R = 0.5$ m.

Find the angular speed $\Omega_2$ of the frame in position 2.

InteractiveThe bars are raised, held and lowered again

The bars swing slowly in the vertical plane that contains the axis. The graph shows $\Omega = \Omega_1/\left(1 + 2\sin\phi + \tfrac43\sin^2\phi\right)$, where $\phi$ is the angle of the bars from the vertical.

Solution

Every solution follows the four-step method. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagram

Take the frame and both bars as the system. The external forces are the weights, which are parallel to the axis, and the bearing forces, which pass through the axis. None of these has a moment about the axis of rotation. The forces that raise the bars are internal.

In both positions the weights are parallel to the axis and the bearing forces $O_x$ and $O_z$ pass through it, so no external force has a moment about the axis. The forces that raise the bars act between the bars and the frame and are internal. ($O_x$ stands for the horizontal bearing force in any direction.)

Step 2 · Write the kinetics equation

The angular momentum about the vertical axis is conserved, and the frame has no mass.

$$I_1\,\Omega_1 = I_2\,\Omega_2$$
Step 3 · Find the moments of inertia about the axis

In position 1 every particle of each bar is at the distance $R$ from the axis, so each bar contributes $mR^2$ and $I_1 = 2mR^2$.

In position 2 each bar lies along a radius, from $r = R$ to $r = 3R$. With mass $m/(2R)$ per unit length,

$$I_{bar} = \int_R^{3R} r^2\,\frac{m}{2R}\,dr = \frac{m}{2R}\cdot\frac{(3R)^3 - R^3}{3} = \frac{13}{3}\,mR^2, \qquad I_2 = \frac{26}{3}\,mR^2$$
Step 4 · Solve $$\Omega_2 = \frac{I_1}{I_2}\,\Omega_1 = \frac{2}{26/3}\,\Omega_1 = \frac{3}{13}\,\Omega_1 = \frac{3}{13}(8) = 1.85\ \text{rad/s}$$

The values of $m$ and $R$ cancel. The kinetic energy drops to $3/13$ of its initial value, because the mechanism that raises the bars must hold them back against their tendency to swing outward.

Answer

$\Omega_2 = \tfrac{3}{13}\,\Omega_1 = 1.85$ rad/s.
