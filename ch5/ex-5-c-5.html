Example 5.C.5Bullet fired into a pendulum

impulse–momentumimpactangular momentum

Problem

A bullet of mass $m$ travels horizontally with speed $v_{b1}$. It strikes a stationary pendulum at a distance $h$ below the pivot O and becomes embedded at point A. The pendulum has mass $M$ and radius of gyration $k_O$ about O, and its mass center G lies a distance $L$ below O.

The data are $v_{b1} = 1500$ ft/s, $m = 0.1$ slug, $M = 2$ slugs, $h = 4$ ft, $k_O = 3$ ft and $L = 3$ ft.

Find the angular speed of the pendulum immediately after the impact.

Solution

Every solution follows the four-step method. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagram during the impact

We take the system consisting of the bullet and the pendulum. During the very short impact, the large forces are the contact force between the bullet and the pendulum, which is internal, and the pin reaction at O, which is external and may also be large. The weights are ordinary forces and produce a negligible impulse during the impact.

During the impact the only large external force is the pin reaction at O, drawn as its impulse components $\hat O_x$ and $\hat O_y$. They pass through O, so they have no moment about O. The weights give a negligible impulse in the short impact time and are left out. The grey arrow is the velocity of the bullet before impact, not a force; its line of action is the distance $h$ below O.

Step 2 · Write the kinetics equation (angular impulse and momentum about O)

The pin force passes through O, so it has no moment about O. The angular momentum about O is therefore conserved during the impact.

$$H_{O1} = H_{O2} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad m\,v_{b1}\,h = \left(M k_O^2 + m h^2\right)\omega_2$$

Linear momentum is not conserved, because the pin pushes on the pendulum during the impact.

Step 3 · Write the kinematics equations

Before the impact the pendulum is at rest, and the momentum of the bullet, $m v_{b1}$, acts along a line a distance $h$ below O. After the impact the bullet moves with point A, so $v_A = h\,\omega_2$ and its contribution to $H_{O2}$ is $m h^2\omega_2$.

Step 4 · Solve $$\omega_2 = \frac{m\,v_{b1}\,h}{M k_O^2 + m h^2} = \frac{0.1(1500)(4)}{2(3)^2 + 0.1(4)^2} = \frac{600}{19.6} = 30.6\ \text{rad/s}$$

About 92% of the kinetic energy of the bullet is lost in the impact. The distance $L$ is not needed to find $\omega_2$. It matters only for the swing after the impact.

Answer

Immediately after the impact, $\omega_2 = 30.6$ rad/s.
