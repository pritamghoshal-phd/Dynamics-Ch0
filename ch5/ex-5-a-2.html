Example 5.A.2Why a braking car dips its nose

Newton–Eulerload transferfriction

Problem

An automobile travels to the right and brakes. Its mass center G is a height $d$ above the road, a distance $b_R$ in front of the rear axle and a distance $b_F$ behind the front axle. It is observed that the front of the car dips toward the road, or "nose dives", during braking.

Explain why this happens.

Solution

Every solution follows the four-step method. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagram

The car is acted on by its weight $mg$ at G and by the road at each axle. At the rear axle the road applies a normal force $N_R$ and a braking friction force, and at the front axle it applies $N_F$ and a braking friction force. Both friction forces point backward, to the left. Their sum is $F$.

Both braking friction forces point backward. Their sum is $F = F_R + F_F$.

Step 2 · Write the kinetics equations

Let the car decelerate at a rate $a$, so $a_G = -a$ in the direction of travel. Treating the car as a single rigid body that translates, Newton's equation gives

$$-F = -m\,a, \qquad N_R + N_F - mg = 0$$

Because the body does not rotate, the moments about G must add to zero. The friction forces act at the road, a distance $d$ below G, and point backward, so they produce a moment that tends to rotate the car nose down. The normal forces must balance it.

$$\sum M_G = N_F\,b_F - N_R\,b_R - F\,d = 0$$
Step 3 · Write the kinematics equation

Apart from the small motion of the suspension, the car translates, so $\alpha = 0$ and every point decelerates at the rate $a$.

Step 4 · Solve

Solving the three equations gives

$$N_F = \frac{m\,(g\,b_R + a\,d)}{b_R + b_F}, \qquad N_R = \frac{m\,(g\,b_F - a\,d)}{b_R + b_F}$$

When the car is not braking ($a = 0$), these reduce to the static loads. During braking, a force $m a d/(b_R + b_F)$ is transferred from the rear axle to the front axle. The front springs carry more load and compress, while the rear springs carry less and extend. The body therefore rotates nose down.

The transfer grows with the deceleration and with the height of G. If $a\,d > g\,b_F$, the rear normal force would become negative, and the rear wheels would lift off the road.

Answer

The braking friction forces act at the road, below G, and produce a moment that tends to pitch the car forward. To keep the body from rotating, the road must push harder on the front wheels and less hard on the rear wheels: $N_F = m(g b_R + a d)/(b_R + b_F)$ and $N_R = m(g b_F - a d)/(b_R + b_F)$. The extra load compresses the front suspension, so the nose dips.
