Example 5.C.3Block sliding out along a spinning disk

angular momentumconservationenergy

Problem

A disk with mass moment of inertia $I_O$ about its center O turns freely about a fixed vertical shaft through O. A small block P of mass $m$ can slide in a smooth radial slot of the disk. The block is displaced slightly from the center when the disk turns at angular speed $\omega_1$, and it then slides outward.

Find the velocity of the block as a function of its distance $r$ from O.

Solution

Every solution follows the four-step method. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagram

Take the disk and the block together as the system. The external forces are the weights, which are vertical, and the bearing forces at the shaft, which pass through the axis. None of them has a moment about the vertical axis through O. The force between the block and the smooth slot is internal and acts perpendicular to the slot.

Left: the weights $W_D$ and $mg$ are vertical and the bearing forces at the shaft pass through the axis, so no external force has a moment about the axis. Right: the smooth slot can only push on the block perpendicular to the slot, in the transverse direction. Viewed for the whole system, this force is internal.

Step 2 · Write the kinetics equations

Because no external moment acts about the axis, the angular momentum about O is conserved. The block starts essentially at the center, so

$$I_O\,\omega_1 = \left(I_O + m r^2\right)\omega$$

The slot is smooth and the shaft does no work, so the kinetic energy of the system is also conserved.

$$\tfrac12 I_O\,\omega_1^2 = \tfrac12 I_O\,\omega^2 + \tfrac12 m\left(\dot r^2 + r^2\omega^2\right)$$
Step 3 · Write the kinematics equations

In polar coordinates that turn with the disk, the velocity of the block has a radial part $\dot r$ along the slot and a transverse part $r\omega$ perpendicular to it.

$$\mathbf v_P = \dot r\,\hat{\mathbf e}_r + r\omega\,\hat{\mathbf e}_\theta$$
Step 4 · Solve

The angular momentum equation gives $\omega = \dfrac{I_O\,\omega_1}{I_O + m r^2}$. Substituting this into the energy equation and simplifying gives

$$\dot r^2 = \frac{I_O\,\omega_1^2 - \left(I_O + m r^2\right)\omega^2}{m} = \frac{I_O\,\omega_1^2\,r^2}{I_O + m r^2}$$ $$\mathbf v_P = \omega_1 r\sqrt{\frac{I_O}{I_O + m r^2}}\;\hat{\mathbf e}_r + \frac{I_O\,\omega_1\,r}{I_O + m r^2}\;\hat{\mathbf e}_\theta$$
Answer

$\mathbf v_P = \omega_1 r\sqrt{\dfrac{I_O}{I_O + m r^2}}\;\hat{\mathbf e}_r + \dfrac{I_O\,\omega_1\,r}{I_O + m r^2}\;\hat{\mathbf e}_\theta$, where $\hat{\mathbf e}_r$ points outward along the slot.
