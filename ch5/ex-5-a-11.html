Example 5.A.11Stepped drum with a pulley

Newton–Eulerfixed-axis rotationcables

Problem

A stepped drum of mass $M$ has a radius of gyration $k_O$ about its center O and turns on a smooth shaft at O. A cable wrapped around the outer radius $R$ hangs vertically and supports block A. A second cable is wrapped around the inner radius $r$. It leaves the top of the inner drum horizontally, passes over an ideal pulley and supports block B. Neither cable slips on the drum, and the system is released from rest.

The data are $m_A = 10$ kg, $m_B = 30$ kg, $M = 20$ kg, $r = 0.2$ m, $R = 0.4$ m and $k_O = 0.25$ m.

Find the angular acceleration of the drum at the instant of release, expressed as a vector.

Solution

Every solution follows the four-step method. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagrams

$T_A$ acts at the left edge of the outer drum (radius $R$) and $T_B$ at the top of the inner drum (radius $r$). The ideal pulley passes $T_B$ on unchanged. The curved arrow shows the positive (clockwise) sense of $\alpha$.

The drum is acted on by its weight $Mg$ and the pin reactions $O_x$ and $O_y$ at its center. The tension $T_A$ pulls downward at the left edge of the outer drum, a distance $R$ from O. The tension $T_B$ pulls to the right at the top of the inner drum, a distance $r$ from O.

Block A is acted on by the tension $T_A$ upward and its weight $m_A g$ downward. Block B is acted on by the tension $T_B$ upward and its weight $m_B g$ downward. The ideal pulley passes the tension on without changing it.

We take the rotation of the drum as positive when it is clockwise, which is the direction in which the weight of B tends to turn it. The displacement of A is positive upward and the displacement of B is positive downward.

Step 2 · Write the kinetics equations

For the drum we take moments about the fixed point O. The pin reactions and the weight $Mg$ pass through O, so they do not appear.

$$T_B\,r - T_A\,R = I_O\,\alpha = M k_O^2\,\alpha$$

Newton's second law for the two blocks gives

$$T_A - m_A g = m_A a_A, \qquad m_B g - T_B = m_B a_B$$
Step 3 · Write the kinematics equations

The cables do not slip, so each block moves with the surface of the drum from which it hangs.

$$a_A = R\,\alpha, \qquad a_B = r\,\alpha$$
Step 4 · Solve

We solve the block equations for the tensions and substitute them into the drum equation. This gives

$$\alpha = \frac{g\,(m_B r - m_A R)}{M k_O^2 + m_A R^2 + m_B r^2} = \frac{9.81\,(30\cdot0.2 - 10\cdot0.4)}{20(0.25)^2 + 10(0.4)^2 + 30(0.2)^2} = \frac{19.62}{4.05} = 4.84\ \text{rad/s}^2$$

The result is positive, so the drum turns clockwise as assumed. With $\hat{\mathbf k}$ pointing out of the page, a clockwise rotation corresponds to the $-\hat{\mathbf k}$ direction.

As a check, the tensions are $T_A = m_A(g + R\alpha) = 117.5$ N and $T_B = m_B(g - r\alpha) = 265.2$ N. Then $T_B r - T_A R = 6.05$ N·m, which equals $M k_O^2\alpha = 1.25\alpha$. The denominator of the expression for $\alpha$ can be interpreted as the effective moment of inertia of the whole system about O.

Answer

$\boldsymbol\alpha = -4.84\,\hat{\mathbf k}$ rad/s², which is 4.84 rad/s² clockwise.

Explore

  • Increase $k_O$. Which of the two tensions changes more, and why?
