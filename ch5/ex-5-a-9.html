Example 5.A.9Bearing force on a bar driven by a moment

Newton–Eulerfixed-axis rotationbearing reactions

Problem

A thin, homogeneous bar of weight $W = 20$ lb is attached to a vertical shaft at O. One part of the bar extends 18 in from the shaft and the other part extends 12 in. A moment $M = 100$ lb·in is applied to the shaft while the bar is at rest.

Find the horizontal reaction force $R$ that the bearings of the shaft exert at that instant.

The animation shows the first quarter turn and then repeats. The red arrow is the horizontal force of the bearings on the bar, drawn to scale. At the instant of release it is perpendicular to the bar. As the bar picks up speed, a component directed toward the shaft appears, because G then also needs a normal acceleration $d\omega^2$.

Solution

Every solution follows the four-step method. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagram

Seen from above, the bearing force $R$ acts at O perpendicular to the bar, in the direction in which G starts to move. The weight $W$ points into the page at G and the vertical bearing force $V$ points out of the page at O. Both are parallel to the axis of rotation, so they have no moment about it.

The bar is acted on by the applied moment $M$, its weight $W$ (vertical), and the bearing reactions. In the horizontal plane the bearings exert a single force $R$, and in the vertical direction they support the weight. The bar is 30 in long, so its mass center G lies 15 in from either end. That places G a distance $d = 18 - 15 = 3$ in from the shaft, on the longer side.

Step 2 · Write the kinetics equations

The shaft is a fixed axis, so Euler's equation about O gives the angular acceleration. The weight is parallel to the axis and the bearing force passes through it, so only $M$ has a moment about the axis.

$$M = I_O\,\alpha, \qquad I_O = \frac{W}{g}\left(\frac{L^2}{12} + d^2\right)$$

In the horizontal plane, Newton's equation states that the bearing force is the only horizontal force on the bar. The moment $M$ is a couple, so it contributes no net force.

$$R = \frac{W}{g}\,a_G$$
Step 3 · Write the kinematics equations

G moves on a circle of radius $d$ about the shaft. The bar starts from rest, so $\omega = 0$ and the normal acceleration $d\,\omega^2$ is zero at this instant. Only the tangential acceleration remains.

$$a_G = d\,\alpha$$
Step 4 · Solve

With $W/g = 20/386.4 = 0.05176$ lb·s²/in, the moment of inertia is

$$I_O = 0.05176\left(\frac{30^2}{12} + 3^2\right) = 0.05176\,(84) = 4.348\ \text{lb·in·s}^2$$

so $\alpha = 100/4.348 = 23.0$ rad/s². The bearing force is then

$$R = \frac{W}{g}\,d\,\alpha = \frac{M\,d}{L^2/12 + d^2} = \frac{100\,(3)}{84} = 3.57\ \text{lb}$$

The weight cancels out, so the answer does not depend on $W$. The force $R$ is horizontal and perpendicular to the bar. The bearings must also support the 20 lb weight, and because G is not on the shaft they carry a couple $W d = 60$ lb·in about a horizontal axis as well.

Answer

$R = \dfrac{M d}{L^2/12 + d^2} = 3.57$ lb, horizontal and perpendicular to the bar, with $\alpha = 23.0$ rad/s².
