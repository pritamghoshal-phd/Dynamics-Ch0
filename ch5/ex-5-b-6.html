Example 5.B.6Bar pushing a rolling disk

work–energyrollingsmooth contact

Problem

A disk of mass $m$ and radius $R$ rolls without slipping along a rough straight guide. A thin, homogeneous bar of mass $m$ and length $L$ is pinned at O and rests against the disk along a smooth contact. O lies on the line through the center C of the disk, parallel to the guide. A constant force $F$ acts at the free end of the bar and is always perpendicular to the bar. The system is released from rest with C a distance $d$ from O, and everything moves in a horizontal plane.

Find the speed of C after C has moved an additional distance $d$ away from O.

Solution

Every solution follows the four-step method. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagrams

We take the system made of the bar and the disk. The external forces in the plane of motion are $F$, the pin reaction at O, and the normal and friction forces from the guide at the contact point of the disk. The contact force between the bar and the disk is internal. The motion is in a horizontal plane, so gravity does no work.

$P$ is the smooth contact force between the bar and the disk. It is internal to the system, and its work on the bar cancels its work on the disk. The pin reaction ($O_x$, $O_y$) acts at the fixed point O, and $N$ and $f$ act at the contact point of the rolling disk, which has zero velocity. None of these forces does work, so only $F$ does.

Step 2 · Write the kinetics equation (work–energy)

The pin reaction does no work because O does not move. The friction and normal forces from the guide act at the contact point of a rolling disk, which has zero velocity, so they do no work. The bar–disk contact is smooth, so the only force there is normal to the surfaces, and since the two bodies stay in contact, its work on the bar cancels its work on the disk. Only $F$ does work. Because $F$ stays perpendicular to the bar at the distance $L$ from O, it acts like a moment $FL$, and its work is

$$U_{1\to2} = F L\,(\varphi_1 - \varphi_2)$$

where $\varphi$ is the angle between the bar and the line OC. The kinetic energy of the system is

$$T = \tfrac12\left(\tfrac13 mL^2\right)\dot\varphi^2 + \tfrac12 m v_C^2 + \tfrac12\left(\tfrac12 mR^2\right)\left(\frac{v_C}{R}\right)^2 = \tfrac16 mL^2\dot\varphi^2 + \tfrac34 m v_C^2$$
Step 3 · Write the kinematics equations

The bar is tangent to the disk, so the distance from C to the bar is $R = x_C\sin\varphi$, where $x_C$ is the distance from O to C. Therefore

$$\sin\varphi = \frac{R}{x_C}, \qquad \dot\varphi = -\frac{R\,v_C}{x_C\sqrt{x_C^2 - R^2}}$$

At the start $x_C = d$, so $\varphi_1 = \sin^{-1}(R/d)$. At the end $x_C = 2d$, so $\varphi_2 = \sin^{-1}(R/2d)$ and $\dot\varphi_2^2 = \dfrac{R^2 v_C^2}{4d^2(4d^2 - R^2)}$.

Step 4 · Solve

Setting $U_{1\to2} = T_2$ with $T_1 = 0$ gives

$$v_C = \sqrt{\frac{F L\left[\sin^{-1}(R/d) - \sin^{-1}(R/2d)\right]}{\dfrac34 m + \dfrac{m L^2 R^2}{24\,d^2\,(4d^2 - R^2)}}}$$

With the sample values used in the animation ($F = 10$ N, $m = 2$ kg, $R = 0.2$ m, $L = 1.2$ m, $d = 0.4$ m) this gives $v_C = 1.448$ m/s. Integrating the equation of motion gives the same value, and the contact force between the bar and the disk stays positive throughout, so they never separate.

Answer

$v_C = \sqrt{\dfrac{F L\left[\sin^{-1}(R/d) - \sin^{-1}(R/2d)\right]}{\tfrac34 m + m L^2 R^2/\left[24\,d^2(4d^2 - R^2)\right]}}$, directed away from O.
