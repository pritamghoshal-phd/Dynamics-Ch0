Example 5.A.1A crate sliding on two feet

Newton–Eulerfrictiontranslation

Problem

A crate of mass $m$ rests on two small feet, A at the front and B at the rear, which are a horizontal distance $2b$ apart. The mass center G is midway between the feet and a height $d$ above the floor. The crate slides to the right on a rough floor with coefficient of kinetic friction $\mu_k$.

Find the reaction forces that the floor exerts on the crate at A and at B.

Solution

Every solution follows the four-step method. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagram

The crate is acted on by its weight $mg$ at G and by a normal force and a friction force at each foot. Because the crate slides to the right, kinetic friction at each foot points to the left and has magnitude $\mu_k$ times the normal force at that foot. We use $x$ to the right and $y$ upward.

The crate slides to the right, so kinetic friction at each foot points to the left.

Step 2 · Write the kinetics equations

The crate translates without rotating, so $\alpha = 0$ and the moment about G must be zero. Newton's equation in the two directions gives

$$-\mu_k N_A - \mu_k N_B = m\,a_G$$ $$N_A + N_B - mg = 0$$

Taking counterclockwise moments about G, the normal force at A acts a distance $b$ in front of G, the normal force at B acts a distance $b$ behind it, and both friction forces act a distance $d$ below it. Each friction force points to the left from a point below G, so each produces a clockwise moment.

$$\sum M_G = (N_A - N_B)\,b - \mu_k (N_A + N_B)\,d = 0$$
Step 3 · Write the kinematics equation

The crate translates, so every point has the acceleration of G, and $\alpha = 0$. No further kinematics is needed.

Step 4 · Solve

The moment equation gives

$$N_A - N_B = \frac{\mu_k d}{b}\,(N_A + N_B) = \frac{\mu_k d}{b}\,mg$$

Combining this with $N_A + N_B = mg$ gives

$$N_A = \frac{mg}{2}\left(1 + \frac{\mu_k d}{b}\right), \qquad N_B = \frac{mg}{2}\left(1 - \frac{\mu_k d}{b}\right)$$

The friction forces are $\mu_k N_A$ and $\mu_k N_B$, both to the left, and the acceleration is $a_G = -\mu_k g$.

The front foot carries more of the weight. The friction forces act below G and try to tip the crate forward, so the floor must push harder at the front to keep it level. The result is valid only while $N_B > 0$, that is, while $\mu_k d < b$. A tall crate on a high-friction floor would tip forward instead.

Answer

At A: normal force $N_A = \dfrac{mg}{2}\left(1 + \dfrac{\mu_k d}{b}\right)$ upward and friction $\mu_k N_A$ to the left.

At B: normal force $N_B = \dfrac{mg}{2}\left(1 - \dfrac{\mu_k d}{b}\right)$ upward and friction $\mu_k N_B$ to the left.
