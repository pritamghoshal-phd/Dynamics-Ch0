Example 5.B.5Two links and a slider pushed by a force

work–energylinkagesconstant force

Problem

Link OA is pinned to the ceiling at O, and link AB connects pin A to a slider block at B that moves in a vertical guide directly below O. Both links are homogeneous, with mass $m$ and length $L$, and the slider has mass $M$. The angle $\theta$ of link OA is measured from the vertical. A constant horizontal force $F$ acts at pin A, as shown in the book's figure. The system moves in a vertical plane and is released from rest at $\theta = 0$.

The data are $m = 5$ kg, $M = 10$ kg, $L = 2$ m and $F = 800$ N.

Find the speed of B when $\theta = 53.13°$.

Solution

Every solution follows the four-step method. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagram

We take the whole system: both links, the slider and the pins. The external forces are the force $F$ at A, the weights $mg$, $mg$ and $Mg$, the pin reaction at O and the normal force from the smooth guide on the slider. The pin reaction at O does no work because O does not move. The guide force is horizontal while B moves vertically, so it does no work either. The forces in the pins A and B are internal and their work cancels.

The problem statement in the book says that $F$ acts at pin B, but the figure shows it at pin A. A horizontal force at B would do no work, because B moves vertically, so we use the figure.

The pin forces at A and B are internal to the system and are not drawn. The pin reaction ($O_x$, $O_y$) does no work because O is fixed, and the guide force $N$ is horizontal while B moves vertically, so it does no work either. Only $F$ and the three weights do work.

Step 2 · Write the kinetics equation (work–energy)

The system starts from rest, so $U_{1\to2} = T_2$. With O as the origin and $\theta$ measured from the vertical, the positions are $x_A = L\sin\theta$, the center of OA is $\tfrac L2\cos\theta$ below O, the center of AB is $\tfrac{3L}{2}\cos\theta$ below O, and B is $2L\cos\theta$ below O. Each of these points rises by its depth times $(1 - \cos\theta)$.

$$U_{1\to2} = F L\sin\theta - mg\,\tfrac L2(1-\cos\theta) - mg\,\tfrac{3L}2(1-\cos\theta) - Mg\,2L(1-\cos\theta)$$

At $\theta = 53.13°$ we have $\sin\theta = 0.8$ and $\cos\theta = 0.6$, so

$$U_{1\to2} = 800(2)(0.8) - 5(9.81)(0.4) - 5(9.81)(1.2) - 10(9.81)(1.6) = 1280 - 235.4 = 1044.6\ \text{J}$$
Step 3 · Write the kinematics equations

Let $\omega = \dot\theta$ be the angular speed of OA. Because the two links have equal length, AB makes the same angle $\theta$ with the vertical on the other side, so it turns with the same angular speed in the opposite sense. Differentiating the positions gives

$$v_A = L\omega, \qquad v_{G,AB}^2 = L^2\omega^2\left(\tfrac14\cos^2\theta + \tfrac94\sin^2\theta\right), \qquad v_B = 2L\sin\theta\,\omega$$

The kinetic energy of the system is then

$$T = \tfrac12\left(\tfrac13 mL^2\right)\omega^2 + \tfrac12 m\,v_{G,AB}^2 + \tfrac12\left(\tfrac1{12}mL^2\right)\omega^2 + \tfrac12 M v_B^2$$

At $\theta = 53.13°$ this evaluates to $T_2 = 70.67\,\omega^2$ J (with $\omega$ in rad/s).

Step 4 · Solve $$70.67\,\omega^2 = 1044.6 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \omega = 3.845\ \text{rad/s}$$ $$v_B = 2L\sin\theta\,\omega = 2(2)(0.8)(3.845) = 12.30\ \text{m/s}$$

B moves upward, toward O. A direct simulation of the equation of motion from $\theta = 0$ gives the same speed.

Answer

$v_B = 12.3$ m/s, directed upward, when $\theta = 53.13°$.
