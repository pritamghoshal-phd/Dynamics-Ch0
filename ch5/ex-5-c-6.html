Example 5.C.6Block tipping over a step

impactangular momentumwork–energy

Problem

A square block of side $a$ and mass $m$ slides to the left on a smooth floor with speed $v$. Its leading bottom corner strikes a small step at O and sticks there, so the block begins to rotate about O.

Find the speed $v$ for which the block just reaches the balanced position on its corner, with G directly above O and zero velocity.

Solution

Every solution follows the four-step method. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagrams for the two phases

During the impact, a large impulsive force acts at the corner O. The weight and the floor reaction are ordinary forces, so their impulses are negligible. After the impact, the block rotates about O under its weight $mg$ and the reaction at O. The floor no longer touches the block.

During the impact (left), only the large impulsive force at the corner O matters, drawn as its components $\hat O_x$ and $\hat O_y$. The weight and the floor reaction give negligible impulses. After the impact (right), the block turns counterclockwise about O under its weight and the reaction at O. The floor no longer touches it.

Step 2 · Write the kinetics equations

For the impact we use angular momentum about O. The impulsive force passes through O, so $H_O$ is conserved. Before the impact the block translates, and G is a height $a/2$ above O while moving to the left with speed $v$. Its angular momentum is therefore $H_{O1} = m v\,(a/2)$, directed counterclockwise. After the impact, $H_{O2} = I_O\,\omega_2$.

$$m v\,\tfrac a2 = I_O\,\omega_2, \qquad I_O = \tfrac16 ma^2 + m\left(\tfrac{a}{\sqrt2}\right)^2 = \tfrac23 ma^2$$

For the rotation after the impact we use the work–energy principle. Only gravity does work, because O does not move. If the block just reaches the top with zero speed, then

$$\tfrac12 I_O\,\omega_2^2 = m g\,\Delta h_G$$
Step 3 · Write the kinematics equation

G is a distance $a/\sqrt2$ from O. It starts at a height $a/2$ above O and ends at a height $a/\sqrt2$ above O, so it rises by

$$\Delta h_G = \frac{a}{\sqrt2} - \frac a2 = \frac{(\sqrt2 - 1)\,a}{2}$$
Step 4 · Solve

The impact equation gives $\omega_2 = \dfrac{3v}{4a}$. Substituting this into the energy equation gives

$$\tfrac12\cdot\tfrac23 ma^2\cdot\frac{9v^2}{16a^2} = \tfrac{3}{16}mv^2 = mg\,\frac{(\sqrt2 - 1)a}{2}$$ $$v = \sqrt{\tfrac83(\sqrt2 - 1)\,g a} \approx 1.05\sqrt{ga}$$

The impact always removes $1 - \tfrac{3/16}{1/2} = 62.5\%$ of the kinetic energy, whatever the speed.

Answer

$v = \sqrt{\tfrac83(\sqrt2-1)\,ga} \approx 1.05\sqrt{ga}$. For $a = 0.5$ m this is about 2.33 m/s.
