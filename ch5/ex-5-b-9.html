Example 5.B.9Bar on a vertical and a circular guide

work–energyinstant centerconstrained motion

Problem

A thin, homogeneous bar AB has mass $m$ and length $L = 2r$. End A slides along a smooth vertical guide, and end B slides along a smooth circular guide of radius $r$. The circular guide is a quarter circle whose center lies a distance $r$ above its lowest point and a distance $2r$ to the left of the vertical guide. In position 1, B is at the upper end of the circular guide, level with the center of the arc, and the bar is released from rest. In position 2 the bar is horizontal, with B at the lowest point of the arc.

Find the speed of end A when the bar reaches position 2.

Solution

Every solution follows the four-step method. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagram

The bar is acted on by its weight $mg$ at its midpoint G and by the normal forces from the two guides at A and B. Both guides are smooth, so each normal force is perpendicular to the path of its point and does no work. Only gravity does work.

The vertical guide pushes on A horizontally, and the circular guide pushes on B along the radius of the arc. Each normal force is perpendicular to the path of its point, so neither does work, and only the weight $mg$ does work.

Step 2 · Write the kinetics equation (work–energy)

Mechanical energy is conserved, $T_1 + V_1 = T_2 + V_2$, with $T_1 = 0$. Put the origin at the lowest point of the arc. In position 1, B is at $(r, r)$ and A is on the vertical guide at $x = 2r$. The length condition $r^2 + (y_A - r)^2 = (2r)^2$ gives $y_A = (1 + \sqrt3)\,r$. The midpoint G is therefore at the height

$$y_{G1} = \frac{r + (1+\sqrt3)r}{2} = \left(1 + \frac{\sqrt3}{2}\right) r$$

In position 2 the bar lies along the floor level, so $y_{G2} = 0$, and G falls $h = \left(1 + \tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\right) r$.

Step 3 · Write the kinematics equations

In position 2 the velocity of B is tangent to the arc at its lowest point, which is horizontal, and the velocity of A is vertical. The instant center of the bar lies where the lines perpendicular to these velocities meet. The vertical line through B and the horizontal line through A meet at B itself. The instant center is therefore B, which means $v_B = 0$ in position 2, and the bar rotates about B with

$$v_A = 2r\,\omega, \qquad T_2 = \tfrac12 I_B\,\omega^2 = \tfrac12\left(\tfrac13 m(2r)^2\right)\omega^2 = \tfrac23 m r^2\omega^2$$
Step 4 · Solve $$\tfrac23 m r^2\omega^2 = m g\left(1 + \frac{\sqrt3}{2}\right) r \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \omega^2 = \frac{3g}{2r}\left(1 + \frac{\sqrt3}{2}\right)$$ $$v_A = 2r\omega = \sqrt{(6 + 3\sqrt3)\,g r} \approx 3.35\sqrt{g r}$$

A direct simulation with $r = 1$ m gives $v_A = 10.48$ m/s, which matches $\sqrt{(6 + 3\sqrt3)(9.81)(1)} = 10.48$ m/s.

Answer

$v_A = \sqrt{(6 + 3\sqrt3)\,g r} \approx 3.35\sqrt{g r}$, directed downward.
