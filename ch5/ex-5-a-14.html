Example 5.A.14Bar in two slots pushed by a force

Newton–Eulergeneral plane motionreactions

Problem

A thin, homogeneous bar AB of weight $W = 60$ lb and length $L = 4$ ft is guided by two smooth slots. End A moves in a horizontal slot and end B moves in a vertical slot, with A above and to the right of B. A horizontal force of 30 lb pushes end A to the right. The bar is released from rest when it makes an angle $\theta = 30°$ with the vertical slot.

Find (a) the angular acceleration of the bar at release and (b) the reaction forces on the bar at A and B at release.

The bar swings back and forth about the equilibrium angle, where $\tan\theta = 2P/W$, because the slots are smooth and nothing removes energy. The arrows show the forces on the bar to scale. The slot at A pushes up or down, and the slot at B pushes left or right.

Solution

Every solution follows the four-step method. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagram

The slots are smooth, so the slot at A can only push vertically ($N_A$, drawn positive upward) and the slot at B only horizontally ($N_B$, drawn positive to the right). The solution finds $N_B < 0$, so the slot at B actually pushes to the left.

The bar is acted on by its weight $W$ at G, the applied force $P = 30$ lb to the right at A, a vertical reaction $N_A$ from the horizontal slot at A, and a horizontal reaction $N_B$ from the vertical slot at B. We take $N_A$ positive upward and $N_B$ positive to the right. The angle $\theta$ is measured from the vertical slot, and the bar turns clockwise when $\theta$ increases.

Step 2 · Write the kinetics equations

With $m = W/g = 60/32.2 = 1.863$ slug and moments taken about G (clockwise positive),

$$P + N_B = m\,\ddot x_G, \qquad N_A - W = m\,\ddot y_G$$ $$P\,\frac L2\cos\theta - N_A\,\frac L2\sin\theta - N_B\,\frac L2\cos\theta = \frac{1}{12}mL^2\,\ddot\theta$$
Step 3 · Write the kinematics equations

Measuring from the corner of the slots, with $y$ upward, G is at $x_G = \tfrac L2\sin\theta$ and $y_G = -\tfrac L2\cos\theta$. At release $\dot\theta = 0$, so

$$\ddot x_G = \frac L2\cos\theta\,\ddot\theta, \qquad \ddot y_G = \frac L2\sin\theta\,\ddot\theta$$
Step 4 · Solve

Substituting the reactions from Newton's equations into Euler's equation gives

$$\ddot\theta = \frac{P L\cos\theta - W\frac L2\sin\theta}{\frac13 mL^2} = \frac{30(4)(0.866) - 60(2)(0.5)}{\frac13(1.863)(16)} = \frac{43.92}{9.938} = 4.42\ \text{rad/s}^2$$

The reactions then follow from Newton's equations.

$$N_B = m\,\frac L2\cos\theta\,\ddot\theta - P = 1.863(2)(0.866)(4.42) - 30 = -15.7\ \text{lb}$$ $$N_A = W + m\,\frac L2\sin\theta\,\ddot\theta = 60 + 1.863(2)(0.5)(4.42) = 68.2\ \text{lb}$$

The negative sign of $N_B$ means that the vertical slot pushes end B to the left.

Answer

(a) $\alpha = 4.42$ rad/s² clockwise, that is $\boldsymbol\alpha = -4.42\,\hat{\mathbf k}$ rad/s². (b) At A the slot pushes upward with 68.2 lb. At B the slot pushes to the left with 15.7 lb.
