Example 5.A.8A semicircular ring released from rest

Newton–Eulerfixed-axis rotationpin reaction

Problem

A thin semicircular ring of mass $m$ and radius $R$ is pinned to the ground at its end O. It is released from rest with its diameter horizontal and the ring hanging below the diameter.

Find (a) the angular acceleration of the ring at the instant of release and (b) the force that the pin exerts on the ring at O at that instant.

InteractiveThe ring swinging about its pin

The ring has radius 0.5 m. Its mass center G is not on the ring itself. G lies on the axis of symmetry at a distance $2R/\pi \approx 0.64R$ from the center of curvature C, in the open space inside the arc (purple dashed line). The mass center is an average position of all the mass, and for a curved body that average can fall where there is no material, just as it does for a ring or a boomerang. The red arrow is the pin force on the ring and the orange arrow is the weight, drawn to the same scale. The dashed lines on the plot mark the values at release, $mg/2$ and $mg/\pi$.

Solution

Every solution follows the four-step method. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagram

The ring is acted on by its weight $mg$ at its mass center G and by the pin force at O, with components $O_x$ and $O_y$. The mass center of a thin semicircular arc lies on its axis of symmetry, a distance $2R/\pi$ from the center of curvature C. At release, C is a distance $R$ to the left of O, so G is at $\mathbf r_{G/O} = -R\,\hat{\mathbf i} - \tfrac{2R}{\pi}\,\hat{\mathbf j}$.

$O_x$ and $O_y$ are drawn in the positive directions. The mass center G of the arc is a distance $2R/\pi$ below C, in the open space inside the arc.

Step 2 · Write the kinetics equations

The ring rotates about the fixed pin O, so we take moments about O.

$$\sum M_O = I_O\,\alpha, \qquad \sum\mathbf F = m\,\mathbf a_G$$

Every element of the ring is a distance $R$ from C, so $I_C = mR^2$. By the parallel-axis theorem applied twice, through G,

$$I_O = I_C - m\left(\tfrac{2R}{\pi}\right)^2 + m\left(R^2 + \tfrac{4R^2}{\pi^2}\right) = 2mR^2$$

The weight acts a horizontal distance $R$ from O, so $\sum M_O = mgR$, counterclockwise.

Step 3 · Write the kinematics equation

At release $\omega = 0$, so the acceleration of G has only a tangential part.

$$\mathbf a_G = \boldsymbol\alpha\times\mathbf r_{G/O} = \alpha\hat{\mathbf k}\times\left(-R\,\hat{\mathbf i} - \tfrac{2R}{\pi}\hat{\mathbf j}\right) = \alpha\left(\tfrac{2R}{\pi}\,\hat{\mathbf i} - R\,\hat{\mathbf j}\right)$$
Step 4 · Solve

The moment equation gives

$$\alpha = \frac{mgR}{2mR^2} = \frac{g}{2R}\quad\text{(counterclockwise)}$$

so $\mathbf a_G = \tfrac{g}{\pi}\,\hat{\mathbf i} - \tfrac g2\,\hat{\mathbf j}$. Newton's equation then gives the pin force.

$$O_x = m\,\frac{g}{\pi}, \qquad O_y - mg = -m\,\frac g2 \;\Longrightarrow\; O_y = \frac{mg}{2}$$

The magnitude is $mg\sqrt{1/\pi^2 + 1/4} \approx 0.593\,mg$. The pin carries much less than the weight at the instant of release, because the ring has started to fall.

Answer

(a) $\boldsymbol\alpha = \dfrac{g}{2R}\,\hat{\mathbf k}$, counterclockwise in the figure.

(b) $\mathbf O = \dfrac{mg}{\pi}\,\hat{\mathbf i} + \dfrac{mg}{2}\,\hat{\mathbf j}$, with magnitude about $0.593\,mg$.
