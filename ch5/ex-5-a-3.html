Example 5.A.3Rear-wheel and front-wheel braking on a bicycle

Newton–Eulerfrictionload transfer

Problem

A cyclist rides to the right and brakes. The mass center G of the bicycle and rider is a height $d$ above the road, a distance $b_R$ in front of the rear contact point and a distance $b_F$ behind the front contact point. In one situation she brakes with only the rear wheel, and in another she brakes with only the front wheel.

Discuss the differences between rear-wheel and front-wheel braking from the point of view of dynamics.

Solution

Every solution follows the four-step method. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagram

The bicycle and rider are acted on by the weight $mg$ at G and by normal forces $N_R$ and $N_F$ at the two contact points. Only the braked wheel carries a friction force $F$, which points backward. The other wheel rolls freely, and because its mass is small it carries no friction force.

The bicycle and rider form one body. Only the braked wheel carries a friction force $F$. The free wheel carries none, because its mass is small.

Step 2 · Write the kinetics equations

With deceleration $a$, Newton's equation and the moment equation about G are the same as for the braking car.

$$F = m\,a, \qquad N_R + N_F = mg, \qquad N_F\,b_F - N_R\,b_R - F\,d = 0$$

so that

$$N_F = \frac{m\,(g\,b_R + a\,d)}{L}, \qquad N_R = \frac{m\,(g\,b_F - a\,d)}{L}, \qquad L = b_R + b_F$$
Step 3 · Write the kinematics and friction conditions

The bicycle translates, so $\alpha = 0$. The braked wheel can supply at most $F = \mu N$, where $N$ is the normal force at that wheel. The rear wheel also stays on the road only while $N_R \ge 0$.

Step 4 · Solve

Rear brake only. The largest friction force is $\mu N_R$, and $N_R$ decreases as the deceleration increases. Setting $m a = \mu N_R$ gives

$$a_{max,rear} = \frac{\mu\,g\,b_F}{L + \mu\,d}$$

If the rider squeezes harder, the rear wheel locks and skids. The bicycle stays stable, but the deceleration is small.

Front brake only. The largest friction force is $\mu N_F$, and $N_F$ increases with the deceleration. Setting $m a = \mu N_F$ gives

$$a_{skid,front} = \frac{\mu\,g\,b_R}{L - \mu\,d}$$

There is a second limit. The rear wheel lifts when $N_R = 0$, which happens at

$$a_{lift} = \frac{g\,b_F}{d}$$

The front brake can therefore stop the bicycle much faster than the rear brake, up to the smaller of $a_{skid,front}$ and $a_{lift}$. On a dry road $a_{lift}$ is usually the smaller value, so hard front braking can pitch the rider over the handlebars.

With the values in the animation ($\mu = 0.7$, $d = 1.0$ m), the rear brake gives at most 1.62 m/s². The front brake could give 13.7 m/s² before skidding, but the rear wheel lifts at 3.92 m/s².

Answer

Braking moves load from the rear wheel to the front wheel. With the rear brake only, the braking wheel loses load as the deceleration grows, so the largest deceleration is small, $a = \mu g b_F/(L + \mu d)$, and the rear wheel simply skids. With the front brake only, the braking wheel gains load, so much larger decelerations are possible, but the rear wheel lifts when $a = g b_F/d$ and the rider can pitch forward over the front wheel.
