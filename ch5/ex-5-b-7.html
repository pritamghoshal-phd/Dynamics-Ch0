Example 5.B.7Link carrying a disk on a pin

work–energyfixed-axis rotationtranslation

Problem

Link OC is pinned to a wall at O and carries a circular disk on a smooth pin at C. The link has length $L = 1.2$ m, mass 10 kg and radius of gyration $k_O = 0.5$ m about O, and its mass center G is $0.3L$ from O. The disk has mass 8 kg and radius $r = 0.4$ m. The system is released from rest with OC horizontal.

Find the angular speed of OC when OC has swung down to the vertical position.

Solution

Every solution follows the four-step method. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagrams

For the whole system, the external forces are the weights of the link and the disk and the pin reaction at O. The pin O does not move, so only gravity does work.

For the disk alone, the forces are its weight, which acts at its center C, and the pin force at C. Both pass through C, so $\sum M_C = 0$. The disk starts at rest, so $\omega_{disk} = 0$ for all time, and the disk translates.

For the system, the pin reaction ($O_x$, $O_y$) does no work, so only the two weights do work. For the disk alone, the weight and the pin force at C both pass through C, so $\sum M_C = 0$ and the disk does not rotate.

Step 2 · Write the kinetics equation (work–energy)

Mechanical energy is conserved, $T_1 + V_1 = T_2 + V_2$, with $T_1 = 0$. The link rotates about the fixed point O and the disk only translates, so

$$T_2 = \tfrac12\,m_L k_O^2\,\omega^2 + \tfrac12\,m_D\,v_C^2$$

Measured from the starting level, G falls $0.3L = 0.36$ m and C falls $L = 1.2$ m.

$$V_1 - V_2 = m_L g(0.36) + m_D g(1.2) = 10(9.81)(0.36) + 8(9.81)(1.2) = 129.5\ \text{J}$$
Step 3 · Write the kinematics equation

Point C moves on a circle of radius $L$ about O, so $v_C = L\omega = 1.2\,\omega$.

$$T_2 = \tfrac12(10)(0.5)^2\omega^2 + \tfrac12(8)(1.2\,\omega)^2 = (1.25 + 5.76)\,\omega^2 = 7.01\,\omega^2$$
Step 4 · Solve $$7.01\,\omega^2 = 129.5 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \omega = 4.30\ \text{rad/s}$$

If the disk were welded to the link, it would also rotate with $\omega$ and add $\tfrac12\left(\tfrac12 m_D r^2\right)\omega^2 = 0.32\,\omega^2$ to $T_2$. The angular speed would then be 4.20 rad/s, slightly smaller.

Answer

$\omega_{OC} = 4.30$ rad/s when OC is vertical.
