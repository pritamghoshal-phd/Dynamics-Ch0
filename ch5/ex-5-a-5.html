Example 5.A.5A platform raised by two parallel links

Newton–Eulercurvilinear translationtwo-force member

Problem

A uniform platform AB of mass $m = 400$ kg is supported by two parallel links OA and BC, each of length $L = 2$ m, whose masses are small compared with that of the platform. A constant moment $M = 4000$ N·m is applied to link OA. At the position where $\theta = 53.13°$, both links are rotating clockwise at $\omega = 3$ rad/s.

Find the magnitude of the force carried by pin A at this position.

Solution

Every solution follows the four-step method. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagrams

Link BC is light and is pinned at both ends with no moment applied to it, so it is a two-force member. The force it exerts on the platform acts along BC.

Link OA is light, so the moments acting on it must add to zero. These are the applied moment $M$ and the moment of the force that the platform exerts on it at A. The pin reaction at O passes through O.

The platform is acted on by its weight $mg$ at G, by the force $\mathbf F_A = A_x\hat{\mathbf i} + A_y\hat{\mathbf j}$ from pin A, and by the force $F_B$ along BC from pin B.

By Newton's third law the platform pushes on link OA at A with $-A_x$ and $-A_y$. Link BC carries only the force $F_B$ along its length, which is a tension here.

Step 2 · Write the kinetics equations

For the platform, Newton's equation and the moment equation about G are

$$m\,\mathbf a_G = \mathbf F_A + F_B\,\hat{\mathbf e}_{BC} - mg\,\hat{\mathbf j}, \qquad \sum M_G = 0$$

The moment equation is zero because the platform translates. For link OA, with $\mathbf r_{A/O}$ the vector from O to A,

$$-M\,\hat{\mathbf k} + \mathbf r_{A/O}\times(-\mathbf F_A) = \mathbf 0$$
Step 3 · Write the kinematics equations

OABC is a parallelogram, so the platform translates along a curved path. Every point of the platform has the same acceleration as point A, which moves on a circle of radius $L$ about O.

$$\mathbf a_G = \mathbf a_A = \boldsymbol\alpha\times\mathbf r_{A/O} - \omega^2\,\mathbf r_{A/O}, \qquad \mathbf r_{A/O} = L(-\cos\theta\,\hat{\mathbf i} + \sin\theta\,\hat{\mathbf j})$$

The angular acceleration of the links is the fourth unknown. An energy argument confirms it quickly. The power of $M$ equals the rate of change of $T + V$, so

$$M\omega = m L^2\omega\,\dot\omega + m g L\cos\theta\,\omega \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \dot\omega = \frac{M}{mL^2} - \frac{g}{L}\cos\theta = 2.500 - 2.943 = -0.443\ \text{rad/s}^2$$

The clockwise rotation is therefore slowing down at this instant.

Step 4 · Solve

There are four scalar unknowns, $A_x$, $A_y$, $F_B$ and $\alpha$, and four equations. With $\theta = 53.13°$ ($\cos\theta = 0.6$, $\sin\theta = 0.8$) and $\omega = 3$ rad/s, the acceleration of the platform is

$$\mathbf a_G = (10.09\,\hat{\mathbf i} - 14.93\,\hat{\mathbf j})\ \text{m/s}^2$$

Solving the equations gives

$$A_x = 3268\ \text{N}, \qquad A_y = -1024\ \text{N}, \qquad F_B = 1280\ \text{N (tension in BC)}$$ $$|\mathbf F_A| = \sqrt{3268^2 + 1024^2} = 3425\ \text{N}$$

This result does not depend on the length of the platform, provided its mass center lies on the line through the two pins. The force at A is smaller than the weight, 3924 N, because the platform is accelerating strongly toward the pins at this instant.

Answer

The pin at A carries a force of magnitude $|\mathbf F_A| \approx 3.43$ kN. On the platform it acts with components $A_x = 3268$ N to the right and $A_y = 1024$ N downward.
