Example 5.B.3Bar restrained by a spring

work–energyspringsfixed-axis rotation

Problem

A thin, homogeneous bar OA of length 4 ft and weight $W = 15$ lb is pinned at its lower end O. A spring of stiffness $k = 3$ lb/ft connects end A to a fixed pin B located 6 ft directly above O. The angle $\theta$ of the bar is measured from the vertical, and the spring is unstretched when $\theta = 0$.

Find the angular speed of the bar at $\theta = 0$ if the bar just reaches $\theta = 90°$ before coming to rest.

Solution

Every solution follows the four-step method. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagram

The bar is acted on by its weight $W$ at its midpoint G, the spring force at A, and the pin reaction at O. The pin O does not move, so the pin reaction does no work. The weight and the spring force are conservative, so we account for them with potential energies.

The spring force $F_s = k\delta$ acts along the spring, from A toward the fixed point B. The pin reaction ($O_x$, $O_y$) does no work because O does not move. The weight $W$ and $F_s$ are conservative and enter through $V_g$ and $V_s$.

Step 2 · Write the kinetics equation (work–energy)

Because no nonconservative force does work, $T_1 + V_1 = T_2 + V_2$, where state 1 is $\theta = 0$ and state 2 is $\theta = 90°$. The bar rotates about the fixed point O, so

$$T = \tfrac12 I_O\,\omega^2, \qquad I_O = \tfrac13 \frac{W}{g} L^2 = \tfrac13\left(\frac{15}{32.2}\right)(4)^2 = 2.484\ \text{slug·ft}^2$$

In state 2 the bar is at rest, so $T_2 = 0$.

Step 3 · Work out the geometry (spring length and height of G)

Place O at the origin with B 6 ft above it. End A is at $(4\sin\theta,\ 4\cos\theta)$, so the spring length is

$$\ell(\theta) = \sqrt{(4\sin\theta)^2 + (6 - 4\cos\theta)^2} = \sqrt{52 - 48\cos\theta}\ \text{ft}$$

At $\theta = 0$ the length is 2 ft, which is the unstretched length. At $\theta = 90°$ the length is $\sqrt{52} = 7.211$ ft, so the stretch is $\delta_2 = 5.211$ ft. Measuring heights from O, G is 2 ft above O at $\theta = 0$ and level with O at $\theta = 90°$.

$$V_1 = W(2) + 0 = 30\ \text{ft·lb}, \qquad V_2 = 0 + \tfrac12(3)(5.211)^2 = 40.73\ \text{ft·lb}$$
Step 4 · Solve $$\tfrac12 I_O\,\omega_1^2 + 30 = 0 + 40.73 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \omega_1 = \sqrt{\frac{2(10.73)}{2.484}} = 2.94\ \text{rad/s}$$

The gravitational energy released (30 ft·lb) is not enough to stretch the spring by 5.211 ft, so the bar must start with 10.73 ft·lb of kinetic energy. A direct simulation of the equation of motion with $\omega_1 = 2.94$ rad/s stops at $\theta = 90.0°$.

Answer

$\omega_1 = 2.94$ rad/s at $\theta = 0$.
