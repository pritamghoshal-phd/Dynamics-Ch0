Example 5.A.10Bar with a disk on a smooth bearing

Newton–Eulerfixed-axis rotationsystems of bodies

Problem

A non-uniform bar OC has length $L = 2$ m and mass $m = 10$ kg. Its mass center G lies $0.3L$ from O, and its centroidal radius of gyration is $k_G = 0.4$ m. The bar is pinned to the ground at O. A homogeneous disk of radius $r = 0.6$ m and mass $M = 15$ kg is pinned to the other end of the bar at its center C. All bearings are smooth, and the system is released from rest with OC horizontal.

Find the angular acceleration of arm OC at the instant of release.

InteractiveThe bar and the disk after release
Bearing at C:

Compare the two cases. With a smooth bearing, nothing exerts a moment on the disk about C, so the disk never starts to rotate. It only translates, and its moment of inertia about C plays no role. If the disk were welded to the bar, it would have to rotate with the bar, and the bar would accelerate more slowly.

Solution

Every solution follows the four-step method. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagrams

The forces $C_x$ and $C_y$ between the bar and the disk act at the smooth bearing C. They are equal and opposite on the two bodies, and on the disk they pass through its center C, so they have no moment about C.

For the whole system the forces at C are internal and cancel. Only the pin reactions at O and the two weights remain, and the pin reactions have no moment about O.

The bar OC is acted on by its weight $mg$ at G, the pin reaction at O, and the force that the disk exerts on it at C. The disk is acted on by its weight $Mg$ at C and by the force from the bar at C. Both forces on the disk pass through C, so they have no moment about C.

Step 2 · Write the kinetics equations

For the disk, Euler's equation about its mass center C gives

$$I_C\,\alpha_{disk} = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \alpha_{disk} = 0$$

The disk starts from rest and is never given an angular acceleration, so it translates. It therefore behaves like a particle of mass $M$ located at C.

For the system consisting of the bar and the disk, we take moments about the fixed pin O, where the unknown pin reaction has no moment. The bar contributes $I_{O,bar}\,\alpha$ and the translating disk contributes the moment of its momentum rate, $M L\,a_C$.

$$m g\,(0.3L) + M g\,L = I_{O,bar}\,\alpha + M L\,a_C$$
Step 3 · Write the kinematics equations

Point C moves on a circle of radius $L$ about O. At release $\omega = 0$, so $a_C = L\,\alpha$. The parallel-axis theorem gives the moment of inertia of the bar about O.

$$I_{O,bar} = m\left(k_G^2 + (0.3L)^2\right) = 10\,(0.16 + 0.36) = 5.2\ \text{kg·m}^2$$
Step 4 · Solve $$\alpha = \frac{m g\,(0.3L) + M g\,L}{I_{O,bar} + M L^2} = \frac{10(9.81)(0.6) + 15(9.81)(2)}{5.2 + 15(2)^2} = \frac{353.2}{65.2} = 5.42\ \text{rad/s}^2$$

The bar turns clockwise, so $\boldsymbol\alpha = -5.42\,\hat{\mathbf k}$ rad/s².

If the disk were welded to the bar, its centroidal moment of inertia $\tfrac12 M r^2 = 2.7$ kg·m² would be added to the denominator, and $\alpha$ would drop to 5.20 rad/s².

Answer

$\alpha = 5.42$ rad/s² clockwise, that is $\boldsymbol\alpha = -5.42\,\hat{\mathbf k}$ rad/s².
