Example 1.D.2A jet that appears to fly sideways

relative velocityvector triangle

Problem

Jet B travels due north with a speed of $v_B = 600$ km/h. Passengers on jet B observe jet A to be flying sideways and moving due east. The velocity of A makes an angle of 30° with the east direction, as shown in the figure.

Find (a) the speed of A and (b) the speed of A as observed by the passengers on jet B.

Solution

Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Write the relative-velocity equation

The passengers on B translate with B, so

$$\mathbf v_A = \mathbf v_B + \mathbf v_{A/B}$$

Take $\hat{\mathbf i}$ east and $\hat{\mathbf j}$ north. We know $\mathbf v_B = 600\,\hat{\mathbf j}$ km/h. The relative velocity points due east with an unknown magnitude, $\mathbf v_{A/B} = v_{A/B}\,\hat{\mathbf i}$. The velocity of A has a known direction and an unknown magnitude, $\mathbf v_A = v_A\left(\cos 30°\,\hat{\mathbf i} + \sin 30°\,\hat{\mathbf j}\right)$. There are two unknowns and two scalar equations.

Step 2 · Draw the vector triangle

$\mathbf v_A = \mathbf v_B + \mathbf v_{A/B}$. The vector $\mathbf v_B$ points north, $\mathbf v_{A/B}$ points east, and $\mathbf v_A$ closes the triangle at 30° north of east.

Step 3 · Match the north components

The relative velocity has no north component, so the north part of $\mathbf v_A$ must equal $v_B$.

$$v_A \sin 30° = 600 \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad v_A = \frac{600}{0.5} = 1200\ \text{km/h}$$
Step 4 · Match the east components

Jet B has no east component, so the east part of $\mathbf v_A$ is the whole relative velocity.

$$v_{A/B} = v_A \cos 30° = 1200(0.8660) = 1039\ \text{km/h}$$
Answer

(a) $v_A = 1200$ km/h. (b) The passengers on B see A move due east at $v_{A/B} = 1039$ km/h.
