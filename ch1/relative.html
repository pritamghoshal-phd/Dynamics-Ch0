DRelative and constrained motion

Relative motion

Let $\mathbf r_A$ and $\mathbf r_B$ locate two particles from a fixed origin. The position of A relative to B is the vector from B to A:

$$\mathbf r_{A/B} = \mathbf r_A - \mathbf r_B \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \mathbf v_{A/B} = \mathbf v_A - \mathbf v_B, \qquad \mathbf a_{A/B} = \mathbf a_A - \mathbf a_B$$

$\mathbf v_{A/B}$ is what an observer riding on B sees, as long as that observer does not rotate (a translating observer). Rotating observers need the tools of Chapter 3. Rearranged, $\mathbf v_A = \mathbf v_B + \mathbf v_{A/B}$ is a vector triangle, which is usually the fastest way to solve these problems.

Constrained motion

Bodies connected by inextensible cables cannot move independently. The method is always the same:

  1. Choose coordinates that measure each body's position from a fixed point, with a clear positive direction.
  2. Write the cable length as a sum of the segment lengths, in terms of those coordinates. Pieces wrapped around pulleys of fixed size add only constants.
  3. Set the length constant and differentiate: $\dot L = 0$ relates the velocities, $\ddot L = 0$ the accelerations.

If several cables are present, each one gives its own constraint equation.

ConceptMore supporting strands, slower load
Load held by:

The load B hangs from n strands, and the free end is pulled down at speed u. Every metre of cable pulled out must come from shortening all n strands equally, so B rises n times slower. This is the trade-off of a block and tackle: less force, more rope.

🌧️
Relative motion in the wild

Rain falling straight down looks slanted from a moving car: $\mathbf v_{\mathrm{rain/car}} = \mathbf v_{\mathrm{rain}} - \mathbf v_{\mathrm{car}}$ adds a backward component equal to the car’s speed. The faster you drive, the more the rain seems to come at you from the front.
