Example 1.D.6Block lifted on a vertical guide by one cable

constrained motionnonlinear constraintchain rule

Problem

Blocks B and C are connected by a single inextensible cable that is wrapped around small pulleys at D and E. Between D and E, the cable passes around a small pulley attached to block A, which slides in a vertical guide midway between D and E. Pulleys D and E are 3 ft on either side of the guide. Blocks B and C move downward with speeds of $v_B = 6$ ft/s and $v_C = 18$ ft/s.

Find the velocity of block A when $s_A = 4$ ft, where $s_A$ is the distance of A below the level of D and E.

Solution

Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Choose coordinates from the fixed pulleys

Measure $s_C$ and $s_B$ downward from the level of D and E to blocks C and B, and measure $s_A$ downward from the same level to the pulley on A. With these choices $\dot s_B = 6$ ft/s and $\dot s_C = 18$ ft/s.

Step 2 · Draw the geometry

Each inclined segment is the hypotenuse of a right triangle with legs of 3 ft and $s_A$. The vertical segments to C and B have lengths $s_C$ and $s_B$.

Step 3 · Write the length of the cable

The cable has two vertical segments and two inclined segments of equal length.

$$L = s_C + 2\sqrt{3^2 + s_A^2} + s_B$$

The radii of the pulleys are small, so the wrapped parts are neglected.

Step 4 · Differentiate with the chain rule $$0 = \dot s_C + \frac{2\,s_A\,\dot s_A}{\sqrt{9 + s_A^2}} + \dot s_B \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \dot s_A = -\frac{\sqrt{9 + s_A^2}}{2\,s_A}\left(\dot s_B + \dot s_C\right)$$

Unlike the earlier examples, the relation between the speeds depends on the position of A.

Step 5 · Solve at $s_A = 4$ ft $$\dot s_A = -\frac{\sqrt{9 + 16}}{2(4)}\,(6 + 18) = -\frac{5}{8}(24) = -15\ \text{ft/s}$$

The negative sign means that $s_A$ decreases, so block A moves upward.

Answer

$\mathbf v_A = 15$ ft/s upward when $s_A = 4$ ft.

Explore

  • Use the graph to explain why A moves faster and faster as it approaches the level of D and E.
  • Find $\ddot s_A$ at $s_A = 4$ ft by differentiating the constraint a second time. Remember that $\ddot s_B = \ddot s_C = 0$.
  • What happens to the speed of A as $s_A \to 0$? Is that physically possible?
