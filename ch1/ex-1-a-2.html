Example 1.A.2Velocity and acceleration from x(t) and y(t)

Cartesiandot productangle

Problem

A particle P moves on a path whose Cartesian components are given by the following functions of time, with $x$ and $y$ in inches and $t$ in seconds.

$$x(t) = t^3 + 10, \qquad y(t) = 2\cos 4t$$

Find at the time $t = 2$ s (a) the velocity vector of P, (b) the acceleration of P and (c) the angle between the velocity and acceleration vectors.

Solution

Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the kinematics diagram

The position is given directly in Cartesian components, so the velocity and acceleration follow by differentiating each component. The sketch shows P on its path at $t = 2$ s, with the velocity tangent to the path and the acceleration pointing toward the inside of the curve.

The angle φ between the velocity and the acceleration follows from their dot product. (The sketch compresses the x direction, so the angle is computed, not measured.)

Step 2 · Differentiate the components $$\dot x = 3t^2, \qquad \dot y = -8\sin 4t, \qquad \ddot x = 6t, \qquad \ddot y = -32\cos 4t$$

At $t = 2$ s the angle $4t = 8$ rad, so $\sin 8 = 0.9894$ and $\cos 8 = -0.1455$.

Step 3 · Evaluate the velocity and the acceleration $$\mathbf v = 3(2)^2\,\hat{\mathbf i} - 8(0.9894)\,\hat{\mathbf j} = 12\,\hat{\mathbf i} - 7.915\,\hat{\mathbf j}\ \text{in/s}$$ $$\mathbf a = 6(2)\,\hat{\mathbf i} - 32(-0.1455)\,\hat{\mathbf j} = 12\,\hat{\mathbf i} + 4.656\,\hat{\mathbf j}\ \text{in/s}^2$$
Step 4 · Find the angle from the dot product

The dot product of two vectors equals the product of their magnitudes and the cosine of the angle between them. The magnitudes are $|\mathbf v| = 14.38$ in/s and $|\mathbf a| = 12.87$ in/s², so

$$\cos\varphi = \frac{\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf a}{|\mathbf v|\,|\mathbf a|} = \frac{(12)(12) + (-7.915)(4.656)}{(14.38)(12.87)} = \frac{107.2}{185.0} = 0.5791$$ $$\varphi = 54.6^\circ$$

The angle is less than 90°, so the component of the acceleration along the velocity is positive and the particle is speeding up at this instant.

Answer

(a) $\mathbf v = 12\,\hat{\mathbf i} - 7.915\,\hat{\mathbf j}$ in/s. (b) $\mathbf a = 12\,\hat{\mathbf i} + 4.656\,\hat{\mathbf j}$ in/s². (c) The angle between them is $\varphi = 54.6^\circ$.
