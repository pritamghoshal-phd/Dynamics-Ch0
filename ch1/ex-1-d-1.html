Example 1.D.1What a passenger in car A sees car B doing

relative accelerationpath componentstranslating observer

Problem

At the instant shown, car B travels with a speed of 50 km/h and is slowing down at a rate of 10 km/h². Car A moves with a speed of 80 km/h, and this speed is increasing at a rate of 10 km/h². At this instant A and B travel in the same direction, which is the $-y$ direction in the figure. Car A drives on a straight road, and car B drives on a curve whose radius of curvature at B is $\rho = 500$ m, with the centre of curvature in the $-x$ direction from B.

Find the acceleration that a passenger in car A observes for car B.

Solution

Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Choose the observer and the relative-acceleration equation

The passenger rides in car A, which moves on a straight road. The observer therefore translates without rotating, and the relative-motion equations take their simplest form.

$$\mathbf a_B = \mathbf a_A + \mathbf a_{B/A} \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \mathbf a_{B/A} = \mathbf a_B - \mathbf a_A$$

We need the absolute accelerations of both cars, written in the same $xy$ components.

Step 2 · Draw the kinematics diagram

The normal part $v_B^2/\rho$ points toward the centre of B’s curve. Car B slows down, so its tangential part points opposite to its direction of travel. Car A speeds up, so $\mathbf a_A$ points along its direction of travel.

Step 3 · Find the acceleration of car B with path components

Car B moves on a curve, so its acceleration has a tangential part $\dot v_B\,\hat{\mathbf e}_t$ and a normal part $(v_B^2/\rho)\,\hat{\mathbf e}_n$. At this instant B moves in the $-y$ direction, so $\hat{\mathbf e}_t = -\hat{\mathbf j}$. The centre of curvature lies in the $-x$ direction, so $\hat{\mathbf e}_n = -\hat{\mathbf i}$. With $\rho = 0.5$ km,

$$\frac{v_B^2}{\rho} = \frac{(50\ \text{km/h})^2}{0.5\ \text{km}} = 5000\ \text{km/h}^2$$

Car B slows down, so $\dot v_B = -10$ km/h². Therefore

$$\mathbf a_B = \dot v_B\,\hat{\mathbf e}_t + \frac{v_B^2}{\rho}\,\hat{\mathbf e}_n = (-10)(-\hat{\mathbf j}) + 5000(-\hat{\mathbf i}) = -5000\,\hat{\mathbf i} + 10\,\hat{\mathbf j}\ \ \text{km/h}^2$$
Step 4 · Find the acceleration of car A

Car A travels on a straight line, so it has no normal acceleration. Its speed increases at 10 km/h² in the direction of travel, $-\hat{\mathbf j}$.

$$\mathbf a_A = \dot v_A\,\hat{\mathbf e}_t = 10(-\hat{\mathbf j}) = -10\,\hat{\mathbf j}\ \ \text{km/h}^2$$
Step 5 · Subtract $$\mathbf a_{B/A} = \mathbf a_B - \mathbf a_A = \left(-5000\,\hat{\mathbf i} + 10\,\hat{\mathbf j}\right) - \left(-10\,\hat{\mathbf j}\right) = -5000\,\hat{\mathbf i} + 20\,\hat{\mathbf j}\ \ \text{km/h}^2$$

One km/h² equals $1000/3600^2 = 7.716\times10^{-5}$ m/s², so

$$\mathbf a_{B/A} = -0.3858\,\hat{\mathbf i} + 0.00154\,\hat{\mathbf j}\ \ \text{m/s}^2, \qquad \left|\mathbf a_{B/A}\right| = 0.386\ \text{m/s}^2$$

The normal acceleration of B dominates. The speed changes of 10 km/h² are very small compared with $v_B^2/\rho$, so the passenger sees B accelerating almost exactly toward the centre of B’s curve.

Answer

$\mathbf a_{B/A} = -5000\,\hat{\mathbf i} + 20\,\hat{\mathbf j}$ km/h², which is $-0.386\,\hat{\mathbf i} + 0.0015\,\hat{\mathbf j}$ m/s². Its magnitude is 0.386 m/s², directed almost exactly in the $-x$ direction, toward the centre of B’s curve.
