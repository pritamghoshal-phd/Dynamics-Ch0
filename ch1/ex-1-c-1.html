Example 1.C.1Rocket under thrust and gravity

path componentsradius of curvatureprojection

Problem

A rocket is traveling at an altitude where the gravitational acceleration is $g = 26.5$ ft/s². The thrust force on the rocket produces an acceleration of $a_T = 29.3$ ft/s² along the axis of the rocket. At the position shown, the axis of the rocket makes an angle $\theta = 36.87^\circ$ with the vertical, and the speed of the rocket is $v = 2800$ ft/s. The rocket moves along its own axis.

Find (a) the rate of change of speed of the rocket at this instant and (b) the radius of curvature of the rocket's path at this instant.

Solution

Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Choose a description of the motion

Both questions concern the path itself. The rate of change of speed is the tangential component of the acceleration, $\dot v = a_t$, and the radius of curvature follows from the normal component, $a_n = v^2/\rho$. Path (normal–tangential) components are therefore the natural choice.

The rocket moves along its own axis, so the tangent unit vector $\hat{\mathbf e}_t$ points along the axis, at the angle $\theta$ from the vertical. The normal unit vector $\hat{\mathbf e}_n$ is perpendicular to $\hat{\mathbf e}_t$ and points toward the center of curvature. Gravity pulls the path downward, so $\hat{\mathbf e}_n$ points down and to the right in the figure.

Step 2 · Draw the kinematics diagram

Left, the rocket axis makes the angle θ with the vertical, and the velocity is along this axis. Right, the total acceleration is the sum of the thrust part and gravity, and it splits into a part along the path and a part across it.

Step 3 · Write the acceleration in Cartesian components

With $\hat{\mathbf i}$ to the right and $\hat{\mathbf j}$ upward, the unit vectors are

$$\hat{\mathbf e}_t = \sin\theta\,\hat{\mathbf i} + \cos\theta\,\hat{\mathbf j} = 0.6\,\hat{\mathbf i} + 0.8\,\hat{\mathbf j}, \qquad \hat{\mathbf e}_n = \cos\theta\,\hat{\mathbf i} - \sin\theta\,\hat{\mathbf j} = 0.8\,\hat{\mathbf i} - 0.6\,\hat{\mathbf j}$$

The total acceleration is the sum of the thrust acceleration and the acceleration of gravity.

$$\mathbf a = a_T\,\hat{\mathbf e}_t - g\,\hat{\mathbf j} = 29.3(0.6)\,\hat{\mathbf i} + \left[29.3(0.8) - 26.5\right]\hat{\mathbf j} = 17.58\,\hat{\mathbf i} - 3.06\,\hat{\mathbf j}\ \text{ft/s}^2$$
Step 4 · Project the acceleration onto the path directions

The tangential and normal components are the projections of $\mathbf a$ onto $\hat{\mathbf e}_t$ and $\hat{\mathbf e}_n$.

$$a_t = \mathbf a\cdot\hat{\mathbf e}_t = 17.58(0.6) + (-3.06)(0.8) = 8.10\ \text{ft/s}^2$$ $$a_n = \mathbf a\cdot\hat{\mathbf e}_n = 17.58(0.8) + (-3.06)(-0.6) = 15.90\ \text{ft/s}^2$$

The same results follow directly from the figure. The thrust lies entirely along the path, while gravity has a component $g\cos\theta$ opposite to $\hat{\mathbf e}_t$ and a component $g\sin\theta$ along $\hat{\mathbf e}_n$. Hence $a_t = a_T - g\cos\theta$ and $a_n = g\sin\theta$.

Step 5 · Find the rate of change of speed and the radius of curvature

The rate of change of speed is the tangential component of the acceleration.

$$\dot v = a_t = 8.10\ \text{ft/s}^2$$

It is positive, so the rocket is speeding up. The radius of curvature follows from $a_n = v^2/\rho$.

$$\rho = \frac{v^2}{a_n} = \frac{(2800)^2}{15.90} = 4.93\times 10^{5}\ \text{ft}$$

This is about 93 miles. The path is only gently curved because the rocket is very fast and only gravity bends the path.

Answer

(a) $\dot v = 8.10$ ft/s², so the rocket is speeding up. (b) $\rho = 4.93\times 10^{5}$ ft, which is about 93 miles.
