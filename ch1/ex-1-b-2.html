Example 1.B.2Particle on a saddle surface

3D Cartesiansurfacechain rule

Problem

A particle moves in three-dimensional space on a surface defined by $z = xy/2$, with

$$x = 6\sin 2\pi t, \qquad y = 6\cos 2\pi t$$

Find the Cartesian components of the velocity and the acceleration of the particle at $t = 1$.

The book does not give units. Lengths are in a consistent length unit and $t$ is in seconds.

Solution

Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the kinematics diagram and express z in time

The particle's $x$ and $y$ coordinates are given, and the surface gives $z$. Substituting,

$$z = \frac{xy}{2} = 18\sin 2\pi t\cos 2\pi t = 9\sin 4\pi t$$

The identity $2\sin u\cos u = \sin 2u$ was used in the last step.

At $t = 1$ s the particle is at $(0, 6, 0)$. Its velocity lies in the $xz$ plane and its acceleration points along $-\hat{\mathbf j}$, toward the $z$ axis.

Step 2 · Find the velocity $$\dot x = 12\pi\cos 2\pi t, \qquad \dot y = -12\pi\sin 2\pi t, \qquad \dot z = 36\pi\cos 4\pi t$$

At $t = 1$, $\cos 2\pi = \cos 4\pi = 1$ and $\sin 2\pi = 0$, so

$$\mathbf v = 12\pi\,\hat{\mathbf i} + 36\pi\,\hat{\mathbf k} = 37.70\,\hat{\mathbf i} + 113.1\,\hat{\mathbf k}$$
Step 3 · Find the acceleration $$\ddot x = -24\pi^2\sin 2\pi t, \qquad \ddot y = -24\pi^2\cos 2\pi t, \qquad \ddot z = -144\pi^2\sin 4\pi t$$

At $t = 1$,

$$\mathbf a = -24\pi^2\,\hat{\mathbf j} = -236.9\,\hat{\mathbf j}$$
Answer

$\mathbf v = 37.70\,\hat{\mathbf i} + 113.1\,\hat{\mathbf k}$ (length units per second) and $\mathbf a = -236.9\,\hat{\mathbf j}$ (length units per second squared).
