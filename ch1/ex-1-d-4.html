Example 1.D.4Block on an incline and a block on a movable pulley

constrained motionpulleysincline

Problem

Block A slides on an incline. It is tied to a cable that runs up the incline over a fixed pulley, down around a movable pulley that carries block B, and up to the ceiling. Block A moves with an acceleration of $\ddot x_A = a_A = 0.44$ m/s², where $x_A$ is measured along the incline and increases up the incline toward the fixed pulley.

Find the acceleration of block B.

Solution

Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Choose coordinates from fixed points

Measure $x_B$ downward from the ceiling to the centre of the pulley that carries B. Measure $x_A$ along the incline from a fixed point, positive up the incline. Both coordinates locate a moving point relative to a fixed one.

Step 2 · Draw the cable diagram

Two strands of length $x_B$ (plus constants) support the pulley of B. The segment from the fixed pulley down the incline to A has length $d - x_A$, where $d$ is a constant and $x_A$ increases up the incline.

Step 3 · Write the length of the cable

The two vertical strands that hold B each have length $x_B$ plus a constant. The segment from the fixed pulley to A has length $d - x_A$, where $d$ is a constant distance along the incline. The parts wrapped on the pulleys have constant length.

$$L = 2x_B + \left(d - x_A\right) + \text{constant}$$
Step 4 · Differentiate twice $$0 = 2\dot x_B - \dot x_A, \qquad 0 = 2\ddot x_B - \ddot x_A \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \ddot x_B = \tfrac12\,\ddot x_A$$
Step 5 · Solve $$a_B = \ddot x_B = \tfrac12(0.44) = 0.22\ \text{m/s}^2$$

The result is positive, and $x_B$ is measured downward, so B accelerates downward. This makes sense, because A moving toward the fixed pulley shortens the incline segment and releases cable to the strands that hold B.

Answer

$a_B = 0.22$ m/s², directed downward.

Explore

  • Does the angle of the incline appear in the answer? Explain why not.
  • If the cable wrapped around the pulley of B twice, giving four supporting strands, what would $a_B$ be?
  • Change $a_A$ with the slider. Is the ratio $a_B/a_A$ always ½?
