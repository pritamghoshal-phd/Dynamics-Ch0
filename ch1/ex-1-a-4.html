Example 1.A.4Motion on a spiral

polarCoriolis termspiral

Problem

Particle A travels on a path such that its radial position is given by $r = 5\theta$, where $r$ is in metres and $\theta$ is in radians. It is also known that $\dot\theta = 2$ rad/s, a constant.

Find (a) the velocity vector and (b) the acceleration vector of A when $\theta = \pi$.

Solution

Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Choose a description and draw the kinematics diagram

The path is given as $r(\theta)$ and the rate $\dot\theta$ is known, so the polar description is the natural choice.

$$\mathbf v = \dot r\,\hat{\mathbf e}_r + r\dot\theta\,\hat{\mathbf e}_\theta, \qquad \mathbf a = \left(\ddot r - r\dot\theta^2\right)\hat{\mathbf e}_r + \left(r\ddot\theta + 2\dot r\dot\theta\right)\hat{\mathbf e}_\theta$$

At $\theta = \pi$ the unit vector $\hat{\mathbf e}_r$ points in the $-\hat{\mathbf i}$ direction and $\hat{\mathbf e}_\theta$ points in the $-\hat{\mathbf j}$ direction.

Step 2 · Find r and its time derivatives

Differentiate $r = 5\theta$ with respect to time, using $\dot\theta = 2$ rad/s and $\ddot\theta = 0$.

$$r = 5\pi = 15.71\ \text{m}, \qquad \dot r = 5\dot\theta = 10\ \text{m/s}, \qquad \ddot r = 5\ddot\theta = 0$$
Step 3 · Evaluate the velocity $$\mathbf v = 10\,\hat{\mathbf e}_r + (5\pi)(2)\,\hat{\mathbf e}_\theta = 10\,\hat{\mathbf e}_r + 31.42\,\hat{\mathbf e}_\theta\ \text{m/s}$$
Step 4 · Evaluate the acceleration $$\mathbf a = \left(0 - 5\pi(2)^2\right)\hat{\mathbf e}_r + \left(0 + 2(10)(2)\right)\hat{\mathbf e}_\theta = -62.83\,\hat{\mathbf e}_r + 40\,\hat{\mathbf e}_\theta\ \text{m/s}^2$$

The transverse component is not zero even though $\dot\theta$ is constant. The term $2\dot r\dot\theta$ (the Coriolis term) appears because A moves outward while the line OA turns.

Step 5 · Express the vectors in Cartesian components

At $\theta = \pi$ the unit vectors are $\hat{\mathbf e}_r = -\hat{\mathbf i}$ and $\hat{\mathbf e}_\theta = -\hat{\mathbf j}$, so

$$\mathbf v = -10\,\hat{\mathbf i} - 31.42\,\hat{\mathbf j}\ \text{m/s}, \qquad \mathbf a = 62.83\,\hat{\mathbf i} - 40\,\hat{\mathbf j}\ \text{m/s}^2$$
Answer

(a) $\mathbf v = 10\,\hat{\mathbf e}_r + 31.42\,\hat{\mathbf e}_\theta$ m/s $= -10\,\hat{\mathbf i} - 31.42\,\hat{\mathbf j}$ m/s.

(b) $\mathbf a = -62.83\,\hat{\mathbf e}_r + 40\,\hat{\mathbf e}_\theta$ m/s² $= 62.83\,\hat{\mathbf i} - 40\,\hat{\mathbf j}$ m/s².
