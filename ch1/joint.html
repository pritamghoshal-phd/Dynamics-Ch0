CJoint description: using two descriptions at once

The key fact of this section is short:

Velocity is velocity, and acceleration is acceleration. The Cartesian, path and polar descriptions are three ways of writing the same two vectors. Only the components change.

That lets us mix descriptions. The information in a problem often arrives in one description (a radar measures $r$ and $\theta$; a slot guides $x$ and $y$), while the question is asked in another (how fast is the speed changing? what is the radius of curvature?).

How to move between descriptions

Two equivalent strategies:

  • Projection. A component along a unit vector is a dot product. For example, $\dot v = a_t = \mathbf a\cdot\hat{\mathbf e}_t$ and $a_n = \mathbf a\cdot\hat{\mathbf e}_n$, with $\hat{\mathbf e}_t = \mathbf v/v$.
  • Balancing coefficients. Write the unit vectors of one description in terms of the other (for instance $\hat{\mathbf e}_r = \cos\theta\,\hat{\mathbf i} + \sin\theta\,\hat{\mathbf j}$), set the two expressions for $\mathbf v$ (or $\mathbf a$) equal, and match the coefficients of each unit vector.

Two formulas that come straight from the path description are worth remembering:

$$\dot v = \frac{\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf a}{v}, \qquad \rho = \frac{v^2}{a_n} = \frac{v^3}{|\mathbf v\times\mathbf a|}$$

The first says the speed changes only through the part of $\mathbf a$ along $\mathbf v$. The second gives the radius of curvature from any description in which you can compute $\mathbf v$ and $\mathbf a$.

ConceptRadius of curvature along a projectile path

Gravity is the whole acceleration. Split it along the path: on the way up, a_t is negative (slowing down); at the top, all of g is normal and ρ = v²/g, the tightest point of the arc; on the way down, a_t is positive.

Kinematics in the wild

Air-traffic and speed-camera radars measure only distance and its rate of change (r and ṙ), plus a bearing angle. Turning those polar measurements into a speed or a turning radius is a joint-description problem, exactly like Example 1.C.4.
