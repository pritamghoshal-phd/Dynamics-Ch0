Example 1.C.4Radar tracking an airplane in a loop

path and polarjoint descriptiontracking

Problem

At the bottom of a loop, an airplane P has a constant speed $v_P$, and the radius of curvature of its path is $\rho$. The airplane is at a radial distance $r$ and at an angle $\theta$ from a radar tracking station at O.

Use $v_P = 75$ m/s, $\rho = 3000$ m, $r = 1000$ m and $\theta = 36.87^\circ$.

Find numerical values for $\ddot r$ and $\ddot\theta$ at this instant.

Solution

Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Describe the motion in path components

The information about the airplane is given in path terms. Its speed is constant, so $\dot v = 0$. At the bottom of the loop the velocity is horizontal and the center of curvature is straight above the airplane, so with $\hat{\mathbf i}$ to the right and $\hat{\mathbf j}$ upward,

$$\mathbf v = 75\,\hat{\mathbf i}\ \text{m/s}, \qquad \mathbf a = \dot v\,\hat{\mathbf e}_t + \frac{v^2}{\rho}\,\hat{\mathbf e}_n = 0 + \frac{(75)^2}{3000}\,\hat{\mathbf j} = 1.875\,\hat{\mathbf j}\ \text{m/s}^2$$
Step 2 · Draw the kinematics diagram

At the bottom of the loop the velocity is horizontal and the center of curvature is straight above P, so the acceleration points straight up.

Step 3 · Write the polar unit vectors at this instant $$\hat{\mathbf e}_r = \cos\theta\,\hat{\mathbf i} + \sin\theta\,\hat{\mathbf j} = 0.8\,\hat{\mathbf i} + 0.6\,\hat{\mathbf j}, \qquad \hat{\mathbf e}_\theta = -\sin\theta\,\hat{\mathbf i} + \cos\theta\,\hat{\mathbf j} = -0.6\,\hat{\mathbf i} + 0.8\,\hat{\mathbf j}$$
Step 4 · Find the rates ṙ and θ̇ from the velocity

In polar components the velocity is $\mathbf v = \dot r\,\hat{\mathbf e}_r + r\dot\theta\,\hat{\mathbf e}_\theta$. Projecting the known velocity onto the two unit vectors gives

$$\dot r = \mathbf v\cdot\hat{\mathbf e}_r = 75(0.8) = 60\ \text{m/s}, \qquad r\dot\theta = \mathbf v\cdot\hat{\mathbf e}_\theta = 75(-0.6) = -45\ \text{m/s}$$

so $\dot\theta = -45/1000 = -0.045$ rad/s. The angle is decreasing because the airplane is moving to the right, away from the vertical.

Step 5 · Find r̈ and θ̈ from the acceleration

In polar components the acceleration is $\mathbf a = (\ddot r - r\dot\theta^2)\,\hat{\mathbf e}_r + (r\ddot\theta + 2\dot r\dot\theta)\,\hat{\mathbf e}_\theta$. Projecting the known acceleration onto the unit vectors gives two scalar equations.

$$\ddot r - r\dot\theta^2 = \mathbf a\cdot\hat{\mathbf e}_r = 1.875(0.6) = 1.125\ \text{m/s}^2$$ $$r\ddot\theta + 2\dot r\dot\theta = \mathbf a\cdot\hat{\mathbf e}_\theta = 1.875(0.8) = 1.50\ \text{m/s}^2$$

Solving each equation for the unknown rate gives

$$\ddot r = 1.125 + 1000(-0.045)^2 = 3.15\ \text{m/s}^2$$ $$\ddot\theta = \frac{1.50 - 2(60)(-0.045)}{1000} = 0.0069\ \text{rad/s}^2$$
Answer

$\ddot r = 3.15$ m/s² and $\ddot\theta = 0.0069$ rad/s² (with $\dot r = 60$ m/s and $\dot\theta = -0.045$ rad/s).

Explore

  • The airplane flies at a constant speed, yet $\ddot r$ is not zero. Explain why in words.
  • Make $\rho$ very large, so that the airplane flies almost in a straight line. Is $\ddot r$ then zero? Why not?
  • Use the graphs to find the time at which $\ddot\theta$ is largest. Where is the airplane at that moment?
