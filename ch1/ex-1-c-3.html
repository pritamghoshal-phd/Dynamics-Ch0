Example 1.C.3Motion along a spiral

Problem

The path of point P is given in polar coordinates as $r = 4 + 2\theta$, where $r$ is in meters and $\theta$ is in radians. The angle $\theta$ increases at the constant rate $\dot\theta$.

Find (a) the acceleration vector of P when $\theta = \pi$, if $\dot\theta = 3$ rad/s, and (b) whether the speed of P is increasing, decreasing or constant.

Solution

Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Choose a description of the motion

The path is given as $r(\theta)$, so polar coordinates are the natural choice. We need $r$, $\dot r$ and $\ddot r$, together with $\theta$, $\dot\theta$ and $\ddot\theta$, at the instant $\theta = \pi$.

Step 2 · Differentiate the path equation

Differentiating $r = 4 + 2\theta$ with respect to time gives

$$\dot r = 2\dot\theta, \qquad \ddot r = 2\ddot\theta$$

The angular rate is constant, so $\ddot\theta = 0$ and $\ddot r = 0$. At $\theta = \pi$ with $\dot\theta = 3$ rad/s,

$$r = 4 + 2\pi = 10.28\ \text{m}, \qquad \dot r = 6\ \text{m/s}, \qquad \ddot r = 0$$
Step 3 · Draw the kinematics diagram

At θ = π the point P is on the negative x axis, so ê_r points in the −x direction and ê_θ points in the −y direction.

Step 4 · Write the velocity and the acceleration in polar components $$\mathbf v = \dot r\,\hat{\mathbf e}_r + r\dot\theta\,\hat{\mathbf e}_\theta = 6\,\hat{\mathbf e}_r + 30.85\,\hat{\mathbf e}_\theta\ \text{m/s}$$ $$\mathbf a = \left(\ddot r - r\dot\theta^2\right)\hat{\mathbf e}_r + \left(r\ddot\theta + 2\dot r\dot\theta\right)\hat{\mathbf e}_\theta = \left(0 - 10.28(3)^2\right)\hat{\mathbf e}_r + \left(0 + 2(6)(3)\right)\hat{\mathbf e}_\theta$$ $$\mathbf a = -92.55\,\hat{\mathbf e}_r + 36.0\,\hat{\mathbf e}_\theta\ \text{m/s}^2$$

At $\theta = \pi$ the unit vectors are $\hat{\mathbf e}_r = -\hat{\mathbf i}$ and $\hat{\mathbf e}_\theta = -\hat{\mathbf j}$. In Cartesian components the acceleration is therefore

$$\mathbf a = 92.55\,\hat{\mathbf i} - 36.0\,\hat{\mathbf j}\ \text{m/s}^2$$

The transverse part, $2\dot r\dot\theta$, is the Coriolis term. It is present because P moves outward while the radial line turns.

Step 5 · Decide whether the speed is changing

The rate of change of speed is the component of the acceleration along the velocity.

$$\dot v = \frac{\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf v}{v} = \frac{(-92.55)(6) + (36.0)(30.85)}{\sqrt{6^2 + 30.85^2}} = \frac{555.3}{31.43} = 17.7\ \text{m/s}^2$$

The result is positive, so the speed is increasing. This holds at every point of the path, because $v^2 = \dot r^2 + r^2\dot\theta^2 = 4\dot\theta^2 + r^2\dot\theta^2$ grows as $r$ grows.

Answer

(a) $\mathbf a = -92.6\,\hat{\mathbf e}_r + 36.0\,\hat{\mathbf e}_\theta = 92.6\,\hat{\mathbf i} - 36.0\,\hat{\mathbf j}$ m/s². (b) The speed is increasing, at $\dot v = 17.7$ m/s².
