Example 4.C.9Bullet passing through a block on a rough floor

impulse–momentumwork–energyfriction

Problem

Block A, of mass $m_A$, is at rest on a rough horizontal floor. Bullet B, of mass $m_B$, strikes A with speed $v_{B1}$, passes through it and leaves its left side with speed $v_{B2}$. Block A then slides to the left on the floor, where the coefficient of kinetic friction is $\mu_k$, and comes to rest after sliding a distance $d$.

The data are $m_A = 5$ kg, $m_B = 0.1$ kg, $v_{B1} = 800$ m/s, $v_{B2} = 350$ m/s and $\mu_k = 0.1$.

Find the distance $d$ that block A slides after the impact.

Solution

Every solution follows the four-step method: free-body diagrams, kinetics equations, kinematics, solve. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagrams

During the impact we take A and B together, so the large force between the bullet and the block is internal. The external forces are the weight, the normal force and friction from the floor. These forces are finite, and the impact lasts only a fraction of a millisecond, so their impulses during the impact are negligible.

After the impact we take block A alone. It is acted on by its weight $m_A g$, the normal force $N$ and the kinetic friction force $\mu_k N$, which acts to the right because A slides to the left.

Left: during the impact the weight, the normal force and the friction force are all finite. The impact lasts only a fraction of a millisecond, so their impulses are negligible and the horizontal momentum of A and B together is conserved. Right: after the impact A slides to the left, so friction acts on it to the right.

Step 2 · Write the kinetics equations

Impact. The horizontal momentum of A and B together is conserved. Take the left as positive, since everything moves to the left.

$$m_B\,v_{B1} + m_A(0) = m_B\,v_{B2} + m_A\,v_{A2}$$

Slide. In the vertical direction A has no acceleration, so $N = m_A g$. The friction force $\mu_k m_A g$ does negative work over the distance $d$. The work–energy principle from just after the impact to the stopping point gives

$$\tfrac12\,m_A\,v_{A2}^2 - \mu_k\,m_A\,g\,d = 0$$
Step 3 · Write the kinematics equations

Block A starts sliding with the speed $v_{A2}$ that it has just after the impact and ends at rest. No other kinematic relation is needed, because the bullet and the block move independently after the bullet leaves.

Step 4 · Solve $$v_{A2} = \frac{m_B\left(v_{B1} - v_{B2}\right)}{m_A} = \frac{0.1\,(800 - 350)}{5} = 9.00\ \text{m/s}$$ $$d = \frac{v_{A2}^2}{2\,\mu_k\,g} = \frac{(9.00)^2}{2\,(0.1)(9.81)} = 41.3\ \text{m}$$

The mass of A cancels from the second equation, so the sliding distance depends only on the speed just after the impact and on $\mu_k$. The block slides for $v_{A2}/(\mu_k g) = 9.17$ s.

Answer

$d = 41.3$ m (with $v_{A2} = 9.00$ m/s just after the impact).
