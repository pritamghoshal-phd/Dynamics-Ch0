Example 4.D.6Particle sticking to a pinned rod

angular momentumplastic impacthorizontal plane

Problem

Particles A and B, of masses $4m$ and $2m$, are attached to the ends of a stationary rigid rod of negligible mass. The rod is pinned to the ground at O, with A a distance $2L$ from O and B a distance $L$ from O. A third particle C, of mass $m$, moves perpendicular to the rod with speed $v_1$ and strikes B. On impact, C sticks to B. The system lies in the horizontal plane.

Find the angular speed of the rod immediately after the impact.

Solution

Every solution follows the four-step method: free-body diagrams, kinetics equations, kinematics, solve. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagram

Take the rod, A, B and C together, so that the impact force between C and B is internal. In the horizontal plane the only external force is the pin force at O, with components $O_x$ and $O_y$. The weights and the vertical support forces are perpendicular to the plane.

In the horizontal plane the only external force on this system is the pin force at O. The weights and the vertical support forces are perpendicular to the plane and have no moment about the vertical axis through O.

Step 2 · Write the kinetics equation (angular impulse and momentum about O)

The pin force acts at O, and the vertical forces are parallel to the axis through O. So no external force has a moment about the vertical axis through O, and the angular momentum about O is conserved through the impact.

$$\left(H_O\right)_1 = \left(H_O\right)_2$$
Step 3 · Write the kinematics equations

Before the impact only C moves, at speed $v_1$ perpendicular to the rod, a distance $L$ from O. So $\left(H_O\right)_1 = m v_1 L$. After the impact the rod turns with angular speed $\omega$. A moves at $2L\,\omega$, and B and C together move at $L\,\omega$, all perpendicular to the rod.

$$\left(H_O\right)_2 = 4m\,(2L)(2L\,\omega) + (2m + m)\,L\,(L\,\omega) = 19\,m L^2\omega$$
Step 4 · Solve $$m v_1 L = 19\,m L^2\,\omega \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \omega = \frac{v_1}{19\,L}$$

The rod turns in the direction in which C pushes B, which is clockwise in the top view of the figure.

Linear momentum is not conserved. Afterward the system has momentum $4m(2L\omega) - 3m(L\omega) = 5mL\omega = \tfrac{5}{19}\,m v_1$ in the direction opposite to $v_1$, so the pin impulse is $\tfrac{24}{19}\,m v_1$, also opposite to $v_1$. Only $1/19$ of the kinetic energy of C remains.

Answer

$\omega = \dfrac{v_1}{19\,L}$, clockwise in the top view. For example, $v_1 = 5$ m/s and $L = 0.5$ m give $\omega = 0.526$ rad/s.
