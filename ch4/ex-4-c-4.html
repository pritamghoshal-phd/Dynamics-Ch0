Example 4.C.4Coupling two cars: the energy lost

perfectly plastic impactconservation of momentumkinetic energy

Problem

Car A, of mass $m_A$, travels to the right with an initial speed $v_{A1}$. It runs into car B, of mass $m_B$, which is at rest. After the impact the two cars stay coupled together.

Find the change in kinetic energy of the system of A and B, $T_2 - T_1$, caused by the impact. What fraction of the initial kinetic energy is this change?

Solution

Every solution follows the four-step method: free-body diagrams, kinetics equations, kinematics, solve. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagrams

Take cars A and B together as the system. During the impact the coupling force $P$ between the cars is very large, but it is internal. The weights and the normal forces are vertical and finite. No horizontal force acts on the system from outside.

During the short impact the coupling force $P$ is very large. For the pair it is internal (equal and opposite). The weights and normal forces are finite and vertical, so they give no horizontal impulse.

Step 2 · Write the kinetics equations

With no horizontal external impulse, linear momentum is conserved in the horizontal direction:

$$m_A v_{A1} + m_B (0) = (m_A + m_B)\,v_2$$

The kinetic energies before and after the impact are

$$T_1 = \tfrac12 m_A v_{A1}^2, \qquad T_2 = \tfrac12 (m_A + m_B)\,v_2^2$$

We cannot use the work–energy equation across the impact, because the coupling force does an unknown amount of work as the bumpers deform.

Step 3 · Write the kinematics equation

The cars stay coupled, so after the impact they share one velocity $v_2$.

Step 4 · Solve

Momentum gives $v_2 = \dfrac{m_A}{m_A + m_B}\,v_{A1}$. Then

$$T_2 = \tfrac12 (m_A + m_B)\left(\frac{m_A v_{A1}}{m_A + m_B}\right)^2 = \frac{m_A}{m_A + m_B}\,T_1$$ $$T_2 - T_1 = -\frac{m_B}{m_A + m_B}\,T_1 = -\frac{m_A m_B\,v_{A1}^2}{2(m_A + m_B)}$$ $$\frac{T_2 - T_1}{T_1} = -\frac{m_B}{m_A + m_B}$$

With the sample values, $v_2 = 12$ m/s and 40 % of the kinetic energy is lost. The lost energy goes into deforming the couplers, sound and heat.

Answer

$T_2 - T_1 = -\dfrac{m_A m_B\, v_{A1}^2}{2(m_A + m_B)}$, which is the fraction $\dfrac{T_2 - T_1}{T_1} = -\dfrac{m_B}{m_A + m_B}$ of the initial kinetic energy.

  • Make the two masses equal. Check that exactly half of the kinetic energy is lost.
