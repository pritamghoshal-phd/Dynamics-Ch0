Example 4.A.10Slider pulled along a rotating slotted arm

polar componentsCoriolis termhorizontal plane

Problem

Particle P (weight $W$) slides in a smooth straight slot cut into an arm. The arm turns in a horizontal plane about a vertical shaft at end O, counterclockwise at a constant rate $\omega$. A cord attached to P passes through the hollow shaft at O and is pulled from below at E, so that P moves toward O at a constant rate $\dot r$.

The data are $W = 5$ lb, $\omega = 5$ rad/s, $r = 0.75$ ft and $\dot r = -0.5$ ft/s.

Find (a) the tension in the cord, (b) the normal force of the slot on P, and (c) which side of the slot, A or B, touches P.

Solution

Every solution follows the four-step method: free-body diagrams, kinetics equations, kinematics, solve. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagrams

Isolate P. In the horizontal plane the cord pulls P toward O with tension $T$, along $-\hat{\mathbf e}_r$. The smooth slot can only push perpendicular to its walls, so its force $N$ acts along $\hat{\mathbf e}_\theta$. We draw $N$ in the $+\hat{\mathbf e}_\theta$ direction and let its sign tell us which wall pushes. In the vertical direction the weight $W$ is balanced by the force $N_z$ from the bottom of the slot.

Left: in the horizontal plane the cord pulls P toward O with tension $T$, and the smooth slot pushes on P with a force $N$ perpendicular to the arm. $N$ is drawn in the $+\hat{\mathbf e}_\theta$ direction, and its sign will tell us which side of the slot touches P. Right: the weight $W$ is balanced by the vertical force $N_z$ from the bottom of the slot.

Step 2 · Write the kinetics equations

With $m = W/g = 5/32.2 = 0.1553$ slug, Newton's second law in polar components gives

$$\sum F_r = m a_r: \quad -T = m a_r, \qquad \sum F_\theta = m a_\theta: \quad N = m a_\theta$$
Step 3 · Write the kinematics equations

The slider is pulled in at a constant rate, so $\ddot r = 0$. The arm turns at a constant rate, so $\dot\omega = 0$. The polar components of the acceleration are then

$$a_r = \ddot r - r\omega^2 = 0 - (0.75)(5)^2 = -18.75\ \text{ft/s}^2$$ $$a_\theta = r\dot\omega + 2\dot r\omega = 0 + 2(-0.5)(5) = -5\ \text{ft/s}^2$$
Step 4 · Solve $$T = -m a_r = (0.1553)(18.75) = 2.91\ \text{lb}$$ $$N = m a_\theta = (0.1553)(-5) = -0.776\ \text{lb}$$

The negative sign means that the slot pushes P in the $-\hat{\mathbf e}_\theta$ direction, backward against the rotation. Only the wall on the leading side of the slot, the side toward which the arm turns, can push P backward. That wall is side A.

The slot holds P back because P moves inward, to points of the arm that move more slowly. The term $2\dot r\omega$ is the Coriolis part of the acceleration.

Answer

(a) $T = 2.91$ lb. (b) $|N| = 0.776$ lb, acting opposite to $\hat{\mathbf e}_\theta$. (c) P touches side A.
