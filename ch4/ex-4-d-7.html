Example 4.D.7Particle on a cord through a hole

central forceangular momentumpath components

Problem

A particle of mass $m$ slides on a smooth horizontal table top. It is attached to a cord that passes through a hole in the table at O, and a force $T$ is applied to the free end of the cord below the table. The particle follows the path $x^2/9 + y^2/36 = 1$, with $x$ and $y$ in metres and O at the center of the ellipse. The speed of the particle at position A, $(3, 0)$ m, is $v_A$.

Use $m = 2$ kg and $v_A = 10$ m/s. Find the force $T$ when the particle is at position B, $(0, 6)$ m.

InteractiveTop view of the table and the elliptical path

The cord force always points at O, so the angular momentum about O never changes. The particle is fastest at A, on the short axis, and slowest at B, on the long axis.

Solution

Every solution follows the four-step method: free-body diagrams, kinetics equations, kinematics, solve. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagrams

In the top view the only horizontal force on the particle is the cord force $T$, directed from the particle toward the hole O. In the side view the weight $mg$ and the normal force $N$ from the smooth table are vertical, and they balance.

The cord pulls the particle toward the hole O. The weight and the normal force from the smooth table are vertical and balance, so the cord force is the only horizontal force, and it always passes through O.

Step 2 · Write the kinetics equations

The cord force passes through O, so it has no moment about O, and the angular momentum about O is conserved.

$$m\,r_A v_A = m\,r_B v_B$$

At B, Newton's second law in the normal direction of the path gives the cord force. At B the normal direction points straight at O.

$$\sum F_n = T = m\,\frac{v_B^2}{\rho_B}$$
Step 3 · Write the kinematics equations

At A and at B the velocity is perpendicular to the position vector, so the angular momentum there is simply $m r v$, with $r_A = 3$ m and $r_B = 6$ m.

Near B the path is $y = 6\sqrt{1 - x^2/9} \approx 6 - x^2/3$, so $y'' = -2/3$ and $y' = 0$ at B. The radius of curvature there is

$$\rho_B = \frac{\left(1 + y'^2\right)^{3/2}}{|y''|} = \frac{1}{2/3} = 1.5\ \text{m}$$
Step 4 · Solve $$v_B = \frac{r_A}{r_B}\,v_A = \frac{3}{6}(10) = 5\ \text{m/s}$$ $$T = 2\,\frac{(5)^2}{1.5} = 33.3\ \text{N}$$

Check: this path is traced by $x = 3\cos\Omega t$, $y = 6\sin\Omega t$ with $\Omega = v_A/6 = 5/3$ rad/s. Its acceleration is $-\Omega^2\mathbf r$, which always points at O, so the cord force is $T = m\Omega^2 r = 2(25/9)(6) = 33.3$ N at B.

Answer

$T = 33.3$ N at B, where the speed is $v_B = 5$ m/s.
