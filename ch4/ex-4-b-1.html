Example 4.B.1Particle on a rod pushed by a normal force

work–energywork of a momentgravity

Problem

A particle P of mass $m = 6$ kg is attached to a light rod of length $L = 2$ m. The rod turns in a vertical plane about a fixed pin O, which divides the rod into a part of length $0.6L$ that carries P and a part of length $0.4L$. A force $F = 100$ N acts at the end of the shorter part and is kept normal to the rod. The angle $\theta$ of the rod is measured counterclockwise from the horizontal, and the assembly is released from rest at $\theta = 0$.

Find the speed of particle P when $\theta = 90°$.

Solution

Every solution follows the four-step method: free-body diagrams, kinetics equations, kinematics, solve. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagram

We isolate the rod together with particle P. The rod is light, so the only weight is $mg$ at P. The other forces are the applied force $F$ at the end of the rod and the pin reactions $O_x$ and $O_y$.

The pin reactions $O_x$ and $O_y$ act at the fixed point O, so they do no work. Only the force $F$ and the weight $mg$ do work as the assembly turns.

Step 2 · Write the work–energy equation

Let state 1 be the release at $\theta = 0$ and state 2 be $\theta = 90°$. The particle starts from rest, so $T_1 = 0$, and the work–energy principle becomes

$$T_1 + U_{1\to2} = T_2 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad U_{1\to2}^{F} + U_{1\to2}^{g} = \tfrac12 m v_2^2$$
Step 3 · Find the work of each force

The force $F$ always acts normal to the rod at a distance $0.4L$ from O. When the rod turns through a small angle $d\theta$, the point of application moves a distance $0.4L\,d\theta$ in the direction of $F$. The work of $F$ is therefore

$$U_{1\to2}^{F} = \int_0^{\pi/2} F\,(0.4L)\,d\theta = F(0.4L)\frac{\pi}{2} = 100(0.8)\frac{\pi}{2} = 125.7\ \text{J}$$

At $\theta = 0$ the particle is level with O. At $\theta = 90°$ it is directly below O, a distance $0.6L = 1.2$ m lower. Gravity therefore does positive work.

$$U_{1\to2}^{g} = mg(0.6L) = 6(9.81)(1.2) = 70.6\ \text{J}$$
Step 4 · Solve $$\tfrac12(6)\,v_2^2 = 125.7 + 70.6 = 196.3\ \text{J} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad v_2 = \sqrt{\frac{2(196.3)}{6}} = 8.09\ \text{m/s}$$

The work of a force that stays normal to a rotating arm is the moment times the angle turned, $U = M\,\Delta\theta$ with $M = F(0.4L)$. The simulation in the animation integrates the equation of motion and gives the same speed at $\theta = 90°$.

Answer

$v_P = 8.09$ m/s when $\theta = 90°$, with P moving horizontally to the right.

Explore

  • Set $F = 0$. The speed now comes from gravity alone. Check it against $v = \sqrt{2g(0.6L)}$.
  • Double the mass of P. Does the speed at $\theta = 90°$ double, halve, or do something else? Explain with the work–energy equation.
  • Watch the work bars. Why does the work of $F$ grow at a constant rate with $\theta$, while the work of gravity does not?
