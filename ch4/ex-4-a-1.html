Example 4.A.1A braking car

Newton's lawconstant accelerationfriction

Problem

As a car of mass $m$ brakes with a constant braking force $F_f$, its speed drops from $v_1$ to $v_2 = 0$ in a distance $s$ and in a time $t$.

The data are $m = 2000$ kg, $v_1 = 80$ m/s and $F_f = 10{,}000$ N.

Find (a) the distance $s$ and (b) the time $t$.

Solution

Every solution follows the four-step method: free-body diagrams, kinetics equations, kinematics, solve. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagram

We treat the car as a particle. Three forces act on it. The weight $mg$ acts downward, the road pushes upward with the normal force $N$, and the braking force $F_f$ acts on the tires at the road, opposite to the motion. We take $x$ in the direction of motion and $y$ upward.

The car moves to the right. The braking force $F_f$ acts on the tires at the road, opposite to the motion. The normal force $N$ stands for the sum of the forces on all four tires.

Step 2 · Write the kinetics equations

Newton's second law in the $x$ and $y$ directions gives

$$\sum F_x = -F_f = m a_x, \qquad \sum F_y = N - mg = 0$$

The car does not leave the road, so its acceleration has no $y$ component.

Step 3 · Write the kinematics equations

The acceleration $a_x = -F_f/m$ is constant, so the constant-acceleration relations of Chapter 0 apply.

$$v_2^2 = v_1^2 + 2 a_x s, \qquad v_2 = v_1 + a_x t$$
Step 4 · Solve

The acceleration is $a_x = -10{,}000/2000 = -5$ m/s². With $v_2 = 0$,

$$s = \frac{v_1^2}{2F_f/m} = \frac{80^2}{2(5)} = 640\ \text{m}, \qquad t = \frac{v_1}{F_f/m} = \frac{80}{5} = 16\ \text{s}$$

The normal force is $N = mg = 19{,}620$ N. The braking force is about half of $N$, so the tires need a friction coefficient of at least 0.51.

Answer

(a) $s = 640$ m. (b) $t = 16$ s.

Explore

  • Double the braking force. Why does the stopping distance halve, and why does the stopping time also halve?
  • Reduce the initial speed to 30 m/s. The stopping distance falls by a factor of about 7. Explain this with the equation $s = v_1^2 m/(2F_f)$.
