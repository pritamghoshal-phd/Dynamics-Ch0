Example 4.C.3Tug hauling in a barge with its winch

conservation of momentumrelative velocityaverage force

Problem

A tug A of mass $m$ tows a coal barge B of mass $M$ at a speed $v_{A1}$. During a short time interval $\Delta t$ the engines of the tug are turned off, and the stern winch of the tug takes in the towing cable at a rate $v_{B/A}$, so the barge closes on the tug at that relative speed. Ignore the water resistance on the tug and the barge, and assume that the cable stays taut.

Find (a) the speed of the tug during this period and (b) the average tension in the towing cable during the time $\Delta t$.

The book solves this problem with symbols. The animation uses the sample values $m = 40$ t, $M = 400$ t, $v_{A1} = 3$ m/s, $v_{B/A} = 1$ m/s and $\Delta t = 5$ s, which you can change.

Solution

Every solution follows the four-step method: free-body diagrams, kinetics equations, kinematics, solve. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagrams

First take the tug and the barge together. The weights and the buoyancy forces are vertical, and water resistance is neglected. The cable tension is internal to this system, so no horizontal force acts on it from outside. Then take the tug alone. The cable pulls it backward with the tension $T$.

The weights and the buoyancy forces are vertical, and water resistance is neglected, so nothing pushes the tug and barge together horizontally from outside. For the system the cable tension $T$ is internal. Taken alone, the tug is pulled backward by $T$ and the barge is pulled forward by $T$. Positive $x$ points to the left, in the direction of travel.

Step 2 · Write the kinetics equations (impulse and momentum)

Take the direction of travel (to the left) as positive. For the tug and barge together there is no horizontal impulse, so the linear momentum of the system is conserved between state 1 (towing) and state 2 (cable coming in):

$$(m + M)\,v_{A1} = m\,v_{A2} + M\,v_{B2}$$

For the tug alone, the cable tension acts backward during the time $\Delta t$:

$$m\,v_{A2} = m\,v_{A1} - T_{avg}\,\Delta t$$
Step 3 · Write the kinematics equation

The winch takes in the cable at the rate $v_{B/A}$, so the barge moves toward the tug with that relative speed:

$$v_{B2} = v_{A2} + v_{B/A}$$
Step 4 · Solve

Substituting the kinematics into the momentum equation gives $(m + M)v_{A1} = (m + M)v_{A2} + M v_{B/A}$, so

$$v_{A2} = v_{A1} - \frac{M}{m + M}\,v_{B/A}$$

The tug equation then gives the average tension

$$T_{avg} = \frac{m\left(v_{A1} - v_{A2}\right)}{\Delta t} = \frac{m M\,v_{B/A}}{(m + M)\,\Delta t}$$

With the sample values, $v_{A2} = 3 - (400/440)(1) = 2.09$ m/s, the barge moves at $3.09$ m/s and $T_{avg} = (40\,000)(0.909)/5 = 7.27$ kN. The heavier the barge compared with the tug, the more of the relative speed comes from slowing the tug.

Answer

(a) $v_{A2} = v_{A1} - \dfrac{M}{m + M}\,v_{B/A}$. (b) $T_{avg} = \dfrac{m M\,v_{B/A}}{(m + M)\,\Delta t}$.
