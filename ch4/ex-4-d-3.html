Example 4.D.3Disk tied to a peg by a rubber band

angular momentumwork–energycentral force

Problem

Disk P, of mass 0.2 kg, slides on a smooth horizontal surface. A rubber band with stiffness $k = 10$ N/m and unstretched length $L_0 = 0.6$ m connects P to a fixed peg at O. P is set into motion with speed $v_{P1} = 15$ m/s when it is a distance $R_1 = 0.4$ m from O. Its velocity makes an angle of $36.87°$ with the radial unit vector $\hat e_R$, on the side of the transverse unit vector $\hat e_\theta$.

Find the polar components of the velocity of P when P is a distance $R_2 = 1.5$ m from O.

Solution

Every solution follows the four-step method: free-body diagrams, kinetics equations, kinematics, solve. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagrams

In the top view the only horizontal force on P is the band force $F_s$. It points from P toward the peg O, with magnitude $k(R - L_0)$ while the band is stretched and zero while it is slack. In the side view the weight $W$ and the normal force $N$ from the smooth table are vertical, and they balance.

The band force $F_s = k(R - L_0)$ points from P toward the peg O while the band is stretched and is zero while it is slack. The weight and the normal force from the table are vertical and balance. So the only horizontal force always passes through O.

Step 2 · Write the kinetics equations

The band force always passes through O, so it has no moment about O, and the angular momentum of P about O is conserved.

$$m R_1 v_{\theta 1} = m R_2 v_{\theta 2}$$

The band force is a spring force and the surface is smooth, so mechanical energy is conserved as well. The band is slack at $R_1 = 0.4$ m, so it stores no energy there.

$$\tfrac12 m v_{P1}^2 + 0 = \tfrac12 m v_{P2}^2 + \tfrac12 k\,(R_2 - L_0)^2$$
Step 3 · Write the kinematics equations

In polar components the velocity is $\mathbf v = v_R\,\hat e_R + v_\theta\,\hat e_\theta$, with $v^2 = v_R^2 + v_\theta^2$. In position 1,

$$v_{R1} = 15\cos 36.87° = 12\ \text{m/s}, \qquad v_{\theta 1} = 15\sin 36.87° = 9\ \text{m/s}$$
Step 4 · Solve

Angular momentum gives the transverse component directly.

$$v_{\theta 2} = \frac{R_1 v_{\theta 1}}{R_2} = \frac{0.4(9)}{1.5} = 2.40\ \text{m/s}$$

Energy gives the speed. The band is stretched by $1.5 - 0.6 = 0.9$ m.

$$v_{P2}^2 = v_{P1}^2 - \frac{k}{m}(R_2 - L_0)^2 = 225 - \frac{10}{0.2}(0.9)^2 = 184.5\ \text{m}^2/\text{s}^2$$ $$v_{R2} = \sqrt{v_{P2}^2 - v_{\theta 2}^2} = \sqrt{184.5 - 5.76} = 13.37\ \text{m/s}$$

The positive root applies because P is still moving away from O the first time it reaches 1.5 m. On the way back in, $v_R$ would be $-13.37$ m/s.

Answer

$\mathbf v_{P2} = 13.37\,\hat e_R + 2.40\,\hat e_\theta$ m/s, a speed of 13.58 m/s.
