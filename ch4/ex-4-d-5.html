Example 4.D.5Bullet embedding in a pinned bar

angular momentumimpactpin reaction

Problem

Particles A and B, each of mass $M$, are attached to a rigid bar OA of negligible mass. B is at the middle of the bar and A is at its end, a distance $L$ from O. The bar is pinned to the ground at O and hangs at rest when a bullet P of mass $m$ strikes A. Just before the impact the bullet moves with speed $v_{P1}$ in the vertical plane, upward and to the right at $36.87°$ from the vertical. The bullet becomes embedded in A.

Use $M = 2$ kg, $m = 0.1$ kg, $L = 3$ m and $v_{P1} = 700$ m/s. Find the angular velocity of bar OA immediately after the collision.

Solution

Every solution follows the four-step method: free-body diagrams, kinetics equations, kinematics, solve. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagram

Take the bar, A, B and the bullet together, so that the large contact force between the bullet and A is internal. During the impact the external forces are the pin force at O, with components $O_x$ and $O_y$, and the weights. The pin force can be very large, but it acts at O. The weights are finite, so their impulse during the very short impact is negligible.

Step 2 · Write the kinetics equation (angular impulse and momentum about O)

The pin force has no moment about O and the weights have negligible impulse, so the angular momentum of the system about O is the same just before and just after the impact.

$$\left(H_O\right)_1 = \left(H_O\right)_2$$
Step 3 · Write the kinematics equations

Take $x$ to the right and $y$ upward, with O as origin. Just before the impact only the bullet moves. It is at $\mathbf r = -L\,\hat\jmath$ with velocity $\mathbf v_{P1} = v_{P1}\left(\sin 36.87°\,\hat\imath + \cos 36.87°\,\hat\jmath\right)$.

$$\left(\mathbf H_O\right)_1 = \mathbf r \times m\mathbf v_{P1} = m L v_{P1}\sin 36.87°\;\hat k$$

Just after the impact the bar turns with angular velocity $\omega_2$. A (with the bullet) moves at $L\omega_2$ and B moves at $(L/2)\,\omega_2$, both perpendicular to the bar.

$$\left(H_O\right)_2 = \left[(M + m)L^2 + M\left(\frac L2\right)^2\right]\omega_2$$
Step 4 · Solve $$\left(H_O\right)_1 = 0.1(3)(700)(0.6) = 126\ \text{kg·m}^2/\text{s}$$ $$(M + m)L^2 + M\left(\frac L2\right)^2 = 2.1(9) + 2(2.25) = 23.4\ \text{kg·m}^2$$ $$\omega_2 = \frac{126}{23.4} = 5.38\ \text{rad/s, counterclockwise}$$

Linear momentum is not conserved, because the pin pushes on the bar during the impact. The pin impulse is $8.08\,\hat\imath - 56.0\,\hat\jmath$ N·s. Only about 1.4% of the bullet's kinetic energy remains after the impact.

Answer

$\boldsymbol\omega_{OA} = 5.38\,\hat k$ rad/s (counterclockwise in the figure).
