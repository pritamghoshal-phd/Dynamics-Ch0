Example 4.D.4Spinning arm with a sliding particle and a rising particle

angular momentumwork–energysystems

Problem

Arm OC turns about a vertical shaft. Particle A slides on the smooth horizontal arm, and particle B slides on the smooth vertical shaft. A cord attached to A runs over a small pulley at O and down to B. The masses of A and B are 0.5 kg each. The masses of the guides and the pulley are negligible, and friction is negligible everywhere. At the position $R = 0.8$ m, A is not sliding over its guide, and the arm turns at $\omega = 6$ rad/s.

Find, for the position where $R = 1.2$ m, (a) the angular speed of arm OC and (b) the speed of particle B.

Solution

Every solution follows the four-step method: free-body diagrams, kinetics equations, kinematics, solve. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagrams

For the whole system (arm, pulley, cord, A and B), the external forces are the two weights and the bearing forces on the shaft. The bearing forces act on the axis, and the weights are parallel to it, so none of these forces has a moment about the axis.

In the top view, the only horizontal force on A is the cord tension $T$, which points at O. The smooth arm cannot push A along the arm, and because the arm is massless and turns freely it cannot push A across the arm either. Particle B is pulled up by $T$, pulled down by its weight, and pushed sideways by the smooth shaft.

For the whole system the bearing forces act on the shaft axis and the weights are parallel to it, so they have no moment about the axis. On A the only horizontal force is the cord tension $T$, which points at O (the vertical weight and normal force are not shown in the top view). On B the cord pulls up and the smooth shaft pushes sideways.

Step 2 · Write the kinetics equations

Angular momentum. The angular momentum of the system about the shaft is conserved. Particle B is on the axis, so it has no angular momentum about it. A contributes $m R\,v_{\theta}$.

$$m R_1 v_{\theta 1} = m R_2 v_{\theta 2}$$

Work and energy. All surfaces are smooth, the cord is inextensible and the pulley is massless, so only gravity does work on the system.

$$T_1 + V_1 = T_2 + V_2$$
Step 3 · Write the kinematics equations

A has a transverse component $v_\theta = R\omega$ and a radial component $\dot R$. The cord is inextensible, so B rises exactly as fast as A moves outward, $v_B = \dot R$, and B rises by $R_2 - R_1 = 0.4$ m. The kinetic energy of the system is therefore

$$T = \tfrac12 m\left(\dot R^2 + R^2\omega^2\right) + \tfrac12 m v_B^2 = \tfrac12 m R^2\omega^2 + m v_B^2$$

In position 1, A is not sliding, so $\dot R = 0$ and $v_B = 0$.

Step 4 · Solve $$\omega_2 = \omega_1\left(\frac{R_1}{R_2}\right)^2 = 6\left(\frac{0.8}{1.2}\right)^2 = 2.67\ \text{rad/s}$$

For the energy equation, $R_1\omega_1 = 4.80$ m/s and $R_2\omega_2 = 3.20$ m/s.

$$\tfrac12(0.5)(4.80)^2 = \tfrac12(0.5)(3.20)^2 + 0.5\,v_B^2 + 0.5(9.81)(0.4)$$ $$5.76 = 2.56 + 0.5\,v_B^2 + 1.962 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad v_B = 1.57\ \text{m/s}$$

The animation integrates the equation of motion $2m\ddot R = mR\omega^2 - mg$ with $\omega = R_1^2\omega_1/R^2$, and it gives the same speed of B at $R = 1.2$ m. A starts to slide because $mR_1\omega_1^2 = 14.4$ N is larger than the weight of B, 4.91 N.

Answer

(a) $\omega_2 = 2.67$ rad/s. (b) $v_B = 1.57$ m/s, upward.
