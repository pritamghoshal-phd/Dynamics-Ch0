Example 4.A.9Zero-gravity flight

path componentsradius of curvaturefree fall

Problem

An aircraft is used to simulate weightlessness for its passengers. It climbs at 45° nose high, flies over the top of its path, and then descends at 30° nose low. During about 25 s near the top of the path the passengers feel weightless. At the maximum height the aircraft travels at $v = 230$ m/s, and the gravitational acceleration there is $g = 9.79$ m/s².

Find the radius of curvature of the path at the maximum height. What is the shape of the trajectory near the maximum height?

Solution

Every solution follows the four-step method: free-body diagrams, kinetics equations, kinematics, solve. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagram

Take a passenger as the particle. A passenger feels weightless when the seat pushes on him with zero force. At the top of the path the only force on the passenger is then his weight $mg$, downward. The same is true for the aircraft as a whole, because the pilot sets the thrust to cancel the drag, so the aircraft moves as a free projectile.

Left: at the top only the weight acts on the passenger, so the seat pushes with zero force. Right: the path near the top. The velocity is horizontal, so $\hat{\mathbf e}_t$ is horizontal and $\hat{\mathbf e}_n$ points down toward the center of curvature.

Step 2 · Write the kinetics equations

Use path components. At the top of the path the velocity is horizontal, so $\hat{\mathbf e}_t$ is horizontal and $\hat{\mathbf e}_n$ points straight down, toward the center of curvature.

$$\sum F_t = m\dot v: \quad 0 = m\dot v, \qquad \sum F_n = m\frac{v^2}{\rho}: \quad mg = m\frac{v^2}{\rho}$$
Step 3 · Write the kinematics equations

The acceleration in path components is $\mathbf a = \dot v\,\hat{\mathbf e}_t + \dfrac{v^2}{\rho}\,\hat{\mathbf e}_n$. Since the acceleration is $\mathbf a = g$ downward and $\hat{\mathbf e}_n$ is downward at the top, the normal component is $a_n = v^2/\rho = g$ and the tangential component is zero.

Step 4 · Solve $$\rho = \frac{v^2}{g} = \frac{(230)^2}{9.79} = 5400\ \text{m} \approx 17{,}700\ \text{ft}$$

While the passengers are weightless, the acceleration of the aircraft is constant and equal to $g$ downward. Its horizontal velocity is therefore constant, and its vertical position changes as $\tfrac12 g t^2$. This is projectile motion, so the trajectory near the top is a parabola. The circle of radius $\rho$ in the animation touches this parabola at the top and has the same curvature there.

The free-fall part from 45° nose high to 30° nose low lasts $v(1 + \tan 30°)/g = 37$ s with these numbers. The real aircraft flies more slowly at the top, which gives the shorter weightless time of about 25 s quoted in the problem.

Answer

$\rho = v^2/g = 5400$ m (about 17,700 ft). Near the top the path is a parabola, the path of a projectile.
