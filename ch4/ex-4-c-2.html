Example 4.C.2Block that must first overcome static friction

linear impulsestatic frictionvariable force

Problem

Block P weighs 100 lb and is initially at rest on a horizontal surface. At $t = 0$ a horizontal force $F(t)$ begins to act on it. The force grows linearly from zero to 200 lb at $t = 10$ s. The coefficients of static and kinetic friction between the block and the surface are $\mu_s = 0.5$ and $\mu_k = 0.2$.

Find the speed of P at $t = 10$ s.

Solution

Every solution follows the four-step method: free-body diagrams, kinetics equations, kinematics, solve. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagrams

The block is the system. The weight $W$ acts downward, the normal force $N$ acts upward and the push $F(t) = 20t$ lb acts to the right. Before the block slips, friction is a static force $F_s$ of unknown size, limited to $\mu_s N$. After the block starts to slide, friction is $\mu_k N$ and points to the left.

Left: P is at rest, so the static friction force $F_s$ simply balances $F$, up to its limit $\mu_s N$. Right: once P slides, the friction force is $\mu_k N$, opposite to the motion.

Step 2 · Find when the block starts to slide

Vertical equilibrium gives $N = W = 100$ lb. The block stays at rest as long as the push can be balanced by static friction, that is, as long as $F(t) \le \mu_s N = 50$ lb. The block therefore starts to move at

$$20\,t_1 = 50 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad t_1 = 2.5\ \text{s}$$

A common mistake is to integrate the force from $t = 0$. Before $t_1$ the friction force cancels the push exactly, so that part of the impulse produces no motion.

Step 3 · Write the kinetics equation (impulse and momentum)

From $t_1 = 2.5$ s, when the speed is zero, to $t_2 = 10$ s, the horizontal impulse–momentum equation is

$$\frac{W}{g}\,v_2 = 0 + \int_{t_1}^{t_2} 20\,t\,dt - \mu_k W\,(t_2 - t_1)$$
Step 4 · Solve

The impulse of the push is

$$\int_{2.5}^{10} 20\,t\,dt = 10\left(10^2 - 2.5^2\right) = 937.5\ \text{lb·s}$$

The impulse of friction is $\mu_k W (t_2 - t_1) = (0.2)(100)(7.5) = 150$ lb·s. The mass is $W/g = 100/32.2 = 3.106$ slug. Therefore

$$v_2 = \frac{937.5 - 150}{3.106} = 253.6\ \text{ft/s}$$

After $t_1$ the push (at least 50 lb) is always larger than kinetic friction (20 lb), so the block keeps speeding up and never stops again.

Answer

$v = 253.6$ ft/s at $t = 10$ s. The block starts to slide at $t_1 = 2.5$ s.

Explore

  • Raise $\mu_s$ to 1. When does the block start to move now, and how fast is it at 10 s?
  • Set $\mu_k = \mu_s$. Why does the speed curve now start with zero slope?
  • What would you get if you integrated the push from $t = 0$ and ignored the static phase? Why is that wrong?
