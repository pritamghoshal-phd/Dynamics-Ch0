Example 4.B.8Block pushed up a smooth slot against a spring

work–energyspringsgeometry

Problem

Block A, of mass $m$, slides in a smooth slot inclined at $\theta = 53.13°$ to the horizontal. A spring of stiffness $k$ connects A to a fixed pin O below the slot. A constant horizontal force $F$ acts on the block. The system is released from rest in position 1, where the spring is perpendicular to the slot and unstretched. In position 2, a distance $d$ farther up the slot, block A is directly above O.

Use $m = 5$ kg, $k = 50$ N/m, $d = 0.2$ m and $F = 300$ N.

Find the speed of A in position 2, assuming that it gets there. If A does not reach position 2, explain why not.

Solution

Every solution follows the four-step method: free-body diagrams, kinetics equations, kinematics, solve. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagram

We isolate block A. The forces are the horizontal force $F$, the weight $mg$, the spring force $F_s$ directed toward O, and the force $N$ of the smooth slot.

The slot is smooth, so its force $N$ is perpendicular to the motion and does no work. The force $F$ and the weight $mg$ are constant. The spring force $F_s = k(\ell - \ell_0)$ points along the spring toward O, and we count its work through the spring potential energy.

Step 2 · Work out the geometry

Let $\ell_0$ be the perpendicular distance from O to the slot, which is the spring length in position 1. In position 2 the spring is vertical and the block has moved $d$ along the slot. The triangle formed by O, position 1 and position 2 has a right angle at position 1, and at position 2 the vertical side makes the angle $90° - \theta$ with the slot. Therefore

$$\ell_0 = d\tan(90° - \theta) = d\,\frac{\cos\theta}{\sin\theta} = 0.2\left(\frac{0.6}{0.8}\right) = 0.15\ \text{m}$$ $$\ell_2 = \sqrt{\ell_0^2 + d^2} = \sqrt{0.15^2 + 0.2^2} = 0.25\ \text{m}$$

The spring is stretched by $\ell_2 - \ell_0 = 0.10$ m in position 2. Moving $d$ up the slot carries the block $d\cos\theta = 0.12$ m to the right and $d\sin\theta = 0.16$ m up.

Step 3 · Write the work–energy equation

The slot force does no work. We count the spring through its potential energy and compute the works of the constant forces $F$ and $mg$ directly.

$$T_1 + U_{1\to2}^{F} + U_{1\to2}^{g} = T_2 + V_{s2} - V_{s1}$$ $$U_{1\to2}^{F} = F(d\cos\theta) = 300(0.12) = 36.0\ \text{J}, \qquad U_{1\to2}^{g} = -mg(d\sin\theta) = -5(9.81)(0.16) = -7.85\ \text{J}$$ $$V_{s2} - V_{s1} = \tfrac12(50)(0.10)^2 - 0 = 0.25\ \text{J}$$
Step 4 · Solve and check that A reaches position 2 $$\tfrac12(5)\,v_2^2 = 36.0 - 7.85 - 0.25 = 27.90\ \text{J} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad v_2 = \sqrt{\frac{2(27.90)}{5}} = 3.34\ \text{m/s}$$

The block does reach position 2. At any distance $s$ up the slot, the kinetic energy is

$$T(s) = 180s - 39.2s - \tfrac12(50)\left(\sqrt{0.15^2 + s^2} - 0.15\right)^2$$

This is positive for every $s$ between 0 and $d$, so the block never stops on the way.

Answer

Block A reaches position 2 with a speed $v_2 = 3.34$ m/s up the slot.
