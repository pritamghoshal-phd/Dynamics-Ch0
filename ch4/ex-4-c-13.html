Example 4.C.13Newton's cradle

successive impactsrestitutionwork–energy

Problem

A Newton's cradle is made of $N$ identical pendulums. The ball of pendulum 1 has a horizontal velocity $v_0$ when it strikes the ball of pendulum 2. All the other pendulums are at rest before this impact. The coefficient of restitution for every impact between balls is $e$.

Find (a) the speed of ball $N$ immediately after the final impact and (b) the largest height $h$ reached by ball $N$.

Solution

Every solution follows the four-step method: free-body diagrams, kinetics equations, kinematics, solve. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagrams

We look at two neighboring balls, $i$ and $i + 1$, during their impact. Both hang from vertical cords, so the tensions and the weights are vertical. The contact forces $F$ are horizontal, equal and opposite. For ball $N$ after its impact we draw the tension along its cord and its weight.

Left: at each impact the cords are vertical and the balls are side by side, so the contact forces $F$ are horizontal and equal and opposite. The tensions and weights are vertical, so the horizontal momentum of the two colliding balls is conserved. Right: after its impact ball N swings up. The tension is perpendicular to its path and does no work.

Step 2 · Write the kinetics equations

For the impact of ball $i$, moving with speed $v_i$, on ball $i + 1$ at rest, the horizontal momentum of the two balls is conserved and the coefficient of restitution applies. All the masses are equal to $m$.

$$m\,v_i = m\,v_i' + m\,v_{i+1}, \qquad v_{i+1} - v_i' = e\,v_i$$

After its impact, ball $N$ swings up. The tension does no work, so the work–energy principle gives

$$\tfrac12\,m\,v_N^2 = m\,g\,h$$
Step 3 · Write the kinematics equations

The balls are at the bottom of their arcs during the impacts, so their velocities are horizontal and the impacts are central. We assume that each impact is over before the next one begins.

Step 4 · Solve

Adding the two impact equations gives $2\,v_{i+1} = (1 + e)\,v_i$, so each impact passes on the fraction $(1 + e)/2$ of the incoming speed. There are $N - 1$ impacts between ball 1 and ball $N$.

$$v_N = \left(\frac{1 + e}{2}\right)^{N - 1} v_0$$ $$h = \frac{v_N^2}{2g} = \left(\frac{1 + e}{2}\right)^{2(N - 1)} \frac{v_0^2}{2g}$$

For example, with $N = 5$, $e = 0.9$ and $v_0 = 1$ m/s, $v_N = 0.815$ m/s and $h = 33.8$ mm. A real ball 1 would rise to 51.0 mm when released from the same height, so a third of the height is lost on the way. With $e = 1$ every impact passes on the full speed and ball $N$ rises exactly as high as ball 1 started.

Answer

(a) $v_N = \left(\dfrac{1 + e}{2}\right)^{N-1} v_0$. (b) $h = \left(\dfrac{1 + e}{2}\right)^{2(N-1)} \dfrac{v_0^2}{2g}$.
