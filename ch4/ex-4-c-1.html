Example 4.C.1Block pushed by a force pulse

linear impulsefrictionforce–time graph

Problem

Block A has mass $m$ and moves to the right with an initial speed $v_1$ on a rough horizontal surface. The coefficient of kinetic friction is $\mu_k$. While the block slides, a force $F(t)$ pushes it to the right. The force equals $F_0$ from $t = 0$ to $t = \Delta t$ and then decreases linearly to zero at $t = 2\Delta t$.

The data are $m = 8$ kg, $F_0 = 20$ N, $v_1 = 15$ m/s, $\mu_k = 0.15$ and $\Delta t = 0.2$ s.

Find the speed of the block (a) at $t = \Delta t$ and (b) at $t = 2\Delta t$.

Solution

Every solution follows the four-step method: free-body diagrams, kinetics equations, kinematics, solve. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagram

The block is the system. Four forces act on it. The push $F(t)$ acts to the right, the weight $mg$ acts downward, the floor pushes up with the normal force $N$, and kinetic friction $\mu_k N$ acts to the left because the block slides to the right.

The block keeps moving to the right, so kinetic friction $\mu_k N$ points to the left during the whole interval. The grey arrow is the velocity, not a force.

Step 2 · Write the kinetics equations (impulse and momentum)

In the vertical direction the block does not accelerate, so $N = mg$ at every instant. In the horizontal direction, linear impulse and momentum between two times $t_1$ and $t_2$ gives

$$m v(t_2) = m v(t_1) + \int_{t_1}^{t_2} F\,dt - \mu_k m g\,(t_2 - t_1)$$

The impulse of the push is the area under the force–time graph. From $0$ to $\Delta t$ the area is a rectangle, $F_0\Delta t$. From $\Delta t$ to $2\Delta t$ it is a triangle, $\tfrac12 F_0\Delta t$.

Step 3 · Check the kinematics

The friction force is $\mu_k m g = (0.15)(8)(9.81) = 11.77$ N. This force always opposes the motion, so the equation above holds only while the block keeps moving to the right. We check at the end that the speed stays positive.

Step 4 · Solve

(a) From $t = 0$ to $t = \Delta t$:

$$v(\Delta t) = v_1 + \frac{(F_0 - \mu_k m g)\,\Delta t}{m} = 15 + \frac{(20 - 11.77)(0.2)}{8} = 15.21\ \text{m/s}$$

(b) From $t = \Delta t$ to $t = 2\Delta t$:

$$v(2\Delta t) = v(\Delta t) + \frac{\tfrac12 F_0\Delta t - \mu_k m g\,\Delta t}{m} = 15.206 + \frac{2.0 - 2.354}{8} = 15.16\ \text{m/s}$$

During the second interval the average push, $F_0/2 = 10$ N, is smaller than the friction force, so the block slows down slightly. Both speeds are positive, so friction acts to the left throughout, as assumed.

Answer

(a) $v(\Delta t) = 15.21$ m/s. (b) $v(2\Delta t) = 15.16$ m/s.
