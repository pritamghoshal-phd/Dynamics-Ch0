Example 4.B.2Block sliding into a spring on a rough incline

work–energyfrictionsprings

Problem

A block of mass $m = 5$ kg is released from rest on a rough incline of angle $\theta = 53.13°$. The coefficient of kinetic friction between the block and the incline is $\mu_k = 0.3$. The block slides a distance $d = 0.75$ m before it reaches an uncompressed spring of stiffness $k = 1000$ N/m.

Find the maximum deflection of the spring.

Solution

Every solution follows the four-step method: free-body diagrams, kinetics equations, kinematics, solve. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagrams

We isolate the block. While it slides down, it is acted on by its weight $mg$, the normal force $N$ and the kinetic friction force $\mu_k N$, which points up the incline because it opposes the sliding. After contact, the spring also pushes up the incline with the force $k\delta$.

The normal force $N$ is perpendicular to the motion and does no work. Gravity does positive work, friction does negative work over the whole distance $d + \delta$, and the spring force does negative work only after contact. We count the spring through its potential energy.

The block does not accelerate perpendicular to the incline, so $N = mg\cos\theta$ during the whole motion.

Step 2 · Write the work–energy equation

Let state 1 be the release and state 2 the maximum compression $\delta$, where the block is momentarily at rest. We treat gravity and the spring through potential energies and friction as a nonconservative force. The block moves a distance $d + \delta$ along the incline, so it drops a height $(d + \delta)\sin\theta$.

$$T_1 + V_1 + U_{1\to2}^{nc} = T_2 + V_2$$ $$0 + mg(d + \delta)\sin\theta - \mu_k mg\cos\theta\,(d + \delta) = 0 + \tfrac12 k\delta^2$$

Here the gravitational potential energy is measured from the level of state 2.

Step 3 · Write the kinematics

Both end states have zero speed, so no velocity relation is needed. The only geometric fact we use is that the distance slid is $d + \delta$.

Step 4 · Solve

Let $F$ be the net driving force along the incline before contact.

$$F = mg(\sin\theta - \mu_k\cos\theta) = 5(9.81)(0.8 - 0.3 \times 0.6) = 30.41\ \text{N}$$

The energy equation then becomes a quadratic in $\delta$.

$$\tfrac12 k\delta^2 - F\delta - Fd = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad 500\,\delta^2 - 30.41\,\delta - 22.81 = 0$$ $$\delta = \frac{F + \sqrt{F^2 + 2kFd}}{k} = \frac{30.41 + \sqrt{30.41^2 + 2(1000)(30.41)(0.75)}}{1000} = 0.246\ \text{m}$$

As a check, gravity does $39.09$ J of work, friction removes $8.79$ J and the spring stores $\tfrac12(1000)(0.246)^2 = 30.29$ J, and $39.09 - 8.79 = 30.30$ J.

Answer

The maximum deflection of the spring is $\delta_{max} = 0.246$ m (246 mm).
