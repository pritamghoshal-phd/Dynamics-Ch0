Example 4.A.6Block A pulled across block B by a cable and pulley

Newton's lawsystems of particlesfriction

Problem

Block A, of mass $m$, rests on block B, of mass $M$, which rests on a smooth floor. A light pulley is attached to the right side of A. A cable is tied to the upright at the right end of B, passes around the pulley, and its free upper end is pulled to the right with the force $F$. The system is initially at rest.

The data are $F = 60$ N, $m = 20$ kg, $M = 100$ kg and $\mu_k = 0.5$.

Find the accelerations of A and B (a) if the surface between A and B is smooth and (b) if the surface between A and B is rough with coefficient of kinetic friction $\mu_k$, given that A slips on B.

Solution

Every solution follows the four-step method: free-body diagrams, kinetics equations, kinematics, solve. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagrams

We draw separate diagrams for A (with its pulley) and for B. The two strands of the cable both pull the pulley to the right, so A feels $2F$ to the right. The lower strand is tied to the upright of B and pulls B to the left with $F$. Between A and B act the normal force $N$ and, in part (b), the friction force $f$. A slides to the right relative to B, so friction acts to the left on A and to the right on B. The floor is smooth and only pushes B upward.

Two strands of the cable pull on the pulley of A, so A feels $2F$ to the right. The lower strand is tied to the upright of B and pulls B to the left with $F$. The friction force $f$ is zero in part (a). In part (b) A slides to the right relative to B, so $f = \mu_k N$ acts to the left on A and to the right on B. $R_1$ and $R_2$ are the floor forces on the rollers.

Step 2 · Write the kinetics equations

With $x$ to the right, the horizontal equations of A and B and the vertical equation of A are

$$m a_A = 2F - f, \qquad M a_B = -F + f, \qquad N = mg$$

Adding the first two equations gives $m a_A + M a_B = F$. This is the equation of the whole system, on which the only horizontal external force is the pull $F$ on the free end of the cable.

Step 3 · Write the kinematics equations

The blocks move independently because A slides on B, so no kinematic relation connects $a_A$ and $a_B$. The friction force is $f = 0$ in part (a) and $f = \mu_k N = \mu_k m g$ in part (b), because A slips.

Step 4 · Solve

(a) Smooth surface.

$$a_A = \frac{2F}{m} = \frac{120}{20} = 6.00\ \text{m/s}^2, \qquad a_B = -\frac{F}{M} = -\frac{60}{100} = -0.600\ \text{m/s}^2$$

(b) Rough surface. The friction force is $f = 0.5(20)(9.81) = 98.1$ N.

$$a_A = \frac{2F - f}{m} = \frac{120 - 98.1}{20} = 1.10\ \text{m/s}^2, \qquad a_B = \frac{f - F}{M} = \frac{98.1 - 60}{100} = 0.381\ \text{m/s}^2$$

The relative acceleration $a_A - a_B = 0.714$ m/s² is positive, so A does slide to the right relative to B, as assumed. If the blocks moved together, their common acceleration would be $F/(m + M) = 0.5$ m/s², which would need a friction force of 110 N. This is more than the 98.1 N available, which confirms that A slips.

Answer

(a) $a_A = 6.00$ m/s² to the right and $a_B = 0.600$ m/s² to the left. (b) $a_A = 1.10$ m/s² and $a_B = 0.381$ m/s², both to the right.

Explore

  • In the rough case, raise $\mu_k$ until A stops slipping on B. At what value does this happen?
  • Check in both cases that $m a_A + M a_B = F$. Why must this hold whatever the friction between the blocks?
