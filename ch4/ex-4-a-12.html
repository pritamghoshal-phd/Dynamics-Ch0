Example 4.A.12Block leaving a circular hill

path componentsloss of contactwork–energy

Problem

A block of mass $m$ slides with speed $v_0$ along a smooth horizontal surface and then over a smooth circular hill of radius $R$, as shown. The angle $\theta$ is measured from the vertical through the center of the hill.

The book asks for (a) the speed $v_0$ for which the block loses contact with the hill at $\theta = 53.13°$, and (b) the normal force on the block at $\theta = 36.87°$ for that speed. Use $m = 10$ kg and $R = 2.5$ m.

Solution

Every solution follows the four-step method: free-body diagrams, kinetics equations, kinematics, solve. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagrams

Isolate the block at a general angle $\theta$ on the hill. The hill is smooth, so only two forces act, the weight $mg$ and the normal force $N$, which points away from the center of the hill. The block leaves the hill at the instant when $N$ drops to zero.

Left: on the smooth hill only the weight $mg$ and the normal force $N$ act. $\hat{\mathbf e}_t$ points along the path in the direction of motion, and $\hat{\mathbf e}_n$ points toward the center O of the circle (opposite to $N$). Right: at the separation angle the normal force has just dropped to zero.

Step 2 · Write the kinetics equations

Use path components, with $\hat{\mathbf e}_t$ along the motion and $\hat{\mathbf e}_n$ toward the center of the hill. The weight has a component $mg\sin\theta$ along the path and $mg\cos\theta$ toward the center.

$$\sum F_t = m\dot v: \quad mg\sin\theta = m\dot v, \qquad \sum F_n = m\frac{v^2}{R}: \quad mg\cos\theta - N = m\frac{v^2}{R}$$
Step 3 · Write the kinematics equations

Write $\dot v = v\,\dfrac{dv}{ds}$ with $ds = R\,d\theta$. The tangential equation becomes $v\,dv = gR\sin\theta\,d\theta$. Integrating from the top of the hill, where $v = v_0$ and $\theta = 0$,

$$v^2 = v_0^2 + 2gR\,(1 - \cos\theta)$$

This is the same result that the work–energy principle gives.

Step 4 · Solve

Substituting $v^2$ into the normal equation gives the normal force as a function of the angle,

$$N = m\left[g\,(3\cos\theta - 2) - \frac{v_0^2}{R}\right]$$

Setting $N = 0$ gives the speed for separation at a chosen angle $\theta_s$, namely $v_0^2 = gR\,(3\cos\theta_s - 2)$.

(a) The data in the book cannot be met. For $\theta_s = 53.13°$ we have $\cos\theta_s = 0.6$, and

$$v_0^2 = (9.81)(2.5)(1.8 - 2) = -4.91\ \text{m}^2/\text{s}^2$$

A negative value of $v_0^2$ means that no speed works. Even a block that starts almost at rest leaves the hill at $\cos\theta_s = 2/3$, which is $\theta_s = 48.2°$, and a faster block leaves earlier. With the angles as given, the numbers fit if the block leaves at $\theta_s = 36.87°$, which is 53.13° measured from the horizontal. Then

$$v_0^2 = (9.81)(2.5)(3 \times 0.8 - 2) = 9.81\ \text{m}^2/\text{s}^2, \qquad v_0 = 3.13\ \text{m/s}$$

(b) With $v_0 = 3.13$ m/s the block is still on the hill at any angle below 36.87°. For example, at $\theta = 20°$,

$$N = 10\left[9.81\,(3\cos 20° - 2) - \frac{9.81}{2.5}\right] = 10\,(8.04 - 3.92) = 41.1\ \text{N}$$

At $\theta = 36.87°$ itself the normal force is zero, since that is where the block leaves the hill.

Answer

(a) Separation at 53.13° from the vertical is impossible, since every block leaves the hill before 48.2°. For separation at 36.87° from the vertical (53.13° from the horizontal), $v_0 = 3.13$ m/s. (b) For that speed, $N = m[g(3\cos\theta - 2) - v_0^2/R]$, which is 41.1 N at $\theta = 20°$ and falls to zero at 36.87°.
