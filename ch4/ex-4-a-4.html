Example 4.A.4A collar on a rod in an accelerating frame

Newton's lawsmooth rodnormal force

Problem

A collar P of mass $m$ slides freely on a smooth rod that is mounted at an angle $\theta = 36.87°$ in a frame. The rod falls from the upper left to the lower right of the frame. The frame moves along a horizontal surface with a constant acceleration $a$ to the right.

Find the value of $a$ for which the collar does not slide along the rod.

Solution

Every solution follows the four-step method: free-body diagrams, kinetics equations, kinematics, solve. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagram

Two forces act on the collar. The weight $mg$ acts downward. The rod is smooth, so it can push on the collar only perpendicular to itself, with the normal force $N$. Because the rod falls to the right, its normal points up and to the right, at the angle $\theta$ from the vertical. If P does not slide, it moves with the frame and its acceleration is $a$ to the right.

The rod is smooth, so the only force from the rod is the normal force $N$, perpendicular to the rod. When P does not slide, it has the same acceleration $a$ as the frame, to the right.

Step 2 · Write the kinetics equations

With $x$ to the right and $y$ upward, Newton's second law gives

$$\sum F_x = N\sin\theta = m a, \qquad \sum F_y = N\cos\theta - mg = 0$$
Step 3 · Write the kinematics equations

The collar does not slide along the rod, so its acceleration equals the acceleration of the frame, $\mathbf a_P = a\,\hat{\imath}$.

Step 4 · Solve

The second equation gives $N = mg/\cos\theta$. Substituting it into the first gives

$$a = g\tan\theta = 9.81 \times 0.75 = 7.36\ \text{m/s}^2$$

If the frame accelerates less, the collar slides down the rod. If it accelerates more, the collar slides up the rod. Relative to the frame, the collar accelerates along the rod at $g\sin\theta - a\cos\theta$, which is zero only for $a = g\tan\theta$.

Answer

$a = g\tan\theta = 7.36$ m/s² to the right.
