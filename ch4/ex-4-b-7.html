Example 4.B.7Bar launched by two springs

work–energyspringssystems of particles

Problem

Particles A and B, with masses $m$ and $2m$, are connected by a rigid bar AB of negligible mass. The bar is pinned to the ground at O, with $OA = 2L$ and $OB = L$. The system is released from rest in position 1, where $\theta = \theta_1$ and particle A rests on a platform supported by two identical springs. Each spring has stiffness $k$, and the springs are unstretched when $\theta = 0$. The particles are small.

Use $\theta_1 = 36.87°$, $L = 0.1$ m, $m = 10$ kg and $k = 100$ N/m.

Find the speeds of A and B in position 2, where A is directly above O.

InteractiveThe springs launch the bar over the top

The springs push A up only while they are compressed. At $\theta = 0$ the platform stops and A leaves it. Because $m(2L) = 2m(L)$, the bar is balanced about O and gravity does no net work. The animation stops when A is directly above O.

Solution

Every solution follows the four-step method: free-body diagrams, kinetics equations, kinematics, solve. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagram

We isolate the bar with both particles. The forces are the weights $mg$ and $2mg$, the pin reactions at O, and the upward push $2k\delta$ of the spring platform on A while the springs are compressed.

The two springs push up on A through the platform with a total force $2k\delta$, where $\delta$ is their compression. This force acts only until A leaves the platform at $\theta = 0$. The pin reactions at O do no work.

Step 2 · Write the work–energy equation

Gravity and the springs are conservative, and the pin reactions do no work. Energy is therefore conserved from position 1 to position 2.

$$T_1 + V_{g1} + V_{s1} = T_2 + V_{g2} + V_{s2}$$

The system starts from rest, so $T_1 = 0$. In position 2 the springs have returned to their unstretched length, so $V_{s2} = 0$.

Step 3 · Work out the geometry

In position 1, A is $2L\sin\theta_1 = 0.12$ m below O, so each spring is compressed by $\delta_1 = 0.12$ m. The stored energy in the two springs is

$$V_{s1} = 2 \cdot \tfrac12 k\delta_1^2 = 100(0.12)^2 = 1.44\ \text{J}$$

From position 1 to position 2, A rises from 0.12 m below O to $2L = 0.2$ m above O, a rise of 0.32 m. B moves from 0.06 m above O to $L = 0.1$ m below O, a drop of 0.16 m. The change in gravitational potential energy is

$$V_{g2} - V_{g1} = mg(0.32) - 2mg(0.16) = 0$$
Step 4 · Write the kinematics and solve

Both particles turn about O with the same angular speed $\omega$, so $v_A = 2L\omega$ and $v_B = L\omega$.

$$T_2 = \tfrac12 m(2L\omega)^2 + \tfrac12(2m)(L\omega)^2 = 3mL^2\omega^2 = 0.3\,\omega^2$$

Setting $T_2 = V_{s1} = 1.44$ J gives $\omega = \sqrt{1.44/0.3} = 2.19$ rad/s. Therefore

$$v_A = 2L\omega = 0.438\ \text{m/s}, \qquad v_B = L\omega = 0.219\ \text{m/s}$$

The figure in the book labels B with $3m$, while the text gives $2m$. With $2m$ the bar is balanced about O, which is clearly intended. With $3m$, gravity would add $15.7$ J and the speeds would be much larger.

Answer

$v_A = 0.438$ m/s and $v_B = 0.219$ m/s in position 2.
