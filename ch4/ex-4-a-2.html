Example 4.A.2A rope sliding off a rough table

Newton's lawvariable forcea = v dv/dx

Problem

A rope of length $L$ and mass per unit length $\rho$ is initially at rest on a rough horizontal surface, with coefficient of kinetic friction $\mu_k$. A part of length $d$ hangs over a light pulley C at the right end of the surface. The rope remains taut.

Use $d = 0.2L$.

Find the speed of the rope when its left end reaches the pulley.

Solution

Every solution follows the four-step method: free-body diagrams, kinetics equations, kinematics, solve. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagrams

Let $x$ be the length of rope hanging below the pulley at time $t$. We cut the rope at the pulley and draw two free-body diagrams. The part on the table, of length $L - x$, has weight $\rho(L - x)g$, a normal force $N$, a friction force $\mu_k N$ opposite to the sliding, and the tension $T$ at the pulley. The hanging part, of length $x$, has weight $\rho x g$ and the tension $T$. The pulley is light and turns freely, so the tension is the same on both sides.

$x$ is the length of rope hanging below the pulley. The pulley is light and ideal, so it passes the same tension $T$ from one part to the other. Friction acts to the left, opposite to the sliding.

Step 2 · Write the kinetics equations

Every point of a taut rope moves with the same speed $v$ and the same rate of change of speed $a$. For the part on the table (horizontal, positive toward the pulley) and the hanging part (vertical, positive downward),

$$\rho(L - x)\,a = T - \mu_k N, \qquad N = \rho (L - x) g$$ $$\rho x\,a = \rho x g - T$$

Adding the first and the last equations eliminates the tension.

$$\rho L\,a = \rho g x - \mu_k \rho g (L - x)$$ $$a = \frac{g}{L}\left[(1 + \mu_k)x - \mu_k L\right]$$
Step 3 · Write the kinematics equations

The acceleration depends on the position $x$, not on time. The chain rule gives $a = \dfrac{dv}{dt} = v\,\dfrac{dv}{dx}$, so we can separate the variables.

$$v\,dv = a(x)\,dx, \qquad \int_0^{v} v\,dv = \int_d^{L} a(x)\,dx$$
Step 4 · Solve $$\frac{v^2}{2} = \frac{g}{L}\left[\frac{(1 + \mu_k)(L^2 - d^2)}{2} - \mu_k L (L - d)\right]$$

With $d = 0.2L$ the bracket becomes

$$L^2\left[0.48(1 + \mu_k) - 0.8\mu_k\right] = L^2(0.48 - 0.32\mu_k)$$

so the speed is

$$v = \sqrt{gL\,(0.96 - 0.64\,\mu_k)}$$

The rope starts to move only if $a > 0$ at $x = d$, which requires $\mu_k < d/(L - d) = 0.25$. For example, with $L = 2$ m and $\mu_k = 0.2$ the speed is $v = 4.04$ m/s.

Answer

$v = \sqrt{gL\,(0.96 - 0.64\,\mu_k)}$ when the left end of the rope reaches the pulley.

Explore

  • Set $\mu_k = 0$. Check that the speed is $\sqrt{0.96\,gL}$ and that the curve on the graph becomes $v^2 = g(x^2 - d^2)/L$.
  • Increase $\mu_k$ toward 0.25. Why does the rope barely start, and what happens above 0.25?
