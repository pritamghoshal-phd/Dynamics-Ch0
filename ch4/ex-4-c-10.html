Example 4.C.10Impact that leaves block B at rest

central impactrestitutionmomentum

Problem

Blocks A and B, of masses $m_A$ and $m_B$, slide on a smooth horizontal surface. A moves to the right with speed $v_{A1}$ and B moves to the left with speed $v_{B1}$. A strikes B, and the coefficient of restitution for the impact is $e$. As a result of the impact, block B comes to rest.

The data are $m_A = 2$ kg, $m_B = 3$ kg, $v_{A1} = 1.5$ m/s and $e = 0.4$.

Find the speed $v_{B1}$ of block B before the impact.

Solution

Every solution follows the four-step method: free-body diagrams, kinetics equations, kinematics, solve. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagrams

For A and B together, the contact force at the impact is internal. The weights and the normal forces are vertical, and the surface is smooth, so no horizontal external force acts. For B alone, the contact force $F$ from A acts to the right. It is this force that brings B to rest.

Left: for A and B together the contact force at the impact is internal, and the remaining external forces are vertical. The horizontal momentum of the pair is therefore conserved. Right: on B alone the contact force $F$ from A is large and horizontal. It is the force that stops B.

Step 2 · Write the kinetics equations

Take the right as positive. Then the velocity of B before the impact is $-v_{B1}$, and B's velocity after the impact is $v_{B2} = 0$. Conservation of the horizontal momentum of A and B gives

$$m_A v_{A1} - m_B v_{B1} = m_A v_{A2} + m_B (0)$$

The coefficient of restitution relates the speeds of separation and approach along the line of impact.

$$v_{B2} - v_{A2} = -e\left[(-v_{B1}) - v_{A1}\right] \quad\Longrightarrow\quad -v_{A2} = e\left(v_{B1} + v_{A1}\right)$$
Step 3 · Write the kinematics equations

The impact is central and the blocks move along one line, so no further kinematics is needed. The condition that B stops is $v_{B2} = 0$, which we have already used.

Step 4 · Solve

Substitute $v_{A2} = -e\left(v_{B1} + v_{A1}\right)$ into the momentum equation.

$$m_A v_{A1} - m_B v_{B1} = -e\,m_A\left(v_{B1} + v_{A1}\right) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad v_{B1} = \frac{(1 + e)\,m_A\,v_{A1}}{m_B - e\,m_A}$$ $$v_{B1} = \frac{(1.4)(2)(1.5)}{3 - (0.4)(2)} = \frac{4.2}{2.2} = 1.91\ \text{m/s}$$

Then $v_{A2} = -0.4\,(1.909 + 1.5) = -1.36$ m/s, so A bounces back to the left.

The result needs $m_B > e\,m_A$. If B is too light, no initial speed can bring it to rest.

Answer

$v_{B1} = 1.91$ m/s to the left. After the impact A moves to the left at 1.36 m/s.
