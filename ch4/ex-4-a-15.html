Example 4.A.15Conical pendulum

path componentscircular motiontension

Problem

Particle P, of mass $m$, is attached to the fixed support O by a cable of length $L$. P moves with speed $v$ on a horizontal circle of radius $r$, so the cable sweeps out a cone.

Find the angle $\beta$ between the cable and the vertical. Also find the tension in the cable and the depth $h$ of the circle below O.

Solution

Every solution follows the four-step method: free-body diagrams, kinetics equations, kinematics, solve. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagram

Isolate P. Only two forces act, the cable tension $T$ along the cable toward O and the weight $mg$. The center of the circle lies on the vertical line through O, a distance $h$ below O.

Only the cable tension $T$, along the cable toward O, and the weight $mg$ act on P. The center of the circular path is on the vertical line through O, so $\hat{\mathbf e}_n$ points horizontally from P toward that line.

Step 2 · Write the kinetics equations

Use path components with $\hat{\mathbf e}_n$ horizontal, from P toward the center of the circle, and $\hat{\mathbf k}$ vertical. The tension has a horizontal component $T\sin\beta$ and a vertical component $T\cos\beta$.

$$\sum F_n = m\frac{v^2}{r}: \quad T\sin\beta = m\frac{v^2}{r}, \qquad \sum F_z = 0: \quad T\cos\beta - mg = 0$$
Step 3 · Write the kinematics equations

P moves at constant speed on a horizontal circle, so $\dot v = 0$, $a_n = v^2/r$ and $a_z = 0$. The geometry of the cone gives $r = L\sin\beta$ and $h = L\cos\beta$.

Step 4 · Solve

Dividing the two kinetics equations eliminates $T$,

$$\tan\beta = \frac{v^2}{g r}, \qquad T = \frac{mg}{\cos\beta}, \qquad h = L\cos\beta = \frac{r}{\tan\beta} = \frac{g r^2}{v^2}$$

The radius, the speed and the cable length are not independent. Since $r = L\sin\beta$, the speed must satisfy $v^2 = gL\sin\beta\tan\beta$. For example, with $L = 1$ m and $\beta = 30°$, the radius is $r = 0.5$ m, the speed is $v = \sqrt{(9.81)(0.5)\tan 30°} = 1.68$ m/s, and a 0.5 kg particle pulls on the cable with $T = 0.5(9.81)/\cos 30° = 5.66$ N.

Answer

$\tan\beta = \dfrac{v^2}{g r}$, $T = \dfrac{mg}{\cos\beta}$ and $h = \dfrac{g r^2}{v^2}$.

Explore

  • Keep the speed fixed and lengthen the cable. Does the angle grow or shrink?
  • Show that the time for one turn is $2\pi\sqrt{h/g}$, so it depends only on the depth of the circle below O.
