Example 4.D.2Two particles released on a spinning arm

angular momentumconservationenergy loss

Problem

An assembly turns about a vertical shaft with angular speed $\omega_0$. Two particles, each of mass $m$, are held fixed relative to the horizontal arm at a distance $r$ on either side of the axis of rotation. The particles are then released, and they slide outward on the smooth arm until they reach stops at a distance $2r$. The mass of the arm and shaft is negligible, and the bearing is frictionless.

Find the angular speed of the arm at this new position.

Solution

Every solution follows the four-step method: free-body diagrams, kinetics equations, kinematics, solve. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagram

Cut the whole assembly, including both particles, free at its bearing. The external forces are the two weights $mg$, the bearing force with components $B_x$ and $B_z$, and the bearing couple $M_B$ about a horizontal axis. The weights are parallel to the axis of rotation, the bearing force acts on the axis, and the couple $M_B$ has no component along the axis. The forces between the particles, the arm and the stops are internal.

The assembly is cut free at its bearing. The bearing force and the couple $M_B$ (about a horizontal axis) act on the axis or about a horizontal line, and the weights are parallel to the axis. None of them has a moment about the vertical axis of rotation.

Step 2 · Write the kinetics equation (angular impulse and momentum)

The external forces have no moment about the vertical axis, so the angular momentum of the system about this axis is conserved between the two positions. The arm has negligible mass, so only the particles contribute. Each one contributes $m R\,v_\theta$, where $v_\theta$ is its velocity component perpendicular to the arm.

$$2\,m\,r\,v_{\theta 1} = 2\,m\,(2r)\,v_{\theta 2}$$
Step 3 · Write the kinematics equations

Each particle stays on the arm, so $v_\theta = R\,\omega$. In position 1, $v_{\theta 1} = r\,\omega_0$. In position 2 the particles rest against the stops, so they have no radial velocity and $v_{\theta 2} = 2r\,\omega_2$.

Step 4 · Solve $$2 m r\,(r\omega_0) = 2 m (2r)\,(2r\,\omega_2) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \omega_2 = \frac{\omega_0}{4}$$

Energy is not conserved here. While the particles slide, no force does work on them, and their kinetic energy stays at $m r^2\omega_0^2$. When they strike the stops, their radial velocity (three quarters of the kinetic energy) is lost. Afterward the kinetic energy is $\tfrac12(2m)(2r\,\omega_0/4)^2 = \tfrac14\,m r^2\omega_0^2$.

Answer

$\omega_2 = \omega_0/4$. For example, an arm turning at 10 rad/s slows to 2.5 rad/s.
