Example 4.C.8Bullet fired into a hanging block

plastic impactballistic pendulumwork–energy

Problem

Block A, of weight $W$, hangs at rest from a cord of length $L$ attached to the fixed point O. Bullet B, of weight $w$, strikes the block horizontally with speed $v_{B1}$ and stays embedded in it.

The data are $w = 0.2$ lb, $W = 75$ lb, $L = 5$ ft and $v_{B1} = 1800$ ft/s.

Find (a) the largest angle $\theta$ that the cord makes with the vertical after the impact and (b) the energy lost during the impact.

Solution

Every solution follows the four-step method: free-body diagrams, kinetics equations, kinematics, solve. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagrams

The motion has two stages, and each stage needs its own diagram. During the impact we take A and B together as the system. The contact force between the bullet and the block is then internal. The external forces are the cord tension and the two weights, and all of them are vertical while the cord hangs straight down.

After the impact, A and B move together as one particle on a circle of radius $L$. The forces on it are the tension $T$ along the cord and the total weight $W + w$.

Left: during the very short impact the only forces are the cord tension $T$ and the weights, and all of them are vertical. No horizontal impulse acts on the system of A and B, so its horizontal momentum is conserved. Right: after the impact the tension is always perpendicular to the path, so it does no work, and only gravity does work on A and B.

Step 2 · Write the kinetics equations

Impact. No horizontal force acts on the system of A and B, so its horizontal momentum is conserved from just before to just after the impact. The bullet stays in the block, so both move with the same speed $v_2$ afterward.

$$m_B\,v_{B1} = \left(m_A + m_B\right) v_2$$

Swing. Use the work–energy principle from just after the impact (position 2) to the highest position (position 3), where the speed is zero. The tension is perpendicular to the path and does no work, so only gravity does work.

$$\tfrac12\left(m_A + m_B\right) v_2^2 = \left(W + w\right) h, \qquad h = L\left(1 - \cos\theta_{max}\right)$$
Step 3 · Write the kinematics equations

A and B move together after the impact, so they share the speed $v_2$. The rise of the block is related to the cord angle by $h = L(1 - \cos\theta)$, because the block stays a distance $L$ from O.

Step 4 · Solve

With $m = W/g$, the masses are $m_B = 0.2/32.2 = 0.006211$ slug and $m_A = 75/32.2 = 2.329$ slug. The speed just after the impact is

$$v_2 = \frac{w}{w + W}\,v_{B1} = \frac{0.2}{75.2}\,(1800) = 4.787\ \text{ft/s}$$

The height and the angle follow from the work–energy equation.

$$h = \frac{v_2^2}{2g} = \frac{(4.787)^2}{2(32.2)} = 0.3559\ \text{ft}, \qquad \cos\theta_{max} = 1 - \frac{0.3559}{5} = 0.9288, \qquad \theta_{max} = 21.7°$$

The kinetic energy just before the impact belongs to the bullet alone, and just after the impact it belongs to A and B together.

$$T_1 = \tfrac12\,(0.006211)(1800)^2 = 10\,062\ \text{ft·lb}, \qquad T_2 = \tfrac12\,(2.335)(4.787)^2 = 26.8\ \text{ft·lb}$$ $$\Delta E = T_1 - T_2 = 10\,035\ \text{ft·lb}$$

Only $T_2/T_1 = w/(w + W) = 0.27\%$ of the bullet's energy survives the impact. The rest goes into deforming the block and the bullet and into heat. This is why energy cannot be used across the impact, while momentum can.

Answer

(a) $\theta_{max} = 21.7°$. (b) About 10 040 ft·lb of kinetic energy, 99.7% of the bullet's energy, is lost in the impact.

Explore

  • Double the bullet weight. Does the angle double? Explain the result using $h = v_2^2/(2g)$.
  • This setup, called a ballistic pendulum, was once used to measure bullet speeds. Measure $\theta_{max}$ in the animation and work backward to $v_{B1}$.
