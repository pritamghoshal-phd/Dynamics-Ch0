Example 4.B.6Three particles linked by bars, pulleys and a cable

work–energysystems of particlespulleys

Problem

The system is made up of particles A, B and E with masses $m_A = 5$ kg, $m_B = 20$ kg and $m_E = 80$ kg. Light bars OA (length $2L$) and OB (length $L$) are welded together at a right angle and welded to a pulley of radius $r$ at O. A cable wrapped on this pulley passes over the top of a second pulley at C and carries particle E. Both pulleys turn on fixed pins and have negligible mass. The angle $\theta$ of bar OA is measured from the downward vertical, and the system is released from rest when $\theta = 0$, with OA hanging straight down and OB horizontal.

Use $r = 0.1$ m and $L = 0.5$ m.

Find the speed of particle E when $\theta = 90°$.

Solution

Every solution follows the four-step method: free-body diagrams, kinetics equations, kinematics, solve. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagrams

We take the whole system, including both pulleys, the bars, the cable and the three particles. The external forces are the three weights and the pin reactions at C and O.

In the system diagram the cable tension is internal. The pin reactions at C and O act at fixed points and do no work. Only the three weights do work. The diagram of E alone shows the tension $T$, which we would need only if we wanted the cable force.

Step 2 · Write the work–energy equation

The system starts from rest, so $T_1 = 0$, and only the weights do work.

$$U_{1\to2} = T_2 = \tfrac12 m_A v_A^2 + \tfrac12 m_B v_B^2 + \tfrac12 m_E v_E^2$$
Step 3 · Write the kinematics

The bars and pulley O turn together with angular speed $\omega$. The cable leaves pulley O at radius $r$, so $v_E = r\omega$ and E moves down a distance $r\theta$. The particles on the bars move on circles, so $v_A = 2L\omega$ and $v_B = L\omega$. In terms of $v_E$,

$$v_A = \frac{2L}{r}\,v_E = 10\,v_E, \qquad v_B = \frac{L}{r}\,v_E = 5\,v_E$$

Between $\theta = 0$ and $\theta = 90°$, E drops $r\pi/2 = 0.1571$ m. Bar OA swings from straight down to horizontal, so A rises $2L = 1.0$ m. Bar OB swings from horizontal to straight down, so B drops $L = 0.5$ m.

Step 4 · Solve $$U_{1\to2} = 80(9.81)(0.1571) + 20(9.81)(0.5) - 5(9.81)(1.0) = 123.3 + 98.1 - 49.1 = 172.3\ \text{J}$$ $$T_2 = \tfrac12\left[80 + 20(5)^2 + 5(10)^2\right] v_E^2 = 540\,v_E^2$$

Setting $T_2 = U_{1\to2}$ gives $v_E = \sqrt{172.3/540} = 0.565$ m/s. The bars then turn at $\omega = v_E/r = 5.65$ rad/s, so $v_A = 5.65$ m/s and $v_B = 2.82$ m/s.

Even though E is by far the heaviest particle, most of the kinetic energy belongs to A and B, because they move ten and five times as fast as E.

Answer

$v_E = 0.565$ m/s downward when $\theta = 90°$.

Explore

  • Make the pulley radius $r$ larger. Does E end up faster or slower at $\theta = 90°$? Explain with the kinematics.
  • Remove particle B (set $m_B = 0$). Does the system still reach $\theta = 90°$?
  • Compare the kinetic energy of E with that of A at $\theta = 90°$.
