BWork and energy

Take the tangential component of Newton's law, $\sum F_t = m\,v\,dv/ds$, and integrate along the path from position 1 to position 2:

$$U_{1\to2} = \int_1^2 \mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r = \tfrac12 m v_2^2 - \tfrac12 m v_1^2 = T_2 - T_1$$

$T = \tfrac12 mv^2$ is the kinetic energy and $U_{1\to2}$ is the work done by the forces. Only the component of a force along the path does work. A force perpendicular to the motion (a normal force on a fixed surface, a cord holding a particle on a circle) does none. Work is a scalar, so this equation trades vector bookkeeping for one scalar equation, and no time appears in it.

ConceptWhich forces do work?

The block is pulled 2 m along a rough floor. Only the horizontal part of F does positive work; friction does negative work; the weight and the normal force are perpendicular to the motion and do none. The total work always equals the kinetic energy ½mv² gained.

Conservative forces and potential energy

Some forces do work that depends only on where the particle starts and ends, not on the path between. For these conservative forces we define a potential energy $V$ so that $U_{1\to2} = V_1 - V_2$:

  • Gravity (near the earth): $V_g = mgh$, with $h$ measured upward from any datum you choose.
  • A linear spring of stiffness $k$: $V_s = \tfrac12 k\,\delta^2$, where $\delta$ is the stretch or compression from the unstretched length.
ConceptThe work done by gravity does not depend on the path

Three frictionless beads drop the same height h along different tracks. Each arrives at the bottom with the same speed √(2gh), because gravity did the same work on each. They arrive at different times: energy says nothing about time.

The work–energy equation

Splitting the forces into conservative ones (written as potential energy) and everything else (friction, applied pushes, motors) gives the working form:

$$T_1 + V_1 + U^{nc}_{1\to2} = T_2 + V_2$$

If the non-conservative forces do no work, $T + V$ is constant: mechanical energy is conserved. Kinetic friction always opposes sliding, so the work it does is always negative: it drains mechanical energy into heat.

Systems of particles

For a system, add the work–energy equations of all the particles. External forces do work as before. Internal forces need a moment's thought: a pair of equal and opposite forces does zero net work if the two points they act on move the same amount along the line joining them, as for a rigid massless link or an inextensible cord. A spring between two particles is internal but stores energy, so include it as $V_s$. Friction between two sliding parts loses energy even when it is internal.

Energy in the wild

Regenerative braking in electric cars and e-bikes recovers kinetic energy that ordinary brakes would turn into heat. In the language of this section, it replaces part of the negative non-conservative work $U^{nc}$ with energy stored in the battery, a kind of potential energy you can spend later.
