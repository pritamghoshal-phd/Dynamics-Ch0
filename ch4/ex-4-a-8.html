Example 4.A.8Two blocks joined by a light rod in perpendicular guides

Newton's lawconstraintstwo-force member

Problem

Blocks A and B, with masses 10 kg and 5 kg, move along smooth vertical and horizontal guides. They are connected by a light rod of length $L = 2.5$ m. A force $F = 50$ N acts to the right on block B. At the position where $s_A = 1.5$ m, block A moves downward with a speed of 4 m/s. Here $s_A$ is the height of A above the horizontal guide and $s_B$ is the horizontal distance from B to the vertical guide.

Find (a) the accelerations of A and B at this instant and (b) the force in rod AB at this instant.

Solution

Every solution follows the four-step method: free-body diagrams, kinetics equations, kinematics, solve. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagrams

The rod is light and pinned at its ends, so it is a two-force member. It acts on A and on B along its own length with the same force $F_{AB}$, which we take as positive in tension. Block A also feels its weight $m_A g$ and the normal force $N_A$ of its vertical guide. Block B feels the force $F$, its weight $m_B g$ and the normal force $N_B$ of its horizontal guide. The rod makes the angle $\phi$ with the horizontal, with $\cos\phi = s_B/L$ and $\sin\phi = s_A/L$.

The rod is light and pinned at both ends, so it is a two-force member: it pulls on A and on B along its own length with the same force $F_{AB}$ (tension positive). The guides are smooth, so they push only perpendicular to themselves. The rod makes the angle $\phi$ with the horizontal, with $\cos\phi = s_B/L = 0.8$ and $\sin\phi = s_A/L = 0.6$.

Step 2 · Write the kinetics equations

Only the directions along the guides are needed. Let $s_A$ increase upward and $s_B$ increase to the left, away from the vertical guide. In tension the rod pulls A down and to the left, and it pulls B up and to the right.

$$m_A\ddot s_A = -m_A g - F_{AB}\sin\phi, \qquad -m_B\ddot s_B = F + F_{AB}\cos\phi$$

The second equation is written for the rightward acceleration of B, which is $-\ddot s_B$.

Step 3 · Write the kinematics equations

The rod keeps the distance between the blocks fixed, so $s_A^2 + s_B^2 = L^2$. At the instant, $s_B = \sqrt{2.5^2 - 1.5^2} = 2.0$ m. Differentiating twice gives

$$s_A\dot s_A + s_B\dot s_B = 0, \qquad s_A\ddot s_A + s_B\ddot s_B + \dot s_A^2 + \dot s_B^2 = 0$$

With $\dot s_A = -4$ m/s, the first equation gives $\dot s_B = -s_A\dot s_A/s_B = 3$ m/s, so B moves to the left at 3 m/s.

Step 4 · Solve

Substituting the numbers, with $\sin\phi = 0.6$ and $\cos\phi = 0.8$, gives three linear equations.

$$10\,\ddot s_A = -98.1 - 0.6F_{AB}, \qquad -5\,\ddot s_B = 50 + 0.8F_{AB}, \qquad 1.5\,\ddot s_A + 2.0\,\ddot s_B = -(16 + 9) = -25$$

Writing $\ddot s_A = -9.81 - 0.06F_{AB}$ and $\ddot s_B = -10 - 0.16F_{AB}$ and substituting into the third equation gives $-34.715 - 0.41F_{AB} = -25$, so

$$F_{AB} = -23.7\ \text{N}, \qquad \ddot s_A = -8.39\ \text{m/s}^2, \qquad \ddot s_B = -6.21\ \text{m/s}^2$$

The negative rod force means that the rod is in compression. A accelerates downward at 8.39 m/s², and B accelerates to the right at 6.21 m/s² while it still moves to the left, so B is slowing down.

Answer

(a) $a_A = 8.39$ m/s² downward and $a_B = 6.21$ m/s² to the right. (b) The rod carries a compressive force of 23.7 N.
