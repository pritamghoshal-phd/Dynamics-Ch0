Example 4.B.5Slider on a curved rod pulled by a cord

work–energyvariable directionsmooth guide

Problem

Slider P, of mass $m = 0.6$ kg, moves freely along a fixed, smooth, curved rod in the vertical plane. A cord attached to P passes over a small guide at the top of a post and carries a constant tension $F = 20$ N. Position 1 is the bottom of the left end of the rod. Position 2 is the top of the rod, 1.2 m to the right of position 1 and $0.3 + 0.4 = 0.7$ m higher. The tip of the guide is 0.3 m to the right of position 2 and 0.3 m above position 1. The slider is released from rest at position 1.

Find the speed of the slider at position 2.

Solution

Every solution follows the four-step method: free-body diagrams, kinetics equations, kinematics, solve. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagram

We isolate slider P. Three forces act on it. They are the weight $mg$, the cord tension $F$ directed from P toward the guide tip, and the force $N$ of the smooth rod.

The rod is smooth, so the force $N$ that it exerts is perpendicular to the rod and does no work. The cord tension $F$ has a constant magnitude but a changing direction, and the weight $mg$ is constant. Both do work.

Step 2 · Write the work–energy equation

The slider starts from rest, so $T_1 = 0$. The rod force does no work, so

$$U_{1\to2}^{F} + U_{1\to2}^{g} = \tfrac12 m v_2^2$$
Step 3 · Find the work of the cord force

The cord tension has a constant magnitude $F$ and always points from P toward the fixed guide tip. For such a force, the work equals $F$ times the decrease in the distance from P to the tip.

$$U_{1\to2}^{F} = F\,(\ell_1 - \ell_2)$$

Taking position 1 as the origin, the tip is at $(1.5,\ 0.3)$ m and position 2 is at $(1.2,\ 0.7)$ m.

$$\ell_1 = \sqrt{1.5^2 + 0.3^2} = 1.530\ \text{m}, \qquad \ell_2 = \sqrt{0.3^2 + 0.4^2} = 0.500\ \text{m}$$ $$U_{1\to2}^{F} = 20(1.530 - 0.500) = 20.59\ \text{J}$$

The slider rises 0.7 m, so gravity does negative work.

$$U_{1\to2}^{g} = -mg(0.7) = -0.6(9.81)(0.7) = -4.12\ \text{J}$$
Step 4 · Solve $$\tfrac12(0.6)\,v_2^2 = 20.59 - 4.12 = 16.47\ \text{J} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad v_2 = \sqrt{\frac{2(16.47)}{0.6}} = 7.41\ \text{m/s}$$

The shape of the rod does not affect the answer, because the rod force does no work and the works of $F$ and $mg$ depend only on the end positions. The animation uses a circular arc through position 1, position 2 and the right foot, and it integrates the equation of motion along that arc. The shape does matter for whether the slider can start. If the rod were vertical at position 1, the upward pull of the cord there ($20 \times 0.30/1.53 = 3.9$ N) would be smaller than the weight ($5.9$ N), and the slider would stay at the bottom. On the arc, the rod starts at about 60° from the horizontal, and the pull along the rod wins.

Answer

$v_2 = 7.41$ m/s at position 2.
