Example 4.C.7Two cars collide at an intersection

conservation of momentum2D impactplastic impact

Problem

Cars A and B, weighing $W_A$ and $W_B$, collide at an intersection. Car A travels north (the $+y$ direction) with speed $v_{A1}$, and car B travels west (the $-x$ direction) with speed $v_{B1}$. After a short collision the cars stick together.

The data are $W_A = 3000$ lb, $W_B = 4000$ lb, $v_{A1} = 40$ mph and $v_{B1} = 25$ mph.

Find the $x$ and $y$ components of the velocity of the cars just after the collision.

The momentum diagram adds the momentum of A (north) to the momentum of B (west). The sum is the momentum of the wreck, so the wreck moves along that direction.

Solution

Every solution follows the four-step method: free-body diagrams, kinetics equations, kinematics, solve. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagram

Take cars A and B together as the system and look at it from above. During the crash the contact forces between the cars are very large, but they are equal and opposite, so they cancel for the system. Road friction is finite and acts only for a very short time, so its impulse is neglected. No horizontal impulse acts on the system from outside.

Seen from above, the only large horizontal forces during the crash are the contact forces $P$ between the cars. They are equal and opposite, so they cancel for the system A + B. Road friction is finite and acts for a very short time, so its impulse is neglected.

Step 2 · Write the kinetics equations (impulse and momentum)

Linear momentum is conserved in both horizontal directions. Because every term contains $1/g$, we may use weights in place of masses:

$$x:\quad W_A(0) + W_B\left(-v_{B1}\right) = \left(W_A + W_B\right)v_x$$ $$y:\quad W_A\,v_{A1} + W_B(0) = \left(W_A + W_B\right)v_y$$
Step 3 · Write the kinematics

The cars stick together, so after the crash they share one velocity $v_x\,\hat{\mathbf i} + v_y\,\hat{\mathbf j}$.

Step 4 · Solve $$v_x = -\frac{(4000)(25)}{7000} = -14.29\ \text{mph} = -20.95\ \text{ft/s}$$ $$v_y = \frac{(3000)(40)}{7000} = 17.14\ \text{mph} = 25.14\ \text{ft/s}$$

The wreck moves at 22.3 mph, at $50.2°$ north of west. About 52 % of the kinetic energy is lost in the crash.

Answer

$v_x = -14.29$ mph $= -20.95$ ft/s and $v_y = 17.14$ mph $= 25.14$ ft/s.

Explore

  • Make the two momenta equal. In which direction does the wreck move?
  • Accident investigators measure the direction of the skid marks after a crash. If they also know the weights, which speeds can they work out?
  • Change the speeds and watch the energy lost. When is the loss smallest?
