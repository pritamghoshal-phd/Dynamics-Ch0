Example 4.B.3Maximum height of a particle launched upward

work–energyconservation of energygravity

Problem

A particle is launched vertically upward with a speed $v_0$. Air resistance on the particle is negligible.

Find the maximum height $h$ reached by the particle.

Solution

Every solution follows the four-step method: free-body diagrams, kinetics equations, kinematics, solve. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagram

Once the particle has left the launcher, the only force acting on it is its weight $mg$.

The weight is the only force on the particle, and it is conservative. Mechanical energy is therefore conserved during the flight.

Step 2 · Write the work–energy equation

Let state 1 be the launch and state 2 the highest point. The weight is conservative and no other force acts, so mechanical energy is conserved. We measure the potential energy from the launch point.

$$T_1 + V_1 = T_2 + V_2 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \tfrac12 m v_0^2 + 0 = T_2 + mgh$$
Step 3 · Write the kinematics

At the highest point the particle is momentarily at rest, so $v_2 = 0$ and $T_2 = 0$.

Step 4 · Solve $$\tfrac12 m v_0^2 = mgh \quad\Longrightarrow\quad h = \frac{v_0^2}{2g}$$

The mass cancels, so every particle launched with the same speed reaches the same height. For example, $v_0 = 10$ m/s gives $h = 100/19.62 = 5.10$ m.

The same result follows from constant-acceleration kinematics, $v^2 = v_0^2 - 2gh$ with $v = 0$. The energy method gets there without ever finding the time of flight.

Answer

$h = \dfrac{v_0^2}{2g}$, which is independent of the mass of the particle.
