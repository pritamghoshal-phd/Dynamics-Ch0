Example 4.B.4Choosing the stiffness of a bumper spring

work–energyspringsdesign

Problem

Block C, of weight $mg = 3200$ lb, is moving with a speed $v_0 = 10$ ft/s toward a fixed wall. A spring of stiffness $k$ is attached to the front of the block. The spring contacts the wall, and the block comes to a stop when the spring compression reaches $\Delta_{max} = 6$ in.

Find the stiffness $k$ of the spring.

Solution

Every solution follows the four-step method: free-body diagrams, kinetics equations, kinematics, solve. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagram

We isolate the block together with its spring. The horizontal force on this system is the push of the wall on the end of the spring. The weight and the floor forces are vertical.

The weight and the wheel forces $N_1$ and $N_2$ are vertical while the car moves horizontally, so they do no work. The wall pushes on the end plate of the spring with the force $k\delta$, and this force removes the kinetic energy. We neglect rolling resistance.

Step 2 · Write the work–energy equation

Let state 1 be the instant the spring touches the wall and state 2 the instant of maximum compression, when the block is momentarily at rest. The spring force is conservative, so

$$T_1 + V_1 = T_2 + V_2 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \tfrac12 m v_0^2 + 0 = 0 + \tfrac12 k\Delta_{max}^2$$
Step 3 · Convert the units

In U.S. customary units the mass is $m = (3200\ \text{lb})/(32.2\ \text{ft/s}^2) = 99.38$ slug, and the compression is $\Delta_{max} = 6\ \text{in} = 0.5$ ft.

Step 4 · Solve $$k = \frac{m v_0^2}{\Delta_{max}^2} = \frac{99.38(10)^2}{(0.5)^2} = 39{,}750\ \text{lb/ft}$$

This is about $3{,}310$ lb/in. The largest force on the wall is $k\Delta_{max} = 19{,}900$ lb, which is a little more than six times the weight of the block.

Answer

$k = 3.98 \times 10^4$ lb/ft, which is about $3{,}310$ lb/in.
