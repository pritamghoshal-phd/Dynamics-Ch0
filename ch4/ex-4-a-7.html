Example 4.A.7A particle sliding on a smooth curved rod

Newton's lawpath componentsnormal force

Problem

Particle P, of weight $W = 5$ lb, slides along a smooth curved rod with radius $R = 3$ ft. The rod is a quarter circle with its center O at the lower left. At the position where $\theta = 53.13°$, measured from the horizontal at O, the speed of P is 20 ft/s and P moves down the rod, away from the wall.

Find (a) the normal force acting on P from the rod and (b) the rate of change of speed of P at this instant.

Solution

Every solution follows the four-step method: free-body diagrams, kinetics equations, kinematics, solve. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagram

Two forces act on P. The weight $W$ acts downward. The rod is smooth, so it pushes on P only perpendicular to the rod, which is along the radius. We draw this normal force $N$ pointing away from O, and a negative answer will mean that it points toward O. We use path components, with $\hat e_t$ along the motion and $\hat e_n$ toward O, the center of curvature.

The rod is smooth, so the only force from the rod is the normal force $N$ along the radius. It is drawn pointing away from O; a negative answer means it points toward O. The unit vector $\hat e_t$ points along the motion (clockwise around O) and $\hat e_n$ points toward O.

Step 2 · Write the kinetics equations

The weight has a component $W\cos\theta$ along the motion and a component $W\sin\theta$ away from O. With $m = W/g$,

$$\sum F_t = W\cos\theta = m\dot v, \qquad \sum F_n = W\sin\theta - N = m\frac{v^2}{R}$$
Step 3 · Write the kinematics equations

P moves on a circle of radius $R$, so its normal acceleration is $v^2/R$ toward O and its tangential acceleration is $\dot v$.

Step 4 · Solve

The mass is $m = 5/32.2 = 0.1553$ slug, and $v^2/R = 400/3 = 133.3$ ft/s².

$$N = W\sin\theta - m\frac{v^2}{R} = 5(0.8) - 0.1553(133.3) = 4.00 - 20.70 = -16.7\ \text{lb}$$ $$\dot v = g\cos\theta = 32.2(0.6) = 19.3\ \text{ft/s}^2$$

The normal force is negative, so the rod pushes P toward O with 16.7 lb. Gravity alone cannot provide the 20.7 lb needed to keep P on the circle at this speed.

Answer

(a) $N = 16.7$ lb, pushing P toward O. (b) $\dot v = 19.3$ ft/s², so P is speeding up.
