Example 4.A.3A crate sliding up a rough incline

Newton's lawfrictionincline

Problem

Crate C of weight $W$ slides up a rough incline of angle $\theta$ with initial speed $v_0$. The coefficient of kinetic friction is $\mu_k$.

The data are $W = 100$ lb, $\theta = 36.87°$, $v_0 = 40$ ft/s and $\mu_k = 0.25$.

Find the time $t$ after which the crate comes to rest.

Solution

Every solution follows the four-step method: free-body diagrams, kinetics equations, kinematics, solve. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagram

Three forces act on the crate. The weight $W$ acts downward. The incline pushes with the normal force $N$, perpendicular to its surface. The crate slides up, so the kinetic friction force $F_f = \mu_k N$ acts down the incline. We take $x$ up the incline and $y$ normal to it.

The crate slides up the incline, so the kinetic friction force $F_f = \mu_k N$ points down the incline. The $x$ axis points up the incline and the $y$ axis is normal to it.

Step 2 · Write the kinetics equations

The mass of the crate is $m = W/g$. Newton's second law in the two directions gives

$$\sum F_x = -W\sin\theta - \mu_k N = \frac{W}{g}\,a_x, \qquad \sum F_y = N - W\cos\theta = 0$$
Step 3 · Write the kinematics equations

The acceleration is constant while the crate slides up, so $v = v_0 + a_x t$. The crate is at rest when $v = 0$.

Step 4 · Solve

The normal force is $N = W\cos\theta = 80$ lb, and the acceleration is

$$a_x = -g\left(\sin\theta + \mu_k\cos\theta\right) = -32.2\,(0.6 + 0.25 \times 0.8) = -25.76\ \text{ft/s}^2$$ $$t = \frac{v_0}{g(\sin\theta + \mu_k\cos\theta)} = \frac{40}{25.76} = 1.553\ \text{s}$$

The weight cancels out, so the result does not depend on $W$. The crate travels $v_0^2/(2 \times 25.76) = 31.06$ ft up the incline before it stops.

Answer

$t = 1.55$ s.
