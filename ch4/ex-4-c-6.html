Example 4.C.6Particle on a curved rod sticks to a block on a spring

work–energyplastic impactspring

Problem

Particle P, weighing 10 lb, is released from rest and slides down a smooth, curved rod. Its release point is 10 ft above the end of the rod. At the end of the rod, where the rod is horizontal, P strikes block A (weight 5 lb) and sticks to it. Block A rests on a smooth horizontal surface and is attached to a spring of stiffness $k = 100$ lb/ft, which is initially unstretched.

Find the maximum deflection of the spring.

InteractiveSlide, impact and compression

The motion has three stages. On the rod, energy is conserved. At the impact, momentum is conserved and energy is lost. During the compression, energy is conserved again.

Solution

Every solution follows the four-step method: free-body diagrams, kinetics equations, kinematics, solve. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagrams

On the rod, P feels its weight $W_P$ and a normal force $N$ from the rod. The rod is smooth, so $N$ is perpendicular to the path and does no work. During the impact, take P and A together. The force between them is internal, the spring is still unstretched, and the vertical forces give no horizontal impulse. During the compression, P and A move together under the spring force $k\delta$, their weights and the normal force from the surface.

Three stages need three diagrams. On the rod only gravity does work. During the very short impact, the force between P and A is internal and the spring is still unstretched, so no horizontal impulse acts on P + A. During the compression the spring force $k\delta$ does negative work.

Step 2 · Stage 1: work and energy on the rod

Only gravity does work on P, so with the datum at the end of the rod

$$0 + W_P h = \tfrac12 \frac{W_P}{g}\,v_P^2 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad v_P = \sqrt{2gh} = \sqrt{2(32.2)(10)} = 25.38\ \text{ft/s}$$

The shape of the rod does not matter. Only the drop in height does.

Step 3 · Stage 2: impulse and momentum during the impact

The rod ends horizontally, so P arrives moving horizontally. No horizontal external impulse acts on P and A, so

$$\frac{W_P}{g}\,v_P = \frac{W_P + W_A}{g}\,v_2 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad v_2 = \frac{10}{15}\,(25.38) = 16.92\ \text{ft/s}$$

Energy is not conserved here, because P sticks to A in a plastic impact.

Step 4 · Stage 3: work and energy during the compression

The surface is smooth and the weights do no work on the horizontal motion. The kinetic energy of P and A turns into spring energy, and at the maximum deflection the speed is zero:

$$\tfrac12 \frac{W_P + W_A}{g}\,v_2^2 = \tfrac12 k\,\delta_{max}^2$$
Step 5 · Solve $$\delta_{max} = v_2\sqrt{\frac{W_P + W_A}{g\,k}} = 16.92\sqrt{\frac{15}{(32.2)(100)}} = 1.155\ \text{ft}$$

The impact loses one third of the kinetic energy, because $T_2/T_1 = W_P/(W_P + W_A) = 2/3$. Using energy conservation from the release point straight to the maximum deflection would therefore overestimate the deflection.

Answer

$\delta_{max} = 1.155$ ft (about 13.9 in).

Explore

  • Make block A very light. How close does the answer get to the result of energy conservation without the impact?
  • Double the drop height. By what factor does the maximum deflection grow?
  • Change the stiffness. Does the time the spring takes to stop the block depend on $k$?
