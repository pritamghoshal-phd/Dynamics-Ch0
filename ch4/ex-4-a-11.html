Example 4.A.11Follower riding on a fixed cam

polar componentscamspringloss of contact

Problem

A slotted arm turns about a vertical shaft through O, which is at the center of a fixed cam. Particle P, of mass $m = 0.2$ kg, moves in the slot and stays in contact with the cam because a spring between P and the outer end of the arm pushes it toward O. The shape of the cam gives the radial distance from O to P as

$$R = R_0 - R_1\cos 6\theta, \qquad R_0 = 0.5\ \text{m}, \quad R_1 = 0.1\ \text{m}$$

The spring has stiffness $k = 500$ N/m and is compressed by $\Delta = 0.2$ m when $\theta = 0$. The arm turns at a constant rate $\omega = 10$ rad/s.

Find the force of the cam on P when P passes over the top of a lobe.

Solution

Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work. The animation is played at one tenth of real speed.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagram

Isolate P and look at it from above. Three forces act in the horizontal plane. The spring pushes P toward O with the force $F_s$. The cam pushes P away from its surface with the normal force $N$. The smooth slot pushes P perpendicular to the arm with a force $A$. At the top of a lobe the cam surface is perpendicular to the arm, so $N$ is radial there. The weight and the vertical support force act perpendicular to the page.

The spring pushes P toward O with the force $F_s$. The cam pushes P outward with the normal force $N$, which is radial at the top of a lobe because there the cam surface is perpendicular to the arm. The smooth slot can only push perpendicular to the arm, with a force $A$. The weight and the vertical support force act perpendicular to the page and cancel.

Step 2 · Write the kinetics equations

In polar components,

$$\sum F_r = m a_r: \quad N - F_s = m a_r, \qquad \sum F_\theta = m a_\theta: \quad A = m a_\theta$$

The spring is compressed by 0.2 m when $R = 0.4$ m and is compressed further as P moves out, so its compression is $\delta = R - 0.2$ m and $F_s = k\,(R - 0.2)$.

Step 3 · Write the kinematics equations

P follows the cam, so $r = R(\theta)$ with $\theta = \omega t$. Differentiating with the chain rule,

$$\dot r = 6R_1\omega\sin 6\theta, \qquad \ddot r = 36R_1\omega^2\cos 6\theta$$

The top of the first lobe is where $\cos 6\theta = -1$, at $\theta = 30°$. There $R = 0.6$ m, $\dot r = 0$ and $\ddot r = -36(0.1)(10)^2 = -360$ m/s². The polar components of the acceleration are

$$a_r = \ddot r - r\omega^2 = -360 - (0.6)(10)^2 = -420\ \text{m/s}^2, \qquad a_\theta = r\dot\omega + 2\dot r\omega = 0$$
Step 4 · Solve

The spring force at the top of the lobe is $F_s = 500\,(0.6 - 0.2) = 200$ N. The radial equation then gives

$$N = F_s + m a_r = 200 + (0.2)(-420) = 116\ \text{N}$$

The transverse equation gives $A = 0$, so at this point the slot does not push on P at all.

The cam force stays positive only if $F_s > m|a_r|$ at the top of each lobe. With these numbers P lifts off when $\omega > 15.4$ rad/s.

Answer

$N = 116$ N, directed radially outward from O. The slot force is zero at that instant.
