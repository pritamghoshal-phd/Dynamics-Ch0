Example 4.A.14Car on a banked turn

path componentsbanked curvefriction

Problem

Car C travels at speed $v$ around a banked turn of radius $\rho = 500$ m. The road is banked at $\theta = 36.87°$.

Find the speed $v$ for which no friction force is needed to keep the car on the road.

Solution

Every solution follows the four-step method: free-body diagrams, kinetics equations, kinematics, solve. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagrams

Isolate the car and look at it in a vertical plane through the center of the turn. With no friction the forces are the weight $mg$ and the normal force $N$, which is perpendicular to the road. At other speeds the road must also supply a friction force $F$ along its surface.

The center of the turn is to the right, in the $\hat{\mathbf e}_n$ direction. Left: at the design speed only the weight and the normal force act. Right: at other speeds a friction force $F$ along the road is also needed. It is drawn down the slope, toward the center, and its sign tells its true direction.

Step 2 · Write the kinetics equations

Use path components. The center of the turn is in the horizontal direction $\hat{\mathbf e}_n$, and $\hat{\mathbf j}$ points up. The normal force is tilted from the vertical by the bank angle $\theta$, toward the center. With no friction,

$$\sum F_n = m\frac{v^2}{\rho}: \quad N\sin\theta = m\frac{v^2}{\rho}, \qquad \sum F_y = 0: \quad N\cos\theta - mg = 0$$
Step 3 · Write the kinematics equations

The car moves at constant speed on a horizontal circle, so its acceleration is $a_n = v^2/\rho$ toward the center and it has no vertical acceleration.

Step 4 · Solve

Dividing the first equation by the second eliminates $N$ and the mass,

$$\tan\theta = \frac{v^2}{g\rho} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad v = \sqrt{g\rho\tan\theta} = \sqrt{(9.81)(500)(0.75)} = 60.7\ \text{m/s}$$

This is about 218 km/h. At other speeds the road must supply friction. With $F$ positive down the slope, the two equations give $F = m\left(\dfrac{v^2}{\rho}\cos\theta - g\sin\theta\right)$. A slower car needs friction up the slope, and a faster car needs friction down the slope.

Answer

$v = \sqrt{g\rho\tan\theta} = 60.7$ m/s (about 218 km/h).
