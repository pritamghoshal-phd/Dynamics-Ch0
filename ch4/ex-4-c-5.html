Example 4.C.5Average force on a bouncing basketball

linear impulseaverage forcevector momentum

Problem

A basketball of weight $W$ strikes the floor with a speed $v_1$. The impact lasts a time $\Delta t$. Immediately after the impact the ball has a speed $v_2$, and the angle between its line of travel before the impact and its line of travel after the impact is $\theta$, as shown in the figure. Treat the ball as a particle.

The data are $W = 1.3$ lb, $v_1 = 20$ ft/s, $v_2 = 17$ ft/s, $\theta = 20°$ and $\Delta t = 0.01$ s.

Find the average force vector that the floor exerts on the ball during the impact, and compare its magnitude with the weight of the ball.

As in the figure, we take the two lines of travel to be symmetric about the vertical, so each makes the angle $\theta/2 = 10°$ with the vertical.

Solution

Every solution follows the four-step method: free-body diagrams, kinetics equations, kinematics, solve. Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the free-body diagram

During the impact three forces act on the ball. The floor pushes up with the normal force $F_y$ and along the floor with a friction force $F_x$, and the weight $W$ acts downward. We draw $F_x$ in the positive $x$ direction and let its sign come out of the solution. Take $x$ to the right along the floor and $y$ upward.

Left: during the impact the floor pushes on the ball with a normal force $F_y$ and a friction force $F_x$ (drawn in the positive direction). The weight $W$ also acts. Right: the impulse of the net force joins the momentum before the impact to the momentum after it.

Step 2 · Write the kinetics equation (impulse and momentum)

Linear impulse and momentum for the ball during the impact, written with average forces, is the vector equation

$$m\,\mathbf v_1 + \left(\mathbf F_{avg} - W\,\hat{\mathbf j}\right)\Delta t = m\,\mathbf v_2$$

so

$$\mathbf F_{avg} = \frac{m\left(\mathbf v_2 - \mathbf v_1\right)}{\Delta t} + W\,\hat{\mathbf j}$$
Step 3 · Write the kinematics

The ball comes down at $10°$ to the left of the vertical and leaves at $10°$ to the right of it:

$$\mathbf v_1 = 20\left(\sin 10°\,\hat{\mathbf i} - \cos 10°\,\hat{\mathbf j}\right) = 3.473\,\hat{\mathbf i} - 19.696\,\hat{\mathbf j}\ \text{ft/s}$$ $$\mathbf v_2 = 17\left(\sin 10°\,\hat{\mathbf i} + \cos 10°\,\hat{\mathbf j}\right) = 2.952\,\hat{\mathbf i} + 16.742\,\hat{\mathbf j}\ \text{ft/s}$$
Step 4 · Solve

The mass is $m = 1.3/32.2 = 0.04037$ slug, and $\mathbf v_2 - \mathbf v_1 = -0.521\,\hat{\mathbf i} + 36.438\,\hat{\mathbf j}$ ft/s. Therefore

$$\mathbf F_{avg} = \frac{0.04037}{0.01}\left(-0.521\,\hat{\mathbf i} + 36.438\,\hat{\mathbf j}\right) + 1.3\,\hat{\mathbf j} = -2.10\,\hat{\mathbf i} + 148.4\,\hat{\mathbf j}\ \text{lb}$$ $$\left|\mathbf F_{avg}\right| = 148.4\ \text{lb} \approx 114\,W$$

The average force is more than one hundred times the weight. This is why the weight is usually neglected during an impact: here it changes the answer by less than 1 %. The small negative $F_x$ is friction, which slows the motion of the ball along the floor.

Answer

$\mathbf F_{avg} = -2.10\,\hat{\mathbf i} + 148.4\,\hat{\mathbf j}$ lb, with magnitude 148.4 lb, about 114 times the weight of the ball.

Explore

  • Make the impact time ten times longer. How does the average force change?
  • Set $v_2 = v_1$. Is the horizontal force zero now? What does that say about friction?
  • Increase $\theta$. Why does the average force get smaller even though the speeds stay the same?
