Example 2.A.2Belt-driven pulley speeding up

fixed-axis rotationbelt without slipacceleration

Problem

A belt-driven pulley and an attached disk turn together about a fixed shaft through O. The belt wraps the inner pulley, of radius $r = 0.075$ m, and the disk has radius $R = 0.15$ m. At a certain instant the belt speed is $v = 1.5$ m/s, with the top run moving to the right, and the magnitude of the acceleration of point A at the bottom of the disk is 75 m/s². The belt does not slip on the pulley. The pulley is speeding up.

Find (a) the angular acceleration of the pulley and disk, (b) the acceleration of point B, which is on the rim of the inner pulley to the left of O, and (c) the acceleration of point C on the top run of the belt.

Solution

Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the kinematics diagram

The top run of the belt moves to the right, so the top of the pulley moves to the right and the pulley turns clockwise. It is speeding up, so the angular acceleration is clockwise as well.

At A the inward part $\omega^2 R$ is 60 m/s² and the tangential part $\alpha R$ is 45 m/s², so their magnitude is 75 m/s². Point C on the straight belt has only the tangential acceleration $\alpha r$ of the rim it leaves.

Step 2 · Find the angular velocity from the belt speed

The belt does not slip, so the rim of the inner pulley moves at the belt speed.

$$\omega = \frac{v}{r} = \frac{1.5}{0.075} = 20\ \text{rad/s (clockwise)}$$
Step 3 · Find the angular acceleration from the magnitude of aA

Point A is on the disk at distance $R$ from the fixed point O. Its acceleration has a normal part of magnitude $\omega^2 R$ and a tangential part of magnitude $|\alpha| R$, at right angles to each other.

$$\omega^2 R = (20)^2(0.15) = 60\ \text{m/s}^2, \qquad |\alpha|\,R = \sqrt{75^2 - 60^2} = 45\ \text{m/s}^2$$ $$|\alpha| = \frac{45}{0.15} = 300\ \text{rad/s}^2, \qquad \boldsymbol\alpha = -300\,\hat{\mathbf k}\ \text{rad/s}^2$$

The magnitude alone fixes only $|\alpha|$. The sign comes from the fact that the pulley is speeding up, so $\boldsymbol\alpha$ has the same direction as $\boldsymbol\omega = -20\,\hat{\mathbf k}$ rad/s.

Step 4 · Acceleration of B

With $\mathbf r_{B/O} = -0.075\,\hat{\mathbf i}$ m,

$$\mathbf a_B = \boldsymbol\alpha\times\mathbf r_{B/O} - \omega^2\,\mathbf r_{B/O} = \left(-300\,\hat{\mathbf k}\right)\times\left(-0.075\,\hat{\mathbf i}\right) - (20)^2\left(-0.075\,\hat{\mathbf i}\right) = 30\,\hat{\mathbf i} + 22.5\,\hat{\mathbf j}\ \text{m/s}^2$$
Step 5 · Acceleration of C on the belt

Point C moves in a straight line with the top run of the belt, so its acceleration has no normal part. Its speed is always equal to the speed of the pulley rim, $r\omega$, so its acceleration equals the tangential acceleration of the rim.

$$\mathbf a_C = r\,|\alpha|\,\hat{\mathbf i} = (0.075)(300)\,\hat{\mathbf i} = 22.5\,\hat{\mathbf i}\ \text{m/s}^2$$

The point of the pulley that touches the belt at the top has the same tangential acceleration, but it also has a normal acceleration $r\omega^2 = 30$ m/s² toward O. The belt does not, because it leaves the pulley and moves in a straight line.

Answer

(a) $\boldsymbol\alpha = -300\,\hat{\mathbf k}$ rad/s² (clockwise). (b) $\mathbf a_B = 30\,\hat{\mathbf i} + 22.5\,\hat{\mathbf j}$ m/s². (c) $\mathbf a_C = 22.5\,\hat{\mathbf i}$ m/s².
