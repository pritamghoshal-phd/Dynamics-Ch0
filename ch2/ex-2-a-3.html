Example 2.A.3Bent bar turning about a vertical shaft

fixed-axis rotation3D geometryvelocity and acceleration

Problem

The bent bar shown turns about a vertical shaft at O with $\omega = 2$ rad/s and $\alpha = 3$ rad/s², both counterclockwise when seen from above. The bar runs 500 mm from O along the $x$ axis, then 250 mm parallel to the $y$ axis, and point P is at the top of a 50 mm post at its end.

Find (a) the velocity of point P and (b) the acceleration of point P.

Solution

Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the kinematics diagram

The axis of rotation is the $z$ axis, so $\boldsymbol\omega = 2\,\hat{\mathbf k}$ rad/s and $\boldsymbol\alpha = 3\,\hat{\mathbf k}$ rad/s². At the instant shown, the position of P relative to O is

$$\mathbf r_{P/O} = 0.5\,\hat{\mathbf i} + 0.25\,\hat{\mathbf j} + 0.05\,\hat{\mathbf k}\ \text{m}$$

Only the components of $\mathbf r_{P/O}$ in the horizontal plane matter, because $\boldsymbol\omega$ and $\boldsymbol\alpha$ are vertical. The 50 mm height of the post at P has no effect.

Step 2 · Velocity of P

Point O is on the fixed axis, so $\mathbf v_O = \mathbf 0$ and

$$\mathbf v_P = \boldsymbol\omega\times\mathbf r_{P/O} = 2\,\hat{\mathbf k}\times\left(0.5\,\hat{\mathbf i} + 0.25\,\hat{\mathbf j} + 0.05\,\hat{\mathbf k}\right) = -0.5\,\hat{\mathbf i} + 1.0\,\hat{\mathbf j}\ \text{m/s}$$

The $\hat{\mathbf k}$ part of $\mathbf r_{P/O}$ is parallel to $\boldsymbol\omega$, so its cross product is zero.

Step 3 · Acceleration of P

Use $\mathbf a_P = \boldsymbol\alpha\times\mathbf r_{P/O} + \boldsymbol\omega\times\left(\boldsymbol\omega\times\mathbf r_{P/O}\right)$.

$$\boldsymbol\alpha\times\mathbf r_{P/O} = 3\,\hat{\mathbf k}\times\left(0.5\,\hat{\mathbf i} + 0.25\,\hat{\mathbf j}\right) = -0.75\,\hat{\mathbf i} + 1.5\,\hat{\mathbf j}\ \text{m/s}^2$$ $$\boldsymbol\omega\times\mathbf v_P = 2\,\hat{\mathbf k}\times\left(-0.5\,\hat{\mathbf i} + 1.0\,\hat{\mathbf j}\right) = -2.0\,\hat{\mathbf i} - 1.0\,\hat{\mathbf j}\ \text{m/s}^2$$ $$\mathbf a_P = -2.75\,\hat{\mathbf i} + 0.5\,\hat{\mathbf j}\ \text{m/s}^2$$

The second term equals $-\omega^2$ times the horizontal part of $\mathbf r_{P/O}$. It points from P toward the axis, not toward O, because P travels on a horizontal circle centered on the axis.

Answer

(a) $\mathbf v_P = -0.5\,\hat{\mathbf i} + 1.0\,\hat{\mathbf j}$ m/s. (b) $\mathbf a_P = -2.75\,\hat{\mathbf i} + 0.5\,\hat{\mathbf j}$ m/s².
