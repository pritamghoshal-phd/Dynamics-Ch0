Example 2.B.4Link sliding on a wall and a floor

instant centerssliding linkangular velocity

Problem

End A of link AB slides along a vertical wall, and end B slides along the horizontal floor. End B moves to the right with speed $v_B$.

The data are $v_B = 3$ m/s, $L = 0.5$ m and $\theta = 36.87°$, where $\theta$ is the angle between the link and the floor.

Find, using the method of instant centers, the angular velocity of AB.

Solution

The method of instant centers has four steps. Find the direction of the velocity of two points of the body, locate the instant center where the perpendiculars to those velocities meet, find ω from one known speed, and then find any other velocity from its distance to the instant center. The problem text in the book mentions $\theta = 30°$, while the parameter list gives $\theta = 36.87°$. We use $\theta = 36.87°$. The animation passes through every angle, so you can also read the result for 30° from the graph.

Step 1 · Find the directions of the velocities of A and B

End A slides along the wall, so $\mathbf v_A$ is vertical. End B slides along the floor, so $\mathbf v_B$ is horizontal.

Step 2 · Locate the instant center

The perpendicular to $\mathbf v_A$ at A is the horizontal line through A. The perpendicular to $\mathbf v_B$ at B is the vertical line through B. They meet at the point directly above B and level with A. Its distances from the two ends are

$$r_{B/IC} = L\sin\theta = 0.5(0.6) = 0.3\ \text{m}, \qquad r_{A/IC} = L\cos\theta = 0.5(0.8) = 0.4\ \text{m}$$
Step 3 · Find the angular velocity

B moves to the right at 3 m/s and lies 0.3 m below the instant center, so

$$\omega_{AB} = \frac{v_B}{r_{B/IC}} = \frac{3}{0.3} = 10.0\ \text{rad/s}$$

A point below the instant center that moves to the right turns counterclockwise about it.

Step 4 · Check with the velocity of A

End A should move down the wall with speed

$$v_A = \omega_{AB}\, r_{A/IC} = 10(0.4) = 4.00\ \text{m/s}$$

The same result follows from the length constraint $x_B^2 + y_A^2 = L^2$. Differentiating gives $x_B v_B = -y_A \dot y_A$, so $\dot y_A = -0.4(3)/0.3 = -4$ m/s.

Answer

$\omega_{AB} = 10.0$ rad/s counterclockwise. End A then slides down the wall at 4.00 m/s. At $\theta = 30°$ the same method gives $\omega_{AB} = v_B/(L\sin 30°) = 12.0$ rad/s.
