Example 2.A.8Flywheel, connecting rod and piston

slider-crankacceleration equationoffset piston

Problem

A flywheel rotates clockwise with a constant angular speed $\dot\theta$ about a fixed shaft through its center O. Pin B on the flywheel is a distance $r$ from O, and the angle $\theta$ of OB is measured clockwise from the upward vertical. The connecting rod BA, of length $L$, drives piston A, which slides along a horizontal surface. The path of the pin A is a distance $d$ below O.

The data are $\dot\theta = 10$ rad/s, $\theta = 90°$, $r = 0.1$ m, $d = 0.2$ m and $L = 0.45$ m.

Find (a) the acceleration of piston A and (b) the angular acceleration of the connecting rod BA.

Solution

Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw a kinematics diagram

Put the origin at O, with $\hat{\mathbf i}$ to the right, $\hat{\mathbf j}$ upward and $\hat{\mathbf k}$ out of the page. At $\theta = 90°$ the pin B is level with O, so $\mathbf r_{B/O} = 0.1\,\hat{\mathbf i}$ m. Pin A is 0.2 m below O and a distance $L$ from B, so its horizontal distance from B is $\sqrt{0.45^2 - 0.2^2} = 0.4031$ m and

$$\mathbf r_{A/B} = 0.4031\,\hat{\mathbf i} - 0.2\,\hat{\mathbf j}\ \text{m}$$

The flywheel turns clockwise at a constant rate, so $\boldsymbol\omega_{OB} = -10\,\hat{\mathbf k}$ rad/s and $\boldsymbol\alpha_{OB} = \mathbf 0$. The piston moves horizontally, so $\mathbf v_A = v_A\,\hat{\mathbf i}$ and $\mathbf a_A = a_A\,\hat{\mathbf i}$.

The flywheel turns clockwise at the constant rate $\omega_{OB} = \dot\theta$, so B has only a centripetal acceleration toward O. The piston moves horizontally, so $\mathbf v_A$ and $\mathbf a_A$ are horizontal. The unknowns $\omega_{AB}$ and $\alpha_{AB}$ are drawn counterclockwise.

Step 2 · Find the velocities

For the flywheel,

$$\mathbf v_B = \boldsymbol\omega_{OB}\times\mathbf r_{B/O} = (-10\,\hat{\mathbf k})\times 0.1\,\hat{\mathbf i} = -1.0\,\hat{\mathbf j}\ \text{m/s}$$

For the connecting rod,

$$v_A\,\hat{\mathbf i} = -1.0\,\hat{\mathbf j} + \omega_{AB}\,\hat{\mathbf k}\times(0.4031\,\hat{\mathbf i} - 0.2\,\hat{\mathbf j}) = 0.2\,\omega_{AB}\,\hat{\mathbf i} + (-1.0 + 0.4031\,\omega_{AB})\,\hat{\mathbf j}$$

The $\hat{\mathbf j}$ components give $\omega_{AB} = 2.481$ rad/s, and the $\hat{\mathbf i}$ components give $v_A = 0.496$ m/s.

Step 3 · Find the accelerations

The flywheel turns at a constant rate, so B has only a centripetal acceleration toward O.

$$\mathbf a_B = -\dot\theta^2\,\mathbf r_{B/O} = -(10)^2(0.1\,\hat{\mathbf i}) = -10\,\hat{\mathbf i}\ \text{m/s}^2$$

For the connecting rod, with $\omega_{AB}^2 = 6.154$ rad²/s²,

$$a_A\,\hat{\mathbf i} = \mathbf a_B + \alpha_{AB}\,\hat{\mathbf k}\times\mathbf r_{A/B} - \omega_{AB}^2\,\mathbf r_{A/B}$$ $$a_A\,\hat{\mathbf i} = (-10 + 0.2\,\alpha_{AB} - 2.481)\,\hat{\mathbf i} + (0.4031\,\alpha_{AB} + 1.231)\,\hat{\mathbf j}$$
Step 4 · Solve

The $\hat{\mathbf j}$ components give $0.4031\,\alpha_{AB} + 1.231 = 0$, so $\alpha_{AB} = -3.05$ rad/s². The $\hat{\mathbf i}$ components then give

$$a_A = -10 + 0.2(-3.053) - 2.481 = -13.09\ \text{m/s}^2$$

The piston is moving to the right but slowing down. Its acceleration is larger than that of B because the rotation of the rod adds a centripetal part along the rod.

Answer

(a) $\mathbf a_A = -13.09\,\hat{\mathbf i}$ m/s² (to the left). (b) $\boldsymbol\alpha_{AB} = -3.05\,\hat{\mathbf k}$ rad/s² (clockwise).
