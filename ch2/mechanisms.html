CKinematic analysis of planar mechanisms

A mechanism is a set of rigid links joined by pins and sliders: an engine's piston and crank, a windshield wiper, a car's suspension. Every kinematics question about a planar mechanism can be answered with just the two rigid-body equations, applied link by link:

$$\mathbf v_B = \mathbf v_A + \boldsymbol\omega\times\mathbf r_{B/A}, \qquad \mathbf a_B = \mathbf a_A + \boldsymbol\alpha\times\mathbf r_{B/A} - \omega^2\,\mathbf r_{B/A}$$

Recipe for a mechanism

  1. Set up axes with $\hat{\mathbf k}$ out of the page, and write every position vector you need in components.
  2. Write one velocity equation for each link, relating two points on that link (pins at its ends, usually). Give every link its own $\omega$ with a unique subscript, e.g. $\omega_{AB}$. Treat unknown $\omega$'s as $+\hat{\mathbf k}$: a negative answer just means clockwise. Known angular velocities must carry their correct sign.
  3. Combine the equations so that shared points (pins) have the same velocity, then balance the $\hat{\mathbf i}$ and $\hat{\mathbf j}$ parts. Count: each vector equation gives two scalar equations. If you have fewer equations than unknowns, you have missed a link or a constraint (a slider's direction, a no-slip condition).
  4. Check the directions with instant centers.
  5. Accelerations second. Repeat with the acceleration equation. It needs every $\omega$ (the $-\omega^2\mathbf r$ terms), so the velocity problem must always be solved first, even if only accelerations are asked.

The slider–crank

A crank OA turns about a fixed pin O. A connecting rod AB joins it to a slider B that moves along a straight guide. The rod is the interesting link: it neither rotates about a fixed point nor translates. Writing $\mathbf v_B = \mathbf v_A + \omega_{AB}\hat{\mathbf k}\times\mathbf r_{B/A}$, with $\mathbf v_B$ horizontal, gives two equations for the two unknowns $\omega_{AB}$ and $v_B$.

The four-bar linkage

Four links (the ground counts as one) joined by four pins: crank OA, coupler AB and rocker BD. Pins A and B each move on a circle (about O and D), so their velocities are perpendicular to OA and to DB. The coupler's instant center is where the lines OA and DB (extended) cross. When those lines are parallel, the coupler is momentarily translating and $\omega_{AB}=0$; Example 2.C.2 is exactly that situation.

Mechanisms in the wild

Every piston engine is a slider–crank: the crankshaft is the crank, the connecting rod is the rod and the piston is the slider. Because the rod has finite length, the piston spends slightly longer near the bottom of its stroke than near the top, which the asymmetric vB curve above shows.
