Example 2.A.10Four-bar linkage with two horizontal links

four-baracceleration equationtranslating link

Problem

In the four-bar linkage O–A–B–D, the links OA, AB and BD all have length $L$. Link OA rotates counterclockwise about the fixed pin O with a constant angular speed $\omega_{OA}$. At the instant shown, links OA and BD are both horizontal, and pin B is a height $d$ above the line OA. Pin D is fixed.

The data are $\omega_{OA} = 3$ rad/s, $L = 0.5$ m and $d = 0.4$ m.

Find, at this instant, (a) the angular acceleration of link AB and (b) the angular acceleration of link BD. Express both answers as vectors.

Solution

Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw a kinematics diagram

Put the origin at O, with $\hat{\mathbf i}$ to the right, $\hat{\mathbf j}$ upward and $\hat{\mathbf k}$ out of the page. Pin A is at $(0.5, 0)$ m. Pin B is 0.4 m higher and $\sqrt{0.5^2 - 0.4^2} = 0.3$ m to the right of A, at $(0.8, 0.4)$ m. Link BD is horizontal, so D is at $(1.3, 0.4)$ m. The relative positions are

$$\mathbf r_{A/O} = 0.5\,\hat{\mathbf i}, \qquad \mathbf r_{B/A} = 0.3\,\hat{\mathbf i} + 0.4\,\hat{\mathbf j}, \qquad \mathbf r_{B/D} = -0.5\,\hat{\mathbf i}\ \text{m}$$

Link OA turns at a constant rate, so $\boldsymbol\omega_{OA} = 3\,\hat{\mathbf k}$ rad/s and $\boldsymbol\alpha_{OA} = \mathbf 0$.

Blue arrows are velocities and red arrows are known parts of the accelerations. Both $\mathbf v_A$ and $\mathbf v_B$ point up, so AB translates at this instant. The centripetal part of $\mathbf a_B$ points from B toward D.

Step 2 · Find the angular velocities $$\mathbf v_A = \boldsymbol\omega_{OA}\times\mathbf r_{A/O} = 3\,\hat{\mathbf k}\times 0.5\,\hat{\mathbf i} = 1.5\,\hat{\mathbf j}\ \text{m/s}$$

Write the velocity of B from link AB and from link BD.

$$\mathbf v_B = 1.5\,\hat{\mathbf j} + \omega_{AB}\,\hat{\mathbf k}\times(0.3\,\hat{\mathbf i} + 0.4\,\hat{\mathbf j}) = -0.4\,\omega_{AB}\,\hat{\mathbf i} + (1.5 + 0.3\,\omega_{AB})\,\hat{\mathbf j}$$ $$\mathbf v_B = \omega_{BD}\,\hat{\mathbf k}\times(-0.5\,\hat{\mathbf i}) = -0.5\,\omega_{BD}\,\hat{\mathbf j}$$

The $\hat{\mathbf i}$ components give $\omega_{AB} = 0$, and the $\hat{\mathbf j}$ components give $\omega_{BD} = -3$ rad/s. Link AB is momentarily translating, because $\mathbf v_A$ and $\mathbf v_B$ are both $1.5\,\hat{\mathbf j}$ m/s.

Step 3 · Write the acceleration equations $$\mathbf a_A = \boldsymbol\alpha_{OA}\times\mathbf r_{A/O} - \omega_{OA}^2\,\mathbf r_{A/O} = -(3)^2(0.5\,\hat{\mathbf i}) = -4.5\,\hat{\mathbf i}\ \text{m/s}^2$$

From link AB, whose angular velocity is zero,

$$\mathbf a_B = -4.5\,\hat{\mathbf i} + \alpha_{AB}\,\hat{\mathbf k}\times(0.3\,\hat{\mathbf i} + 0.4\,\hat{\mathbf j}) = (-4.5 - 0.4\,\alpha_{AB})\,\hat{\mathbf i} + 0.3\,\alpha_{AB}\,\hat{\mathbf j}$$

From link BD,

$$\mathbf a_B = \alpha_{BD}\,\hat{\mathbf k}\times(-0.5\,\hat{\mathbf i}) - (-3)^2(-0.5\,\hat{\mathbf i}) = 4.5\,\hat{\mathbf i} - 0.5\,\alpha_{BD}\,\hat{\mathbf j}$$
Step 4 · Solve

The $\hat{\mathbf i}$ components give $-4.5 - 0.4\,\alpha_{AB} = 4.5$, so $\alpha_{AB} = -22.5$ rad/s². The $\hat{\mathbf j}$ components give $0.3(-22.5) = -0.5\,\alpha_{BD}$, so $\alpha_{BD} = 13.5$ rad/s².

Link AB has zero angular velocity at this instant but a large angular acceleration. A momentarily translating link does not have zero angular acceleration in general.

Answer

(a) $\boldsymbol\alpha_{AB} = -22.5\,\hat{\mathbf k}$ rad/s² (clockwise). (b) $\boldsymbol\alpha_{BD} = 13.5\,\hat{\mathbf k}$ rad/s² (counterclockwise).

Explore

  • Watch the plot near the instant. How does the slope of the $\omega_{AB}$ curve relate to $\alpha_{AB}$?
  • Change the height $d$. Does $\omega_{AB}$ stay zero at the instant? Explain with the directions of $\mathbf v_A$ and $\mathbf v_B$.
