Example 2.C.2Four-bar linkage with a momentarily translating coupler

four-barmechanisminstant center at infinity

Problem

In the mechanism shown, link OA has length $L$ and turns counterclockwise with a constant angular velocity $\omega_{OA}$ as it moves into a vertical position. At the same instant, link BD, of length $2L$, is also vertical. The fixed pins O and D lie on the same horizontal line, a distance $2L$ apart.

The data are $\omega_{OA} = 0.5$ rad/s.

Find, at this instant, (a) the angular velocity of link AB, (b) the angular velocity of link BD, (c) the angular acceleration of link AB and (d) the angular acceleration of link BD.

Solution

Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw a kinematics diagram

Put the origin at O, with $\hat{\mathbf i}$ to the right, $\hat{\mathbf j}$ upward and $\hat{\mathbf k}$ out of the page. At the instant, $A = (0, L)$, $D = (2L, 0)$ and $B = (2L, 2L)$, so

$$\mathbf r_{A/O} = L\,\hat{\mathbf j}, \qquad \mathbf r_{B/A} = 2L\,\hat{\mathbf i} + L\,\hat{\mathbf j}, \qquad \mathbf r_{B/D} = 2L\,\hat{\mathbf j}$$

Link OA turns at a constant rate, so $\boldsymbol\omega_{OA} = 0.5\,\hat{\mathbf k}$ rad/s and $\boldsymbol\alpha_{OA} = \mathbf 0$. The length of AB is $\sqrt 5\,L$, but we never need it.

The velocities of A and B are both horizontal, so the lines perpendicular to them (thin red lines along OA and DB) are parallel and never meet. The instant center of AB is at infinity. OA turns at a constant rate, so A has only its centripetal acceleration toward O.

Step 2 · Write the velocity equations $$\mathbf v_A = \boldsymbol\omega_{OA}\times\mathbf r_{A/O} = 0.5\,\hat{\mathbf k}\times L\,\hat{\mathbf j} = -0.5L\,\hat{\mathbf i}$$ $$\mathbf v_B = \omega_{BD}\,\hat{\mathbf k}\times 2L\,\hat{\mathbf j} = -2L\,\omega_{BD}\,\hat{\mathbf i}$$ $$\mathbf v_B = \mathbf v_A + \omega_{AB}\,\hat{\mathbf k}\times(2L\,\hat{\mathbf i} + L\,\hat{\mathbf j}) = (-0.5L - L\,\omega_{AB})\,\hat{\mathbf i} + 2L\,\omega_{AB}\,\hat{\mathbf j}$$

The $\hat{\mathbf j}$ components give $\omega_{AB} = 0$. The $\hat{\mathbf i}$ components then give $-2L\,\omega_{BD} = -0.5L$, so $\omega_{BD} = 0.25$ rad/s.

As a check, $\mathbf v_A$ and $\mathbf v_B$ are both horizontal. The lines perpendicular to them, OA and DB, are parallel, so the instant center of AB is at infinity and AB translates at this instant.

Step 3 · Write the acceleration equations $$\mathbf a_A = -\omega_{OA}^2\,\mathbf r_{A/O} = -(0.5)^2 L\,\hat{\mathbf j} = -0.25L\,\hat{\mathbf j}$$ $$\mathbf a_B = \alpha_{BD}\,\hat{\mathbf k}\times 2L\,\hat{\mathbf j} - \omega_{BD}^2(2L\,\hat{\mathbf j}) = -2L\,\alpha_{BD}\,\hat{\mathbf i} - 0.125L\,\hat{\mathbf j}$$

From link AB, whose angular velocity is zero,

$$\mathbf a_B = \mathbf a_A + \alpha_{AB}\,\hat{\mathbf k}\times(2L\,\hat{\mathbf i} + L\,\hat{\mathbf j}) = -L\,\alpha_{AB}\,\hat{\mathbf i} + (2L\,\alpha_{AB} - 0.25L)\,\hat{\mathbf j}$$
Step 4 · Solve

The $\hat{\mathbf j}$ components give $-0.125L = 2L\,\alpha_{AB} - 0.25L$, so $\alpha_{AB} = 0.0625$ rad/s². The $\hat{\mathbf i}$ components give $-2L\,\alpha_{BD} = -L\,\alpha_{AB}$, so $\alpha_{BD} = 0.03125$ rad/s².

The length $L$ cancels from every equation, so the answers do not depend on the size of the linkage.

Answer

(a) $\boldsymbol\omega_{AB} = \mathbf 0$. (b) $\boldsymbol\omega_{BD} = 0.25\,\hat{\mathbf k}$ rad/s. (c) $\boldsymbol\alpha_{AB} = 0.0625\,\hat{\mathbf k}$ rad/s². (d) $\boldsymbol\alpha_{BD} = 0.0313\,\hat{\mathbf k}$ rad/s².
