Example 2.A.1Angular velocity and acceleration from one acceleration vector

fixed-axis rotationnormal and tangential partsacceleration

Problem

A disk turns at a non-constant rate $\Omega$ about a fixed axis through its center O. At a particular instant the acceleration of point P on the disk is $\mathbf a_P = 3\,\hat{\mathbf i} + 4\,\hat{\mathbf j}$ m/s². Point P is a distance $r = 0.4$ m from O, on the negative $x$ axis.

Find (a) the angular velocity of the disk and (b) its angular acceleration at this instant. Write both answers as vectors.

InteractiveThe disk slows down while it turns counterclockwise
View:

The dashed arrows are the two parts of $\mathbf a_P$, the normal part $\omega^2 r$ toward O and the tangential part $\alpha r$, placed head to tail. Their sum (red) equals $3\,\hat{\mathbf i} + 4\,\hat{\mathbf j}$ m/s² at the instant. Switch to a clockwise spin and notice that the acceleration at the instant is exactly the same.

Solution

Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the kinematics diagram

Point P is on the negative $x$ axis, so its position relative to O is $\mathbf r_{P/O} = -0.4\,\hat{\mathbf i}$ m. Because O is fixed, the acceleration of P has a normal part, which points from P toward O, and a tangential part, which is perpendicular to OP.

The normal part $\omega^2 r$ always points from P toward O. The tangential part $\alpha r$ is perpendicular to OP. Here it points up, so the disk has a clockwise angular acceleration.

Step 2 · Write the rigid-body acceleration equation

Let $\boldsymbol\omega = \omega\,\hat{\mathbf k}$ and $\boldsymbol\alpha = \alpha\,\hat{\mathbf k}$. Since $\mathbf a_O = \mathbf 0$,

$$\mathbf a_P = \boldsymbol\alpha\times\mathbf r_{P/O} - \omega^2\,\mathbf r_{P/O} = \alpha\hat{\mathbf k}\times\left(-0.4\,\hat{\mathbf i}\right) - \omega^2\left(-0.4\,\hat{\mathbf i}\right) = 0.4\,\omega^2\,\hat{\mathbf i} - 0.4\,\alpha\,\hat{\mathbf j}$$
Step 3 · Match the components

Setting this equal to $3\,\hat{\mathbf i} + 4\,\hat{\mathbf j}$ gives one equation for each component.

$$\hat{\mathbf i}:\quad 0.4\,\omega^2 = 3 \;\;\Rightarrow\;\; \omega^2 = 7.5\ \text{rad}^2/\text{s}^2, \qquad |\omega| = 2.74\ \text{rad/s}$$ $$\hat{\mathbf j}:\quad -0.4\,\alpha = 4 \;\;\Rightarrow\;\; \alpha = -10\ \text{rad/s}^2$$
Step 4 · Interpret the signs

The angular velocity enters the acceleration only through $\omega^2$, so the acceleration of P cannot tell us the direction of rotation. The figure shows $\Omega$ counterclockwise, so $\boldsymbol\omega = 2.74\,\hat{\mathbf k}$ rad/s. The angular acceleration is negative, which means it is clockwise. A counterclockwise spin with a clockwise angular acceleration is a disk that is slowing down.

The animation lets you switch to a clockwise spin. The acceleration of P at the instant does not change, which confirms that only the magnitude of $\omega$ can be found from $\mathbf a_P$.

Answer

(a) $\boldsymbol\omega = \pm 2.74\,\hat{\mathbf k}$ rad/s. With the counterclockwise rotation shown in the figure, $\boldsymbol\omega = 2.74\,\hat{\mathbf k}$ rad/s. (b) $\boldsymbol\alpha = -10\,\hat{\mathbf k}$ rad/s².
