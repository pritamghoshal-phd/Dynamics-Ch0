BInstant centers of rotation

If a body is pinned at a fixed point A, every point moves on a circle around A: its velocity is perpendicular to the line from A, and its speed is $|\omega|$ times its distance from A. The remarkable fact is that any body in planar motion (that is not purely translating) behaves this way at each instant, about some point C whose velocity is momentarily zero. That point is the instantaneous center of rotation, or instant center (IC).

Put $\mathbf v_C = \mathbf 0$ in the velocity equation and you get, for any point D of the body,

$$\mathbf v_D = \boldsymbol\omega\times\mathbf r_{D/C}, \qquad |\mathbf v_D| = |\omega|\,|\mathbf r_{D/C}|$$

So at this instant:

  • the velocity of every point is perpendicular to the line joining it to the IC;
  • speeds are proportional to the distance from the IC;
  • the IC does not have to lie on the body itself; it is just the point the body is turning about right now.
ConceptEvery velocity is perpendicular to the line to the IC

Finding the instant center

Recipe

  1. Find two points on the body whose velocity directions you know (for example, a pin moving on a circle, or a slider moving along a guide). For at least one of them, also know the speed.
  2. Through each point, draw a line perpendicular to its velocity.
  3. The two lines cross at the instant center C.
  4. Angular speed: $|\omega| = |\mathbf v_A|/|\mathbf r_{A/C}|$. The sense (CW or CCW) follows from picturing the body turning about C so that A moves the way it does.
  5. Any other point D: speed $|\omega|\,|\mathbf r_{D/C}|$, direction perpendicular to CD.

Two special cases: if the two perpendicular lines are parallel (the velocities are parallel and not lined up), they meet "at infinity", so $\omega = 0$ and the body is translating at that instant. For a wheel rolling without slipping, the contact point is the IC.

The IC moves as the body moves. In the sliding-link animation below, the IC sweeps out a circle around the corner. Its path is called the centrode.

ConceptA sliding link and its moving instant center

End A slides down the wall and end B along the floor. The perpendiculars to their velocities (red dashed) cross at the IC. The midpoint G stays at distance L/2 from the IC, and the IC traces a quarter circle of radius L about the corner.

Instant centers are for velocities only

The IC has zero velocity at this instant, but in general it does not have zero acceleration. The rolling wheel is the classic example: the contact point is the IC, yet its acceleration is $R\omega^2$ upward. Writing the acceleration equation about C,

$$\mathbf a_D = \mathbf a_C + \boldsymbol\alpha\times\mathbf r_{D/C} - \omega^2\,\mathbf r_{D/C}$$

shows two terms ($\mathbf a_C$ and $-\omega^2\mathbf r_{D/C}$) that are not perpendicular to CD. So never use the instant center to find accelerations. Use it to find velocities quickly, or to check the direction of a velocity answer you got with vectors.
