Example 2.A.5Acceleration of a point on a rolling wheel

rolling without slipaccelerationsketch

Problem

A wheel of radius $r = 2$ ft rolls without slipping on a rough horizontal surface. At one instant the center O moves to the right with speed $v_O = 5$ ft/s, and this speed is decreasing at 3 ft/s². At that instant point P on the rim is at $\theta = 90°$, directly above O.

Find the acceleration of P at this instant, and sketch the acceleration vector at P.

Solution

Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the kinematics diagram

Take $\hat{\mathbf i}$ to the right, $\hat{\mathbf j}$ up and $\hat{\mathbf k}$ out of the page. The center moves to the right and slows down, so

$$\mathbf v_O = 5\,\hat{\mathbf i}\ \text{ft/s}, \qquad \mathbf a_O = -3\,\hat{\mathbf i}\ \text{ft/s}^2$$

The three parts of $\mathbf a_P$ placed head to tail (the vertical part is drawn at 70% of its length). The centripetal part $\omega^2 r = 12.5$ ft/s² dominates, so $\mathbf a_P$ points mostly toward the center, and backward because the wheel is slowing down.

Step 2 · Use rolling without slipping

For rolling without slipping, $v_O = -r\omega$ and $a_O = -r\alpha$.

$$\omega = -\frac{5}{2} = -2.5\ \text{rad/s (clockwise)}, \qquad \alpha = -\frac{-3}{2} = 1.5\ \text{rad/s}^2\ \text{(counterclockwise)}$$

The wheel turns clockwise but is slowing down, so its angular acceleration is counterclockwise.

Step 3 · Relative acceleration from O to P

With $\mathbf r_{P/O} = 2\,\hat{\mathbf j}$ ft,

$$\mathbf a_P = \mathbf a_O + \boldsymbol\alpha\times\mathbf r_{P/O} - \omega^2\,\mathbf r_{P/O} = -3\,\hat{\mathbf i} + \left(1.5\,\hat{\mathbf k}\right)\times\left(2\,\hat{\mathbf j}\right) - (2.5)^2\left(2\,\hat{\mathbf j}\right)$$ $$\mathbf a_P = -3\,\hat{\mathbf i} - 3\,\hat{\mathbf i} - 12.5\,\hat{\mathbf j} = -6\,\hat{\mathbf i} - 12.5\,\hat{\mathbf j}\ \text{ft/s}^2$$
Step 4 · Sketch and check

The vector points down and to the left. Its largest part is the centripetal part, 12.5 ft/s² toward the center, and it has a backward part of 6 ft/s² because the wheel is slowing down. Its magnitude is $\sqrt{6^2 + 12.5^2} = 13.9$ ft/s². Freeze the animation at the instant and compare with the red arrow.

Answer

$\mathbf a_P = -6\,\hat{\mathbf i} - 12.5\,\hat{\mathbf j}$ ft/s², with magnitude 13.9 ft/s², pointing down and backward toward the center.
