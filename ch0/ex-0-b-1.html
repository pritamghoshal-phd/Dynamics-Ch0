Example 0.B.1Block pushed by an inclined force

FBDfrictionnormal force

Problem

Block A, of mass $m$, moves to the right along a rough horizontal surface under the action of the applied force $F$. The force pushes on the upper left corner of the block and is directed down and to the right at the angle $\theta$ below the horizontal. The coefficient of kinetic friction between the block and the surface is $\mu_k$.

Find the free-body diagram of the block.

Solution

Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Choose the body

The body is block A alone. Imagine cutting it free from the floor and from whatever applies the force $F$. Every place where we cut it, we must draw the force that was transmitted there.

Step 2 · Draw the free-body diagram

The block is acted on by four forces. The weight $mg$ is a body force and acts downward at the mass center. The applied force $F$ acts at the upper left corner, at the angle $\theta$ below the horizontal. The floor exerts a normal force $N$, upward. Because the block slides to the right, the floor also exerts the kinetic friction force $\mu_k N$, to the left.

The floor is replaced by the two forces it exerts: the normal force $N$ and the kinetic friction force $\mu_k N$, which opposes the sliding. The weight $mg$ acts at the mass center.

Step 3 · Use the diagram to find the normal force

The block does not move vertically, so the vertical forces balance.

$$N - mg - F\sin\theta = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad N = mg + F\sin\theta$$

The friction force is therefore $\mu_k N = \mu_k\,(mg + F\sin\theta)$. The downward part of the push presses the block into the floor, so the friction is larger than $\mu_k mg$. This is why pushing a heavy box downward at an angle is harder than pulling it upward at the same angle.

Step 4 · Look ahead to the motion

Newton's second law in the horizontal direction then gives the acceleration of the block.

$$m a = F\cos\theta - \mu_k\,(mg + F\sin\theta)$$
Answer

The free-body diagram of A shows the weight $mg$ (down), the applied force $F$ (down and to the right at $\theta$), the normal force $N$ (up) and the kinetic friction force $\mu_k N$ (to the left). From the vertical balance, $N = mg + F\sin\theta$.

Explore

  • Raise θ until the block slows down even though F is large. Show that this happens when $F(\cos\theta - \mu_k\sin\theta) < \mu_k m g$.
  • Set θ = 0. Is N equal to mg now? Why?
  • What changes in the free-body diagram if the block slides to the left?
