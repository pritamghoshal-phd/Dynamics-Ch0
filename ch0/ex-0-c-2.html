Example 0.C.2Acceleration from the speed as a function of position

scalar kinematicschain rule

Problem

Point P moves along a straight-line path. Its speed is given as a function of its position by

$$v(s) = 5\sin 3s$$

Find the acceleration of P.

The book does not state units. The animation uses metres for $s$ and seconds for $t$.

InteractiveSpeed versus position, and the acceleration it implies

The motion is shown eight times slower than real time. In the left graph the dashed line is tangent to v(s), and its slope is dv/ds. The acceleration on the right is zero where the speed is largest, at s = π/6, and negative after that point.

Solution

Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the kinematics diagram

The position $s$ of P is measured from O along the path. The speed $v$ is known at each position, not at each time.

Step 2 · Use the chain rule

Because $v$ is known as a function of $s$, we cannot differentiate with respect to time directly. The chain rule gives

$$a = \frac{dv}{dt} = \frac{dv}{ds}\,\frac{ds}{dt} = v\,\frac{dv}{ds}$$
Step 3 · Differentiate the speed with respect to position $$\frac{dv}{ds} = 15\cos 3s$$
Step 4 · Multiply by the speed $$a = v\,\frac{dv}{ds} = (5\sin 3s)(15\cos 3s) = 75\sin 3s\cos 3s$$

With the identity $\sin 2\alpha = 2\sin\alpha\cos\alpha$, this becomes

$$a = 37.5\,\sin 6s$$
Answer

$a(s) = 75\sin 3s\cos 3s = 37.5\sin 6s$

Explore

  • Pause the animation near s = π/6 ≈ 0.52. Why is the acceleration zero there, even though the speed is at its largest value?
  • Compare the last two statistics. Why does the measured change in speed over a short time agree with $v\,dv/ds$?
  • Is it correct to write $a = dv/ds$? Check the units of each side.
