Example 0.B.3Piston, connecting rod and crankshaft

FBDpinsmechanisms

Problem

In the piston and cylinder system, the piston A, of mass $m_p$, moves along a smooth horizontal surface under the action of an applied force $F$. The piston is connected to a crankshaft of mass $m_c$ by a connecting rod of mass $m_{rod}$, with pin joints at A and O. The crankshaft turns about a frictionless bearing at its center C, which is fixed to the ground.

Find the free-body diagrams of (a) the piston alone, (b) the crankshaft alone, (c) the connecting rod alone and (d) the whole system of piston, crankshaft and connecting rod.

Hint. The connecting rod cannot be modeled as a two-force member.

Solution

Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Identify the connections

Each pin joint transmits a force with two unknown components and no moment. The piston touches a smooth surface, which can only push perpendicular to itself, so it exerts a normal force $N$ and no friction. The bearing at C is a frictionless pin, so it exerts a force $(C_x, C_y)$ and no moment.

Step 2 · Explain why the rod is not a two-force member

A two-force member carries forces only at its two ends, and those forces must be equal, opposite and along the line between the pins. Two conditions are needed for this. The member must have no weight, and it must not accelerate. The connecting rod has weight $m_{rod}g$, and it both translates and rotates with nonzero acceleration. The forces at A and O are therefore not directed along the rod, and each must be drawn with two components.

Step 3 · Draw the four free-body diagrams

(a) The piston is acted on by $F$, its weight $m_p g$, the normal force $N$ and the pin force $(A_x, A_y)$ from the rod.

(b) The crankshaft is acted on by its weight $m_c g$, the bearing force $(C_x, C_y)$ and the pin force $(O_x, O_y)$ from the rod.

(c) The rod is acted on by its weight $m_{rod} g$ at its mass center G and by the pin forces at A and O. By Newton's third law, these are equal and opposite to the forces that the rod exerts on the piston and on the crankshaft.

(d) For the whole system, the pin forces at A and O are internal. Only $F$, $N$, the three weights and the bearing force $(C_x, C_y)$ remain.

The pin forces at A and at O appear twice, with opposite directions, because of Newton's third law. In (a) the rod pushes on the piston with $A_x$ and $A_y$, so in (c) the piston pushes on the rod with $A_x$ and $A_y$ reversed. The same holds for $O_x$ and $O_y$ in (b) and (c). In (d) all the pin forces at A and O are internal.

Step 4 · Count the unknowns

The unknown forces are $N$, $A_x$, $A_y$, $O_x$, $O_y$, $C_x$ and $C_y$, which is seven. Newton's law gives one useful equation for the piston in each direction, and three equations for each of the rod and the crankshaft, which is eight equations. The extra equation determines the angular acceleration of the crank, which is one more unknown. The animation solves exactly this set of equations at every instant and lists the forces on the chosen body.

Answer

(a) Piston: $F$, $m_p g$, $N$ and $(A_x, A_y)$. (b) Crankshaft: $m_c g$, $(C_x, C_y)$ and $(O_x, O_y)$. (c) Connecting rod: $m_{rod} g$ at G, and pin forces at A and O that are equal and opposite to those in (a) and (b). (d) Whole system: $F$, $N$, $m_p g$, $m_{rod} g$, $m_c g$ and $(C_x, C_y)$.

Explore

  • Choose the connecting rod and watch the pin forces at A and O. Are they ever equal and opposite? When would they be, if the rod had no mass?
  • Raise F until the crank can no longer complete a full turn. What happens to the motion?
  • Choose the whole system. Why do $C_x$ and $C_y$ change even though the crank center never moves?
