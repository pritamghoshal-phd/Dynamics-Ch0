Example 0.B.2Block, two pulleys and a hanging block

FBDpulleysinternal forces

Problem

Block A, of mass $m$, moves to the right along a rough horizontal surface, and block B, of mass $M$, hangs from a rope and pulley system. The rope runs from A over pulley O at the corner of the table, down and around the lower pulley that carries B, and back up to the axle of pulley O. Both pulleys are massless and frictionless. The coefficient of kinetic friction between A and the surface is $\mu_k$.

Find the free-body diagrams of (a) A alone, (b) B together with the lower pulley, (c) the upper pulley alone and (d) the whole system made of A, B and both pulleys.

Solution

Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Find the tension in each part of the rope

A massless, frictionless pulley cannot change the tension in a rope that passes over it. Therefore the same tension $T$ acts in every straight part of the rope, from block A to the axle of pulley O.

Step 2 · Draw the four free-body diagrams

(a) Block A is acted on by its weight $mg$, the normal force $N$, the tension $T$ toward the pulley and the kinetic friction force $\mu_k N$, which opposes the sliding.

(b) For B together with the lower pulley, two parts of the rope are cut, and each pulls upward with $T$. The weight $Mg$ acts downward. The force between B and its pulley is internal to this system and does not appear.

(c) The upper pulley is pulled by three parts of the rope. The part that goes to A pulls to the left, the part that leaves the right side of the pulley pulls down, and the end that is tied to the axle also pulls down. The bracket holds the pulley with the pin force $(O_x, O_y)$.

(d) For the whole system, only the forces that cross the boundary remain. These are $mg$, $N$ and $\mu_k N$ on A, $Mg$ on B and the pin force $(O_x, O_y)$ at O.

Every rope tension has the same value $T$, because the pulleys are massless and frictionless. In diagram (d) the boundary encloses both blocks, both pulleys and all the rope, so every tension is internal and only the external forces remain.

Step 3 · Use diagram (c) to find the pin force

The pulley is massless, so the forces on it must balance even while it turns.

$$O_x - T = 0, \qquad O_y - 2T = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad O_x = T, \quad O_y = 2T$$
Step 4 · Relate the motions of A and B

The rope length is constant. When A moves a distance $x_A$ to the right, the horizontal part of the rope shortens by $x_A$, and the two vertical parts that hold the lower pulley must together lengthen by $x_A$. Each of them lengthens by $x_A/2$, so

$$x_B = \tfrac12 x_A, \qquad a_B = \tfrac12 a_A$$

Combining diagrams (a) and (b) with Newton's law and this constraint gives $a_A = \dfrac{(M - 2\mu_k m)\,g}{2m + M/2}$, which is the result used in the animation. Constrained motion of this kind is studied in Chapter 1.

Answer

(a) A: $mg$ (down), $N$ (up), $T$ (to the right), $\mu_k N$ (to the left).

(b) B and the lower pulley: $Mg$ (down) and two tensions $T$ (up).

(c) The upper pulley: $T$ toward A, two tensions $T$ downward and the pin force $O_x = T$, $O_y = 2T$.

(d) The whole system: $mg$, $N$, $\mu_k N$, $Mg$, $O_x$ and $O_y$ only.

Explore

  • Make M small. For what value of M does A stop accelerating? Compare your answer with $M = 2\mu_k m$.
  • Switch between diagrams (a) and (d). Which forces appear in one but not in the other, and why?
  • What would change in diagram (c) if the pulley had mass?
