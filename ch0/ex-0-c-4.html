Example 0.C.4Speed from the acceleration as a function of position

scalar kinematicsintegrationchain rule

Problem

The acceleration of point P, which travels on a straight-line path, is given as a function of its position by

$$a(s) = 8s^3, \qquad v(s = 0) = 5$$

Find the speed of P as a function of its position.

The book does not state units. The animation uses metres for $s$ and seconds for $t$.

Solution

Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the kinematics diagram

The acceleration is known as a function of position, and we want the speed as a function of position as well.

Step 2 · Choose the right form

Write the acceleration with the chain rule, $a = v\,dv/ds$, and separate the variables.

$$v\,dv = a\,ds = 8s^3\,ds$$
Step 3 · Integrate from the known starting point $$\int_{5}^{v} v\,dv = \int_0^{s} 8s^3\,ds \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \tfrac12 v^2 - \tfrac12(5)^2 = 2s^4$$
Step 4 · Solve for the speed $$v^2 = 25 + 4s^4 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad v = \sqrt{25 + 4s^4}$$

We take the positive root because P starts with a positive speed and the acceleration is positive for $s > 0$, so P keeps moving forward.

Answer

$v(s) = \sqrt{25 + 4s^4}$
