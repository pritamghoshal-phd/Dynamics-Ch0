Example 0.C.1Acceleration from the speed as a function of time

scalar kinematicsdifferentiation

Problem

Point P moves along a straight-line path. Its speed is given as a function of time by

$$v(t) = 3t^2$$

Find the acceleration of P.

The book does not state units. The animation uses metres for $s$ and seconds for $t$.

InteractiveThe motion of P, its speed and its acceleration

The animation starts P at s = 0 when t = 0. In the left graph, the dashed red line is tangent to v(t), and its slope is the acceleration shown on the right.

Solution

Try the problem first, and then open one step at a time to check your work.

Step 1 · Draw the kinematics diagram

P moves along a straight line. We measure its position $s$ from a fixed point O, with the positive direction to the right. The speed is $v = ds/dt$ and the acceleration is $a = dv/dt$.

Step 2 · Recognize the type of problem

The speed is known as a function of time. The acceleration is the rate of change of the speed with respect to time, so we can differentiate directly.

$$a = \frac{dv}{dt}$$
Step 3 · Differentiate $$a = \frac{d}{dt}\left(3t^2\right) = 6t$$

The acceleration grows linearly with time. At $t = 1$, for example, $v = 3$ and $a = 6$, which is the slope of the tangent line in the animation at that instant.

Answer

$a(t) = 6t$
